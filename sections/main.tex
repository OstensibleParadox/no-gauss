\begin{abstract}
We classify bounded invertible linear transformations preserving the measure
class of a non-Gaussian i.i.d. product law.  For a centered, variance-one
density that is positive almost everywhere and has finite location and scale
Fisher information in the square-root Sobolev sense, equivalence holds
exactly for Hilbert--Schmidt perturbations of coordinate permutations whose
signs preserve the scalar law.  Equivalently, the Hellinger energies of the
actual finite output marginals remain bounded; otherwise the image law is
singular.  A rotational Fourier defect detects non-Gaussianity and yields
dimension-free local coercivity, which tail symmetries turn into the global
operator condition.  For symmetric stable products, off-diagonal mixing
instead has an anisotropic column cost.  When densities have zeros, we
separate Hellinger accumulation from the additional null-set conditions
required for equivalence.
\end{abstract}
