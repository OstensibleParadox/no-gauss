\begin{abstract}
Kakutani's theorem tensorizes equivalence of product measures, but dense
bounded linear mixing destroys the independence on which that test rests.
The resulting difficulty is genuinely infinite-dimensional: every fixed
finite output marginal may look regular while the full transformed law is
singular.  We resolve this problem for a non-Gaussian i.i.d. product with
a positive density, finite second moment, and finite location and scale
Fisher information.  The weighted Fourier jet supplies finite-frequency,
dimension-free Hellinger witnesses for the non-Gaussian directions, turning
local mixing into Frobenius cost.  The global answer is that the Gaussian
continuous symmetry collapses to the discrete stabilizer \(\mathcal P\),
and the only continuous thickness that preserves the measure class is
Hilbert--Schmidt: for every bounded invertible \(T\), equivalence,
Hilbert--Schmidt proximity to \(\mathcal P\), and bounded projected
Hellinger energy are equivalent.  When the density has zeros, this metric
criterion has an exact support-aware extension: the rows of both \(T\)
and \(T^{-1}\) must avoid the zero set.  Half-line and bounded supports
then reduce the equivalence group to explicit permutation classes.
\end{abstract}
