\begin{abstract}
Feldman--H\'ajek theory classifies equivalence of Gaussian measures;
Kakutani's theorem compares product measures. Neither criterion classifies
general linear mixing of a non-Gaussian product, whose output coordinates
need not remain independent. We give this classification for centered
non-Gaussian i.i.d.\ coordinates with finite variance, a positive density,
and finite location and scale Fisher information. A bounded invertible
linear mixing preserves the measure class exactly when it is a
Hilbert--Schmidt perturbation of a coordinate permutation with
law-preserving signs; otherwise the image is singular. Thus the
Hilbert--Schmidt scale of Gaussian equivalence survives, but its reference
symmetries reduce from the orthogonal group to the coordinate symmetries
of the product. The new step is to recover this operator criterion without
a product factorization of the image law. The weighted Fourier jet detects
non-Gaussian mixing through dimension-free Hellinger bounds, and tail
exchanges turn local detection into square summability. For symmetric
stable products, the off-diagonal cost becomes \(\ell^\alpha(\ell^2)\),
while scales retain a quadratic cost. For densities with zeros, forward
and inverse preservation of the positivity set supplies the additional
condition for equivalence.
\end{abstract}
