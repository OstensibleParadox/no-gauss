\begin{abstract}
Kakutani's theorem tensorizes equivalence of product measures, but a bounded
invertible linear mixing operator \(T\) destroys the independence on which that test rests.
The resulting difficulty is genuinely infinite-dimensional: every fixed
finite output marginal may look regular while the full image law is
singular.  For a non-Gaussian i.i.d. product whose coordinate law has an
a.e.-positive density, finite second moment, and finite location and scale Fisher information, we prove
that equivalence, Hilbert--Schmidt proximity to the exact linear stabilizer,
and bounded projected Hellinger energy are equivalent.
The weighted Fourier jet supplies finite-frequency, dimension-free
Hellinger witnesses that turn local mixing into Frobenius cost.  We also
classify standard symmetric \(\alpha\)-stable products for
\(0<\alpha<2\).  In this infinite-variance branch, the exact cost is
anisotropic: diagonal deviations are square summable, while the
\(\ell^2\)-norms of the off-diagonal input columns are \(\alpha\)-summable.
Thus even an everywhere-positive coordinate density does not make
Hilbert--Schmidt proximity sufficient.
When a finite-variance density has zeros, the Hilbert--Schmidt criterion has
an exact extension by the zero-set row condition: the rows of both \(T\) and
\(T^{-1}\) must avoid the zero set.  Half-line and bounded supports then reduce the
equivalence group to explicit permutation classes.
\end{abstract}
