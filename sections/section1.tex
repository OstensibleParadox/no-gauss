\section{Introduction and Main Results}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Historical context: from Kakutani to dense linear mixing}
\label{sec:history}

Kakutani's theorem turns equivalence of infinite products into a scalar
summability problem: the product of the one-coordinate Hellinger affinities
is positive exactly on the equivalence branch, and vanishes exactly on the
singular branch~\cite{kakutani1948equivalence}.  This is one of the cleanest
forms of an infinite-dimensional zero--one phenomenon.  Its direct scope,
however, is coordinatewise.  Sharp criteria for sequences of Euclidean and,
more generally, Lie-group perturbations retain this product structure and
identify Fisher information as the local Hellinger metric
\cite{steele1986fisher,marques1992quasi}.  If a bounded operator mixes every output
coordinate with infinitely many inputs, the transformed coordinates cease
to be independent and the affinity no longer tensorizes.

The Gaussian law conceals this obstruction.  For the standard Gaussian
product, every orthogonal operator on \(\ell^2\) has a measurable row-series
realization and preserves the product law.  Translation and linear
measure-class changes are governed by the Cameron--Martin and
Feldman--H\'ajek theories, with explicit Gaussian density criteria developed
by Shepp; Ramer's theorem gives a nonlinear extension
\cite{cameron1944transformations,feldman1958equivalence,hajek1958property,
shepp1966radon,ramer1974nonlinear,bogachev1998gaussian}.  A non-Gaussian
product behaves differently: exact
independence rigidifies the orthogonal group to coordinate permutations and
to those coordinate signs already preserving the one-dimensional law.
The present paper identifies the corresponding measure-class enlargement of
that discrete stabilizer.  Non-Gaussian quasi-invariant measures on Hilbert
and linear topological spaces have long been known
\cite{feldman1961examples,takahashi1975quasi}; the issue here is instead a
complete classification for dense linear mixing of one fixed i.i.d. product
law.

There is a second obstacle.  Every fixed finite output marginal can have a
strictly positive density even when the full transformed law is singular to
the reference product.  Thus neither convergence on cylinder functions nor
any fixed-dimensional calculation detects the boundary.  The correct
completion must retain the entire directed family of projected likelihoods.
Our Hellinger energy does precisely this: bounded projected energy is
equivalent to positive global affinity, and a translation-ergodic zero--one
law then upgrades positive affinity to equivalence.

\subsection{The phase transition: continuous Gaussian symmetry and discrete rigidity}
\label{sec:phase-transition}

Let \(X\) be centered with finite first moment and characteristic function
\(\phi(u)=\E e^{iuX}\).  The fundamental detector is
\begin{equation}
 R_\mu(s,t)=t\phi'(s)\phi(t)-s\phi(s)\phi'(t).
 \label{eq:intro-fourier-defect}
\end{equation}
It is the first variation of a bounded, centered two-frequency feature under
a planar rotation.  The correct geometric curve is the weighted Fourier jet
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_\mu(u)=\bigl(u\phi(u),\phi'(u)\bigr)\in\mathbb C^2.
 \label{eq:weighted-fourier-jet}
\end{equation}
Up to sign, \(R_\mu(s,t)\) is the wedge product of
\(\Gamma_\mu(s)\) and \(\Gamma_\mu(t)\).  Hence zero defect means that
the weighted jet lies in one fixed complex line through the origin.  The
resulting global linear ODE has only Gaussian characteristic functions as
solutions.  This formulation is important: the unweighted curve
\(u\mapsto(\phi(u),\phi'(u))\) is not a straight line even in the Gaussian
case.

For a non-Gaussian coordinate law, a finite set of frequencies therefore
detects every infinitesimal matrix direction.  The corresponding Hellinger
orbit is uniformly coercive in Frobenius norm near the discrete stabilizer,
independently of dimension.  In infinite dimensions the surviving continuous
thickness is exactly Hilbert--Schmidt:
\[
 \text{exact stabilizer }\mathcal P_f
 \quad\leadsto\quad
 \text{measure-class stabilizer }
 \{T:\ TQ^{-1}-I\in\mathcal S_2\text{ for some }Q\in\mathcal P_f\}.
\]
This is the non-Gaussian phase transition studied below.

\subsection{Main theorem: equivalence, energy, and the stabilizer}
\label{sec:main-results}

We now state the assumptions used for the full arbitrary-mixing theorem.
Let \(f\) be a probability density on \(\R\), put \(g=\sqrt f\), and
assume
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 f>0\quad\text{Lebesgue-almost everywhere},\qquad
 g\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R),\qquad xg'\in L^2(\mathbb R),\\
 \E_fX=0,\qquad \E_fX^2=1,\qquad
 f\ne(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}
\end{equation}
All derivatives are weak derivatives.  Cusps are allowed, and no moment of
order greater than two is assumed.  The two Sobolev conditions are finite
location and scale Fisher information, respectively.

Put \(H=\ell^2(\N;\R)\) and
\(\mu=f^{\otimes\N}\) on the product Borel space \(\R^{\N}\).  The
space \(H\) indexes coefficients; a sample from \(\mu\) is not asserted to
belong to \(H\).  Define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Sigma_f&=\{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}:\Law(\varepsilon X)=\Law(X)\},\\
 \mathcal P_f&=\{Q:Q\text{ is a coordinate permutation with signs in }
 \Sigma_f\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:allowed-permutations}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\mathcal P_f\) is the full signed-permutation group when \(f\) is
even and the ordinary permutation group when it is not.  For
\(T\in\GL(H)\), let \(\Phi_T\) be the measurable row-series realization
constructed in \cref{lem:ng-measurable-action}, and write
\(\nu_T=(\Phi_T)_\#\mu\).

For probability measures \(\lambda,\nu\), set
\begin{equation}
 \Aff(\lambda,\nu)
 =\int\sqrt{\frac{d\lambda}{dm}\frac{d\nu}{dm}}\,dm,
 \qquad
 h(\lambda,\nu)^2=2-2\Aff(\lambda,\nu),
 \label{eq:ng-affinity-convention}
\end{equation}
where \(m\) dominates both measures.  Let \(P_n\) select the first
\(n\) coordinates and define the projected Hellinger energy
\begin{equation}
 E_n(T)=-2\log\Aff\bigl((P_n)_\#\mu,(P_n)_\#\nu_T\bigr).
 \label{eq:intro-projected-energy}
\end{equation}
These are actual output marginals, not laws obtained from principal matrix
corners.

\begin{theorem}[Complete criterion for arbitrary coordinate mixing]
\label{thm:ng-iid-endpoint}
Under \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}, for every \(T\in\GL(H)\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \nu_T\sim\mu
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists Q\in\mathcal P_f:\ TQ^{-1}-I\in\mathcal S_2(H)\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sup_nE_n(T)<\infty.
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:ng-global-candidate}
\end{equation}
If these conditions fail, then \(\nu_T\perp\mu\) and
\(E_n(T)\uparrow\infty\).  The exact bounded invertible linear stabilizer
is \(\Stab(\mu)=\mathcal P_f\).  On the equivalence branch, the projected
likelihoods and reciprocal likelihoods are uniformly integrable in their
respective reference measures.

For any admissible \(Q\), put \(K=TQ^{-1}-I\) and
\(T_n=I+P_nKP_n\).  Then \(T_n\) is eventually invertible and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d\nu_{T_n}}{d\mu}\longrightarrow\frac{d\nu_{TQ^{-1}}}{d\mu}
       \quad\text{in }L^1(\mu),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{d\nu_{T_n}}{d\mu}}
       \longrightarrow
 \sqrt{\frac{d\nu_{TQ^{-1}}}{d\mu}}
       \quad\text{in }L^2(\mu).
 \label{eq:intro-likelihood-closure}
\end{equation}
Since \(Q_\#\mu=\mu\), the limiting law is also \(\nu_T\).
\end{theorem}

The theorem contains the global energy formulation through
\(\sup_nE_n(T)\).  The Fourier defect is not an additional scalar
condition on \(T\): it is the calibration mechanism that turns small
Hellinger displacement into squared Frobenius displacement.  This distinction
keeps the one-dimensional Gaussian characterization separate from the
operator-valued equivalence criterion.

Two endpoint refinements separate the minimal assumptions cleanly.

\begin{corollary}[Moment-only stabilizer]
\label{cor:moment-only-stabilizer}
If the common coordinate law is centered, variance one, and non-Gaussian,
then its bounded invertible linear stabilizer is \(\mathcal P_f\).  No density
or Fisher-information assumption is needed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Orthogonal endpoint]
\label{cor:intro-orthogonal-endpoint}
If \(f>0\) almost everywhere, \(\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\R)\), and \(f\) is
centered, variance one, and non-Gaussian, then every orthogonal \(U\) obeys
\[
 U_\#\mu\sim\mu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 U-Q\in\mathcal S_2\quad\text{for some }Q\in\mathcal P_f.
\]
Thus scale Fisher information is needed for general linear mixing but not for
the orthogonal subproblem.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Organization and notation}
\label{sec:organization}

\Cref{sec:fourier-defect} proves the global Fourier ODE theorem and explains
the weighted-jet geometry.  \Cref{sec:finite-coercivity} constructs finite
Fourier duals and establishes dimension-free local Hellinger coercivity; it
also states the global coercivity theorem.  \Cref{sec:measurable-dichotomy}
constructs the measurable action and proves the likelihood and zero--one
machinery.  \Cref{sec:proof-main} proves the Hilbert--Schmidt criterion, then
uses a block-sum argument to complete the deferred globalization step.
\Cref{sec:applications} gives exact operator-level applications and records
the block and affine product criteria.  Numerical illustrations are deferred
to separate supplementary material.
