\section{The Rotational Fourier Defect and Gaussian Characterization}
\label{sec:fourier-defect}

The purpose of this section is to isolate the only genuinely
one-dimensional obstruction in the paper.  The detector is bounded, uses no
logarithm of the characteristic function, and remains valid in the presence
of characteristic-function zeros.  The conclusion belongs to the same broad
lineage as the Kac--Bernstein theorem and the classical characterization
theory of independent linear forms
\cite{kac1939characterization,kagan1973characterization}.  Its hypothesis is
different: an infinitesimal rotational Fourier response vanishes at every
frequency pair, leading directly to a global linear ODE.

\subsection{The bivariate Fourier feature and cross-dependence isolation}
\label{sec:ng-fourier-feature}

This subsection requires only $\E X=0$ and $\E|X|<\infty$; neither a
density nor a second moment is needed.  Write
$\phi(u)=\E e^{iuX}$, and for independent copies $X_1,X_2$ put
\[
 O_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
                         \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 W_{\xi,\eta}(x,y)
   =(e^{i\xi x}-\phi(\xi))(e^{i\eta y}-\phi(\eta)).
\]
This fixes the sign convention for rotation.  Centering removes the two
marginal responses and leaves only the dependence created by rotation.  In
particular,
\begin{equation}
 \E W_{\xi,\eta}(X_1,X_2)=0,
 \qquad
 \E|W_{\xi,\eta}(X_1,X_2)|^2
 =(1-|\phi(\xi)|^2)(1-|\phi(\eta)|^2)\le1.
 \label{eq:ng-feature-product-variance}
\end{equation}
Pointwise, $|W_{\xi,\eta}|\le4$, so its variance under an arbitrary
comparison law is at most $16$.  Both bounds are dimension-free and require
no tail assumption.

\subsection{A global linear ODE: zero defect characterizes Gaussians}
\label{sec:ng-fourier-ode}

\begin{lemma}[Logarithm-free characterization by rotational Fourier defect]
\label{lem:ng-fourier-ode}
Let
$G_{\xi,\eta}(\theta)=\E W_{\xi,\eta}(O_\theta(X_1,X_2))$.
Then $G_{\xi,\eta}(0)=0$ and
\begin{equation}
 R_\mu(\xi,\eta):=G'_{\xi,\eta}(0)
   =\eta\phi'(\xi)\phi(\eta)
          -\xi\phi(\xi)\phi'(\eta).
 \label{eq:ng-rotational-fourier-defect}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 R_\mu\equiv0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Law}(X)\text{ is a centered Gaussian, possibly degenerate}.
 \label{eq:ng-fourier-gaussian-characterization}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first moment implies $\phi\in C^1(\mathbb R)$.  The joint
characteristic function after rotation is
\[
 \phi(\xi\cos\theta+\eta\sin\theta)
 \phi(-\xi\sin\theta+\eta\cos\theta).
\]
The marginal characteristic functions are
$\phi(\xi\cos\theta)\phi(-\xi\sin\theta)$ and
$\phi(\eta\sin\theta)\phi(\eta\cos\theta)$.
Their derivatives at zero vanish because $\phi'(0)=i\E X=0$.
Differentiating the joint characteristic function therefore gives
\eqref{eq:ng-rotational-fourier-defect}.

If $R_\mu$ vanishes everywhere, continuity and $\phi(0)=1$ allow us
to choose $\eta_0\ne0$ with $\phi(\eta_0)\ne0$.  Fix this
single frequency.  The defect equation becomes the global ODE
\[
 \phi'(\xi)=c\xi\phi(\xi),\qquad
 c=\frac{\phi'(\eta_0)}{\eta_0\phi(\eta_0)},\qquad
 \phi(0)=1.
\]
No division by $\phi(\xi)$ has occurred.  Uniqueness for this
linear ODE gives $\phi(\xi)=\exp(c\xi^2/2)$ on all of
$\mathbb R$, including any putative zero of $\phi$.
The identity $\phi(-\xi)=\overline{\phi(\xi)}$ forces
$c\in\mathbb R$, and $|\phi(\xi)|\le1$ forces $c\le0$.
Writing $c=-\sigma^2$ proves the Gaussian conclusion; $\sigma=0$
gives the point mass at zero.  Substitution proves the converse.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Wedge-product structure and Fourier curvature}
\label{sec:ng-fourier-wedge}

Define the weighted jet
\[
 \Gamma_\mu(u)=\bigl(u\phi(u),\phi'(u)\bigr)\in\mathbb C^2.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \det\!\begin{pmatrix}
 s\phi(s)&\phi'(s)\\
 t\phi(t)&\phi'(t)
 \end{pmatrix}
 =-R_\mu(s,t).
 \label{eq:ng-fourier-wedge}
\end{equation}
Thus the defect measures the change of projective direction of the weighted
Fourier jet.  In the Gaussian case
\(\phi'(u)=-\sigma^2u\phi(u)\), so the jet lies in the fixed line
spanned by \((1,-\sigma^2)\).  Conversely, vanishing of every wedge product
forces precisely the anchored ODE used above.  This is the rigorous content
of the ``zero Fourier curvature'' description.

For a non-Gaussian law, some two distinct frequencies have nonzero
defect.  By continuity a compact neighborhood of this pair already
detects the law.  Thus a compact frequency set may be fixed after the
coordinate law is chosen, independently of the ambient dimension.
The diagonal $\xi=\eta$ always has zero defect and cannot serve this
purpose.  A global scalar energy is, for example,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_w(\mu)=\iint|R_\mu(\xi,\eta)|^2w(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi\,d\eta,
 \qquad
 w>0\ \text{a.e.},\quad
 \iint(1+\xi^2+\eta^2)w(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi\,d\eta<\infty.
 \label{eq:ng-fourier-defect-energy}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $|R_\mu(\xi,\eta)|\le\E|X|(|\xi|+|\eta|)$, so this
energy is finite and is zero exactly for centered Gaussian laws.
Integrability of $w$ alone need not make the energy finite.

The same feature gives a local Hellinger lower bound for one rotation.
Choose a phase $\alpha$ with
$\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\alpha}R_\mu(\xi,\eta))>0$ and apply
\[
 |\E_\lambda F-\E_\nu F|
 \le h(\lambda,\nu)
             \{2\E_\lambda F^2+2\E_\nu F^2\}^{1/2}
\]
to $F=\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\alpha}W_{\xi,\eta})$.
It follows that
$h((O_\theta)_\#\operatorname{Law}(X)^{\otimes2},
\operatorname{Law}(X)^{\otimes2})\ge c|\theta|$ for small
$|\theta|$.  A first moment suffices for this lower bound.  The
dimension-independent matrix estimates below require the additional
second-moment and Sobolev hypotheses.

\subsection{Dimension-free variance and affine normalization}
\label{sec:ng-fourier-variance}

Equation \eqref{eq:ng-feature-product-variance} is the variance estimate used
for empirical or finite-frequency detection: averaging \(N\) independent
pairs produces standard error at most \(N^{-1/2}\) under the reference
product.  The rough bound \(16/N\) remains available under an arbitrary
comparison distribution.  In particular, the detector does not require a
fourth moment, symmetry, or a density.

\paragraph{Centering, scaling, and phase.}
For an uncentered variable $Y$ with mean $m$, use $R_Y$ for the raw
expression on the right of \eqref{eq:ng-rotational-fourier-defect}.
Differentiating the centered feature now also differentiates its
marginal terms, and the actual dependence response is
\[
 D_Y(a,b)=R_Y(a,b)+im(a-b)\phi_Y(a)\phi_Y(b).
\]
If $X=(Y-m)/\sigma$, $\sigma>0$, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 R_X(\xi,\eta)
 =e^{-i(m/\sigma)(\xi+\eta)}
       D_Y(\xi/\sigma,\eta/\sigma).
 \label{eq:ng-centering-phase}
\end{equation}
Thus centering rotates the mean-corrected response by
$-(m/\sigma)(\xi+\eta)$; scaling also changes the frequencies.
At fixed corresponding frequencies its modulus is unchanged.  The
raw expression $R_Y$ alone does not transform by this phase factor.
For a centered symmetric law the defect is real, but at a particular
frequency pair an asymmetric law can also have a real or zero defect.
Consequently its phase is not, by itself, a complete measure of skewness.

For the concrete comparison $Y\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(2,1)$,
$m=2$, $\sigma=\sqrt2$, and $(\xi,\eta)=(1,2)$, one obtains
\[
 D_Y(1/\sqrt2,\sqrt2)=\frac4{27},\qquad
 R_{(Y-2)/\sqrt2}(1,2)=\frac4{27}e^{-3i\sqrt2}.
\]
At this particular pair, the entire phase of the Gamma response is
therefore the centering phase, namely $-3\sqrt2$ modulo $2\pi$.
The variance-one Laplace response is $R_\mu(1,2)=-4/27$.
Their moduli agree at these frequencies, while their principal phases
are approximately $116.915^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, respectively.
