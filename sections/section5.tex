\section{Density Zeros and Support Rigidity}
\label{sec:zero-set-row-condition-and-support-geometry}

When the density has zeros, Hilbert--Schmidt proximity controls Hellinger
accumulation but not absolute continuity.  The missing condition is whether
the rows of \(T\) and \(T^{-1}\) land almost surely in \(\{f>0\}\).  We prove
this criterion and then show that bounded or one-sided support forces
permutation rigidity.  Each result states its own hypotheses.

\subsection{The metric input when the density has zeros}
\label{sec:metric-input-with-density-zeros}

The Fourier/Fisher mechanism still supplies the metric part of the
classification; for its matrix estimates the appropriate regularity is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 f\geq0,\quad \int f=1,\quad
 g=\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\R),\quad xg'\in L^2(\R),\\
 \E X=0,\quad \E X^2=1,\quad f\text{ non-Gaussian}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:zero-set-row-assumptions}
\end{equation}
The global zero extension is essential.  Membership of \(g\) in
\(W^{1,2}(\R)\) rules out a hidden boundary jump and makes its weak derivative
vanish almost everywhere on \(\{g=0\}\).  Consequently the identity
\(sg=-2g'\) and the integrations by parts used by the Fourier/Fisher argument
remain valid on the whole line, with no boundary term.  Interior regularity
alone does not suffice: the uniform density has zero interior derivative but a
jump in its zero-extended half-density.

\begin{proposition}[Local Fourier coercivity with zeros of the density]
\label{prop:local-coercivity-with-density-zeros}
Under \eqref{eq:zero-set-row-assumptions}, the matrix Fisher operator decomposition,
finite-dimensional Hellinger path bound, and dimension-free local
Frobenius coercivity in
\cref{sec:rotational-fourier-defect-and-hellinger-coercivity} remain valid.
Write \(\mu=\rho^{\otimes\N},\quad\rho(dx)=f(x)\,dx\).
There are constants \(c,C,\delta>0\), with \(\delta<1\), such that
\begin{equation}
 c\|K\|_{\mathcal S_2}\leq h(\mu,\nu_{I+K})
       \leq C\|K\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \qquad(\|K\|_{\mathcal S_2}\leq\delta).
 \label{eq:local-hs-bound-with-density-zeros}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every \(T\in\GL(H)\), the following necessary
condition survives:
\begin{equation}
 \nu_T\ll\mu
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T-Q\in\mathcal S_2
 \quad\text{for some }Q\in\mathcal P.
 \label{eq:hs-necessity-with-density-zeros}
\end{equation}
For orthogonal transformations one may omit \(xg'\in L^2\),
retaining non-Gaussianity, \(g\in W^{1,2}(\R)\), and normalization to a
centered law with variance one.
These statements do not assert absolute continuity of
\(\nu_{I+K}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A Sobolev function has weak derivative zero almost everywhere on its zero
set.  Thus, defining \(s=-2g'/g\) on \(\{g>0\}\) and \(s=0\) elsewhere,
we have \(sg=-2g'\) on \(\R\).  All cutoff integration-by-parts identities,
including \(\E s(X)=0\), \(\E[Xs(X)]=1\), and the formulas for
\(J,\kappa\), remain valid.  Moreover, \(J=1\) would give the global
Gaussian equation \(g'=-xg/2\), while \(\kappa=0\) would give
\(2xg'+g=0\), which has no nonzero square-integrable solution.  Hence the
finite-dimensional Fourier/Fisher proofs apply unchanged.

For \(K\in\mathcal S_2\) with \(I+K\) invertible, put
\(T_n=I+P_nKP_n\).
The Hellinger path bound and invariance give, for large \(m,n\),
\[
 h(\nu_{T_n},\nu_{T_m})
 \leq C\|T_n^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
                 \|T_n-T_m\|_{\mathcal S_2}.
\]
Relative to one measure dominating
\(\mu,\nu_{T_1},\nu_{T_2},\ldots\), the square-root densities are therefore
Cauchy in \(L^2\).  Their limit has norm one, and convergence of finite row
sums identifies its law with \(\nu_{I+K}\).  Passing the finite-dimensional
bounds to this Hellinger limit proves
\eqref{eq:local-hs-bound-with-density-zeros}; it does not assert
\(\nu_{I+K}\ll\mu\).  For orthogonal operators, the polar approximants and
skew-generator bound from \cref{prop:orthogonal-sufficiency} give the same
conclusion.

The proof of \cref{lem:hilbert-schmidt-necessity} uses absolute continuity
only through
\cref{lem:tail-symmetry-uniform,lem:compact-covariance-rigidity},
both of which assume only \(\nu_T\ll\mu\); its remaining inputs are
moment-only rigidity, the measurable inverse action, and the local estimate
for finite-rank conjugated tail perturbations.  The estimate just proved
supplies the last input, so the same tail-matching argument proves
\eqref{eq:hs-necessity-with-density-zeros}.
\end{proof}

For a Gamma density centered with variance one, the zero-extended square root is in
\(W^{1,2}(\R)\) exactly when its shape parameter exceeds two.
For an affine rescaling of a Beta density, the corresponding condition is
that both shape parameters exceed two.
These assertions follow by squaring the derivative of the corresponding
boundary power.
At shape one the square root instead has a jump, so its vanishing
interior power derivative is not an exception.
The support classifications below require none of this regularity
and include every positive Gamma or Beta shape.

\subsection{The linear equivalence group for arbitrary zero sets of the density}
\label{sec:linear-equivalence-group}

Hilbert--Schmidt proximity remains the metric condition.  The zero-set row
condition adds the exact null-set requirement for \(T\) and \(T^{-1}\), using
the positivity set of the density rather than its closed support.

\begin{theorem}[The linear equivalence group with density zeros]
\label{thm:linear-equivalence-group}
Assume \eqref{eq:zero-set-row-assumptions}, let $\rho(dx)=f(x)\,dx$, and put
$\mathcal U=\{x\in\R:f(x)>0\}$.  For every $T\in\GL(H)$,
the almost-sure requirements below are called the forward and inverse
zero-set row conditions.
\begin{equation}
 \nu_T\ll\mu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \begin{cases}
 T-Q\in\mathcal S_2(H)\text{ for some }Q\in\mathcal P,\\
 \displaystyle
 \mu\!\left\{x:\sum_jT_{ij}x_j\notin\mathcal U\right\}=0
       \text{ for every }i.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:absolute-continuity-operator-criterion}
\end{equation}
Consequently the linear equivalence group of $\mu$ is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal G_\rho
 &:=\{T\in\GL(H):\nu_T\sim\mu\}\\
 &=\left\{T\in\GL(H):
 \begin{array}{l}
 T-Q\in\mathcal S_2(H)\text{ for some }Q\in\mathcal P,\\
 \displaystyle\mu\!\left\{x:\sum_jT_{ij}x_j\notin\mathcal U\right\}=0,\\
 \displaystyle\mu\!\left\{x:\sum_j(T^{-1})_{ij}x_j\notin\mathcal U\right\}=0
       \quad\text{for every }i
 \end{array}\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:linear-equivalence-group}
\end{equation}
This set is a subgroup of $\GL(H)$.  For orthogonal members of
$\mathcal G_\rho$, the same criterion holds with $T^{-1}=T^*$
and without the scale condition $xg'\in L^2$.

More precisely, whenever $T-Q\in\mathcal S_2(H)$ for some
$Q\in\mathcal P$, the Lebesgue decomposition of $\nu_T$ relative to
$\mu$ is its restriction to $\mathcal U^{\N}$ plus its restriction
to the complement: the former is absolutely continuous and the latter
is singular.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $T-Q\in\mathcal S_2$ and put $K=TQ^{-1}-I$.  Since
$\nu_Q=\mu$, we have $\nu_T=\nu_{I+K}$.  For the eventually invertible
operators $T_n=I+P_nKP_n$, \cref{prop:local-coercivity-with-density-zeros}
gives
\[
 h(\nu_{T_n},\nu_T)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence $\nu_{T_n}\to\nu_T$ in total variation.  Each $\nu_{T_n}$ has a
Lebesgue density in its first $n$ coordinates and agrees with $\mu$ on the
tail; since the density of $\mu_n$ is positive on $\mathcal U^n$, Fubini
gives
\[
 \left.\nu_{T_n}\right|_{\mathcal U^{\N}}\ll\mu.
\]
Passing to the limit proves $\nu_T|_{\mathcal U^{\N}}\ll\mu$, while
$\nu_T|_{(\mathcal U^{\N})^c}\perp\mu$.  Consequently
\[
 \nu_T\ll\mu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \nu_T(\mathcal U^{\N})=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mu\!\left\{x:\sum_jT_{ij}x_j\notin\mathcal U\right\}=0
       \text{ for every }i.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:hs-necessity-with-density-zeros}, this proves
\eqref{eq:absolute-continuity-operator-criterion}.

If $\nu_T\sim\mu$, the measurable inverse action gives
$\nu_{T^{-1}}\sim\mu$, so both row conditions are necessary.  Conversely,
$T-Q\in\mathcal S_2$ implies
\[
 T^{-1}-Q^{-1}=-T^{-1}(T-Q)Q^{-1}\in\mathcal S_2.
\]
Thus the two row conditions and
\eqref{eq:absolute-continuity-operator-criterion} give
$\nu_T,\nu_{T^{-1}}\ll\mu$.  The inverse action then yields
\[
 \mu=(\Phi_T)_\#\nu_{T^{-1}}\ll(\Phi_T)_\#\mu=\nu_T,
\]
and hence equivalence.  Finally, if $S,T\in\mathcal G_\rho$, measurable
composition gives
$\nu_{ST}=(\Phi_S)_\#\nu_T\sim\nu_S\sim\mu$, and the preceding argument
gives $T^{-1}\in\mathcal G_\rho$; thus $\mathcal G_\rho$ is a group.  In the
orthogonal case, the same restriction argument uses the approximants from
\cref{prop:orthogonal-sufficiency}, which require only location Fisher
information.
\end{proof}

Every nonzero row sum has an absolutely continuous scalar law, so the
condition is unchanged by modifying \(f\) on a null set.  It is automatic when
\(f>0\) almost everywhere, while the contraction in
\cref{sec:zero-set-row-condition-counterexample} satisfies only the forward
condition and therefore gives strict absolute continuity rather than
equivalence.

\subsection{Boundary behavior of the density and summability criteria}
\label{sec:boundary-behavior-and-summability}

The zero-set row condition governs absolute continuity, whereas projected
Hellinger energy governs accumulated overlap.  Hard and smoothly vanishing
boundaries separate these effects.

Let \(\rho\) be uniform on \([-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]\), let
\(\mu_2=\rho^{\otimes2}\), and consider \(T=\bigoplus_kO_{\theta_k}\).
Write
\(\delta_k=\min_{m\in\mathbb Z}|\theta_k-m\pi/2|\in[0,\pi/4]\).
For one block the affinity is the relative area of intersection
of a square and its rotation.
Removing the four corner triangles gives, for \(0<\delta\leq\pi/4\),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Aff(\mu_2,(O_\delta)_\#\mu_2)
 &=1-\frac{(\cos\delta+\sin\delta-1)^2}
                {2\sin\delta\cos\delta},\\
 1-\Aff(\mu_2,(O_\delta)_\#\mu_2)
 &=\frac{\delta}{2}+O(\delta^2).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:uniform-square-rotation-affinity}
\end{equation}
At \(\delta=0\), the affinity is one.  Each nonzero \(\delta\) leaves
positive area on both sides of the support difference.
Consequently \cref{thm:block-kakutani} gives the complete trichotomy
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{c|l}
  \delta_k=0\text{ for every }k&\nu_T=\mu\\
  0<\sum_k\delta_k<\infty&
      \nu_T\not\perp\mu,\quad \nu_T\not\ll\mu,\quad\mu\not\ll\nu_T\\
  \sum_k\delta_k=\infty&\nu_T\perp\mu.
 \end{array}
 \label{eq:uniform-square-block-trichotomy}
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\theta_k=1/k\) gives \(T-I\in\mathcal S_2\) but
\(\nu_T\perp\mu\).
With \(\theta_k=1/k^2\), the laws instead have positive affinity
and neither is absolutely continuous with respect to the other.

For comparison, rescale to variance one a density proportional
to \((1-x^2)^r\mathbf1_{\{|x|<1\}}\), \(r>1\).
Its zero-extended square root satisfies the Sobolev hypotheses.
Let \(f\) be this density, \(\rho(dx)=f(x)\,dx\),
\(\mu_2=\rho^{\otimes2}\), and \(g=\sqrt f\).
Writing \(J=4\|g'\|_2^2>1\), the skew-generator formula gives
\begin{equation}
 1-\Aff(\mu_2,(O_\theta)_\#\mu_2)=\frac{J-1}{4}\theta^2+o(\theta^2).
 \label{eq:smooth-boundary-rotation-affinity}
\end{equation}
Indeed, for the planar skew generator
\(A=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}\), one has
\(\|A\|_F^2=2\) and
\(h^2=\theta^2\langle A,\mathcal I A\rangle_F/4+o(\theta^2)\).
The only rotations preserving the square support are its signed
permutation symmetries.
Every rotated square shares a neighborhood of the origin with the
original square, on which both densities are positive; hence
\(\Aff(\mu_2,(O_\theta)_\#\mu_2)>0\) for every angle.
Affinity is continuous and strictly below one away from them,
so the three cases in \eqref{eq:uniform-square-block-trichotomy}
now use \(\sum_k\delta_k^2\) in place of \(\sum_k\delta_k\).
Equivalence still requires every \(\delta_k=0\).
Thus the quadratic metric supplied by the Fourier/Fisher method controls
accumulation of Hellinger loss, while the zero-set row condition separately
controls absolute continuity.

\subsection{Complete linear classification for bounded and half-line laws}
\label{sec:bounded-and-half-line-classification}

Write \(\rho=\Law(X)\), \(\rho^-=\Law(-X)\), and
\(\mu=\rho^{\otimes\N}\).
Throughout this subsection \(X\) is centered with variance one.
The operator action is the measurable row-series realization of
\cref{lem:measurable-row-series-realization}.

For bounded and one-sided supports, conditional expectations turn the support
boundary into an \(\ell^1\)-type row constraint, and the inverse relations
force a monomial operator.  No density or Fisher information is needed.

\begin{theorem}[Linear equivalence for bounded and half-line coordinate laws]
\label{thm:bounded-and-half-line-classification}
Let \(T\in\GL(H)\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Suppose \(a=\operatorname{ess\,inf}X<0<
 b=\operatorname{ess\,sup}X\) are finite.
 Then \(\nu_T\sim\mu\) forces \(T\) to be a signed permutation.
 The complete equivalence group is as follows:
 \[
 \begin{array}{c|l}
 \text{relation between }\rho^-\text{ and }\rho&\text{allowed signed permutations}\\ \hline
 \rho^-=\rho&\text{all signs}\\
 \rho^-\sim\rho,\ \rho^-\ne\rho&
                  \text{only finitely many negative signs}\\
 \rho^-\not\sim\rho&\text{no negative signs}.
 \end{array}
 \]
 In particular, \(a+b\ne0\) permits only ordinary permutations.
 The support need not fill an interval, and a density is not required.
 \item Suppose \(\operatorname{ess\,inf}X=a>-\infty\) and
 \(\operatorname{ess\,sup}X=+\infty\).
 Then
 \begin{equation}
  \nu_T\sim\mu
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  T\text{ is an ordinary coordinate permutation}.
  \label{eq:half-line-equivalence-classification}
 \end{equation}
 The same conclusion holds for a law bounded above and unbounded below.
\end{enumerate}
In the second case every equivalence is exact invariance.
Failure of any of these equivalence criteria does not, by itself,
imply singularity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(\nu_T\sim\mu\), and set \(S=T^{-1}\).
The measurable inverse action also gives \(\nu_S\sim\mu\).
For every row \(v\) of \(T\) or \(S\), the series
\(Z=\sum_jv_jX_j\) converges in \(L^2\), and
\begin{equation}
 \E[Z\mid X_1,\ldots,X_m]=\sum_{j\leq m}v_jX_j.
 \label{eq:finite-row-conditional-expectation}
\end{equation}

In the bounded case \(Z\in[a,b]\) almost surely, so its conditional
expectation does too.  The essential width of the finite sum in
\eqref{eq:finite-row-conditional-expectation} is
\((b-a)\sum_{j\leq m}|v_j|\); hence every row of \(T\) and \(S\) has
\(\ell^1\)-norm at most one.

Let \(A_{ij}=|T_{ij}|\) and \(B_{ij}=|S_{ij}|\).
The \(\ell^2\) row/column Cauchy--Schwarz inequality makes the entries of
\(TS\) and \(ST\) absolutely convergent, so \(AB\ge I\) and \(BA\ge I\)
entrywise.  Tonelli and the row bounds give
\[
 \sum_j(AB)_{ij}
 =\sum_k A_{ik}\sum_jB_{kj}\leq1,
 \qquad
 \sum_j(BA)_{ij}\leq1.
\]
Thus \(AB=BA=I\).  For each \(i\), choose \(k\) with
\(A_{ik}B_{ki}>0\).  The off-diagonal entries of \(AB\) force row \(k\) of
\(B\) to vanish outside column \(i\), and those of \(BA\) force row \(i\)
of \(A\) to vanish outside column \(k\).  This gives a bijective monomial
pattern.  Its weights and reciprocals are at most one by the row bounds, so
every weight is one; hence \(T\) is a signed permutation.

For a negative row sign, scalar equivalence requires
\(\rho^-\sim\rho\), and therefore \(a=-b\).  Each negative sign contributes
\(\Aff(\rho,\rho^-)\) to the product affinity.  This number equals one
exactly when \(\rho^-=\rho\); otherwise Kakutani's theorem
\cite{kakutani1948equivalence} permits only finitely many negative signs.
This proves the three alternatives.

In the lower-bounded, upper-unbounded case, centering and nondegeneracy give
\(a<0\).  Every finite sum in
\eqref{eq:finite-row-conditional-expectation} is bounded below by \(a\), so
no coefficient can be negative.  Its essential infimum is
\(a\sum_{j\le m}v_j\), whence
\[
 a\sum_{j\leq m}v_j\geq a,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \sum_jv_j\leq1.
\]
Thus \(T\) and \(S\) are nonnegative with row sums at most one, and the
preceding inverse-matrix argument makes \(T\) an ordinary permutation.  Such
permutations preserve \(\mu\); reflection handles the upper-bounded case.
\end{proof}

For \(Y\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(r_\Gamma,\theta_\Gamma)\), where
\(r_\Gamma>0\) is the shape and \(\theta_\Gamma>0\) is the scale, put
\[
 X=\frac{Y-r_\Gamma\theta_\Gamma}
         {\sqrt{r_\Gamma}\,\theta_\Gamma}.
\]
Its essential endpoints are \(-\sqrt{r_\Gamma}\) and \(+\infty\).
Thus \eqref{eq:half-line-equivalence-classification} gives the complete
homogeneous linear equivalence classification for every
\(r_\Gamma,\theta_\Gamma>0\), including the shape-two Fourier example in
\cref{sec:variance-and-affine-normalization}: only ordinary coordinate
permutations are allowed.
Compact support permits signs only to the extent allowed by the scalar pair
\(\rho,\rho^-\).
For compactly supported densities positive almost everywhere inside
\((-a,a)\), reflection equivalence is automatic, so an asymmetric such
density permits finitely many sign changes although they do not preserve the
measure exactly.
An asymmetric Beta law centered and rescaled to variance one has unequal
endpoint magnitudes, so it
permits only permutations.
These conclusions come from support geometry rather than from the
Fourier/Fisher estimates.

\subsection{Why the zero-set row condition is indispensable}
\label{sec:zero-set-row-condition-counterexample}

Even a rank-one perturbation separates positive Hellinger overlap from
two-sided absolute continuity.

Let \(f\) be the rescaling to variance one of
\(C(1-x^2)^2\mathbf 1_{\{|x|<1\}}\), and write its support as
\([-a,a]\).
Set \(\rho(dx)=f(x)\,dx\).
Its zero-extended square root belongs to \(W^{1,2}(\R)\), and
\(x(\sqrt f)'\in L^2(\R)\).
For \(0<c<1\), set
\[
 T=c\oplus I,\qquad
 f_c(y)=c^{-1}f(y/c),\qquad \mu=\rho^{\otimes\N}.
\]
Then \(T-I\) has rank one and
\[
 \nu_T=(f_c(y)\,dy)\otimes\rho^{\otimes\{2,3,\ldots\}}\ll\mu,
 \qquad \mu\not\ll\nu_T.
\]
Indeed, \(f_c\) is positive on \((-ca,ca)\) and zero on the rest
of \((-a,a)\).
Nevertheless,
\[
 \Aff(\mu,\nu_T)=\int\sqrt{f(y)f_c(y)}\,dy>0,
 \qquad \sup_n E_n(T)<\infty.
\]
Thus neither Hilbert--Schmidt proximity nor bounded projected Hellinger energy
implies equivalence without the inverse zero-set row condition.
The failure occurs with finite location and scale Fisher information.

\subsection{Exact block criteria without equivalence of the factors}
\label{sec:block-products-without-factor-equivalence}

The boundary examples use a block-product criterion that separates local
absolute continuity from accumulated Hellinger loss.

\begin{theorem}[Block products without equivalence of the factors]
\label{thm:block-kakutani}
Let \(\mu=\bigotimes_{k\geq1}\lambda_k\) and
\(\nu=\bigotimes_{k\geq1}\eta_k\) be probability products over
finite-dimensional coordinate blocks.
Set
\[
 r_k=\Aff(\lambda_k,\eta_k),\qquad
 L=\sum_k-\log r_k\in[0,\infty],
 \qquad -\log0=+\infty.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \Aff(\mu,\nu)=\prod_kr_k,\qquad
 \mu\perp\nu\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L=\infty.
 \label{eq:block-product-affinity-and-singularity}
\end{equation}
If \(L<\infty\), then
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \nu\ll\mu&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                         \eta_k\ll\lambda_k\text{ for every }k,\\
  \mu\ll\nu&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                         \lambda_k\ll\eta_k\text{ for every }k.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:block-product-absolute-continuity}
\end{equation}
Thus positive affinity allows equivalence, either direction of strict
absolute continuity, or neither direction.
For a linear block \(A_k\in\GL(d_k)\) and
\(\lambda_k=\rho^{\otimes d_k}\), where \(\rho(dx)=f(x)\,dx\),
the comparison density is
\[
 |\det A_k|^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{d_k}
                         f((A_k^{-1}y)_i).
\]
The local absolute-continuity conditions concern its actual zero
set, not just the closure of its support.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Affinity tensorizes on finite block prefixes, and projected affinities
decrease to global affinity by the conditional-expectation argument in
\cref{prop:two-sided-finite-marginal-ratio-tails}.  This proves
\eqref{eq:block-product-affinity-and-singularity}.

The local absolute-continuity conditions are necessary by projection.  If
\(\eta_k\ll\lambda_k\) for every \(k\) and \(\prod_kr_k>0\), set
\(\ell_n=\prod_{k\le n}d\eta_k/d\lambda_k\).  Independence gives
\[
 \|\sqrt{\ell_m}-\sqrt{\ell_n}\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2
       =2\left(1-\prod_{n<k\leq m}r_k\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Their \(L^2\) limit has norm one, and its square defines a probability
measure which cylinder tests identify with \(\nu\); hence \(\nu\ll\mu\).
Interchanging \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) proves the reverse assertion.  The local
condition cannot be omitted
\cite{kakutani1948equivalence,shepp1965translate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Masses of the absolutely continuous parts]
\label{cor:block-absolutely-continuous-masses}
Take \(m_k=(\lambda_k+\eta_k)/2\), with densities \(p_k,q_k\), and put
\[
 a_k=\lambda_k(\{q_k>0\}),\qquad
 b_k=\eta_k(\{p_k>0\}).
\]
If \(L<\infty\), denote the absolutely continuous parts of \(\nu\)
relative to \(\mu\), and of \(\mu\) relative to \(\nu\), by
\(\nu_a\) and \(\mu_a\), respectively.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \nu_a(\R^\N)=\prod_kb_k,\qquad
 \mu_a(\R^\N)=\prod_ka_k.
 \label{eq:block-ac-part-masses}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since \(r_k^2\leq a_kb_k\) and \(\prod_kr_k>0\), both displayed products
are positive.  On \(C=\prod_k\{p_kq_k>0\}\), the conditional product laws
have equivalent factors and affinities \(r_k/\sqrt{a_kb_k}\) with positive
product, so \cref{thm:block-kakutani} makes them equivalent.  The
restrictions to \(C\) are therefore precisely the absolutely continuous
parts, with masses \(\prod_kb_k\) and \(\prod_ka_k\), respectively.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Gamma scale changes and the effect of centering.}
Fix \(r_\Gamma>0\).  For the uncentered shape-\(r_\Gamma\), scale-one
Gamma density, positive
scaling by \(c\) preserves the scalar measure class and gives
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^\infty\sqrt{f(y)c^{-1}f(y/c)}\,dy
 =\left(\frac{2\sqrt c}{1+c}\right)^{r_\Gamma}
 =\cosh\!\left(\frac{\log c}{2}\right)^{-r_\Gamma}.
 \label{eq:gamma-scale-affinity}
\end{equation}
Integration of \(y^{r_\Gamma-1}e^{-(1+c^{-1})y/2}\) proves the formula.
Since \(\log\cosh(u/2)\sim u^2/8\) at zero, it follows that
\begin{equation}
 \bigotimes_k\Law(c_kY)\sim\bigotimes_k\Law(Y)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_k(\log c_k)^2<\infty,
 \qquad c_k>0.
 \label{eq:gamma-scale-product-equivalence}
\end{equation}
The condition is equivalently \(\sum_k(c_k-1)^2<\infty\).
This does not contradict \cref{thm:bounded-and-half-line-classification}:
for \(X=(Y-r_\Gamma)/\sqrt{r_\Gamma}\), the map \(Y\mapsto cY\) becomes
\[
 X\longmapsto cX+\sqrt{r_\Gamma}(c-1),
\]
an affine map fixing the lower endpoint, not the homogeneous
linear map \(X\mapsto cX\).

\begin{corollary}[Coordinatewise affine equivalence for Gamma products]
\label{cor:affine-gamma-classification}
Let \(\mu\) be the product of the shape-\(r_\Gamma\) Gamma law centered with
variance one.
For \(T\in\GL(H)\) and \(b\in\ell^2\), the law of
\(\Phi_T(x)+b\) under \(\mu\) is equivalent to \(\mu\) if and only if
there are a coordinate
permutation \(\pi\) and numbers \(c_i>0\) such that
\[
 (Tx)_i=c_ix_{\pi(i)},\qquad
 b_i=\sqrt{r_\Gamma}(c_i-1),\qquad
 \sum_i(\log c_i)^2<\infty.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The inverse affine map has linear part \(T^{-1}\) and shift
\(-T^{-1}b\in\ell^2\).
For the row-series realizations, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(\Phi_S(z+b)=\Phi_S(z)+Sb\) wherever the selected row limits exist,
for every \(b\in\ell^2\).
The affine orbit satisfies the covariance bound required by
\cref{lem:measurable-row-series-realization}; hence that lemma and the
translation identity show that the inverse affine map also preserves the
measure class.  Conditioning finite row sums, as in
\cref{thm:bounded-and-half-line-classification}, shows that both \(T\) and
\(T^{-1}\) have nonnegative entries: a negative coefficient would create an
unbounded lower tail that no fixed shift can remove.  The inverse identities
then make \(T\) monomial with positive weights \(c_i\).  Equality of the
scalar lower endpoints forces
\(-\sqrt{r_\Gamma}c_i+b_i=-\sqrt{r_\Gamma}\).
Now \eqref{eq:gamma-scale-product-equivalence} proves necessity and
sufficiency.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact finite-marginal equivalence criterion}
\label{sec:finite-marginal-equivalence-criterion}

The following finite-marginal criterion isolates the measure-theoretic content
without Sobolev assumptions or a dichotomy; under
\eqref{eq:zero-set-row-assumptions}, \cref{thm:linear-equivalence-group}
specializes it to bounded invertible linear mixing.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-marginal tails characterize equivalence]
\label{prop:two-sided-finite-marginal-ratio-tails}
Let \(\mu,\nu\) be any probability measures on \(\R^\N\), and let
\(\mu_n,\nu_n\) be their first \(n\) marginals.
Choose densities \(p_n,q_n\) with respect to any common dominating
measure \(m_n\).
For \(M>1\), define
\[
 \Tail_n(M)=
 \int_{\{q_n>Mp_n\}}q_n\,dm_n+
 \int_{\{p_n>Mq_n\}}p_n\,dm_n.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mu\sim\nu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_n\Tail_n(M)=0.
 \label{eq:two-sided-finite-marginal-ui}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, every finite pair is equivalent and its forward and
reverse likelihood-ratio martingales are uniformly integrable in their
respective reference measures.
Without any finite-marginal equivalence assumption,
\begin{equation}
 \Aff(\mu_n,\nu_n)\downarrow\Aff(\mu,\nu),\qquad
 \sup_n[-2\log\Aff(\mu_n,\nu_n)]<\infty
     \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mu\not\perp\nu.
 \label{eq:finite-marginal-energy-overlap}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If the limit in \eqref{eq:two-sided-finite-marginal-ui} is zero, a set
where \(p_n=0<q_n\) must have zero \(\nu_n\)-mass for every \(n\);
the second integral gives the reverse, so \(\mu_n\sim\nu_n\).  With
\(\ell_n(x)=q_n(\pi_n x)/p_n(\pi_n x)\), the two summands in
\(\Tail_n(M)\) are
\[
 \E_\mu[\ell_n\mathbf1_{\{\ell_n>M\}}],
 \qquad
 \E_\nu[\ell_n^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{\ell_n^{-1}>M\}}].
\]
Thus the displayed limit is exactly uniform integrability of the two
mean-one likelihood-ratio martingales.  Their \(L^1\) limits have mass one,
and cylinder tests identify the corresponding absolutely continuous
measures, proving mutual absolute continuity.  Conversely, under
equivalence the martingales are conditional expectations of the full
likelihood ratio and its reciprocal, and are therefore uniformly integrable.

For the affinity assertion put \(m=(\mu+\nu)/2\),
\(u=d\mu/dm\), and \(v=d\nu/dm\).
For \(\mathcal F_n=\sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), set
\(u_n=\E_m[u\mid\mathcal F_n]\) and
\(v_n=\E_m[v\mid\mathcal F_n]\).
Since \(u,v\le2\), martingale convergence and bounded convergence give
\[
 \Aff(\mu_n,\nu_n)
       =\int\sqrt{u_nv_n}\,dm
       \longrightarrow\int\sqrt{uv}\,dm.
\]
Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives monotonicity, and global affinity vanishes
exactly for mutually singular measures.  This proves
\eqref{eq:finite-marginal-energy-overlap}.
\end{proof}

For bounded invertible linear mixing, finite output marginals still have
Lebesgue densities, though their zero sets may differ from those of the product
density.  Thus \eqref{eq:two-sided-finite-marginal-ui} remains exact;
\cref{thm:linear-equivalence-group,thm:bounded-and-half-line-classification}
turn it into explicit operator classifications in the regular zero-set and
support-boundary regimes.

\subsection{What the Finite-Variance Hypotheses Control}
\label{sec:finite-variance-hypothesis-roles}

It remains to isolate the roles of the analytic hypotheses within the
positive-density regime.

The a.e.-positive-density theorem includes the centered, variance-one Laplace
density despite its cusp, as well as the normalized density proportional to
\(e^{-x^4}\); symmetry is never used.

For examples allowing both asymmetry and heavy tails, take a density
proportional to
\begin{equation}
 (1+y^2)^{-(\tau+1)/2}
                           e^{\vartheta\arctan y},
 \qquad \tau>2,\quad \vartheta\in\R,
 \label{eq:asymmetric-heavy-tail-example}
\end{equation}
and center and rescale to variance one.
Its location score before centering and rescaling is
\[
 \frac{(\tau+1)y-\vartheta}{1+y^2}.
\]
The score and its product with \(y\) are bounded, so centering and rescaling
give an everywhere-positive example satisfying \eqref{eq:main-assumptions}.
For \(2<\tau\le4\) its fourth moment is infinite, and for
\(\vartheta\ne0\) its two tails have unequal leading constants.  Thus the
theorem assumes neither evenness nor a fourth moment; \(\vartheta=0\) recovers
a rescaled Student \(t\) family.

The centered Gamma example has a half-line zero set: its rotational Fourier
defect detects local rotations, while the support theorem leaves only
coordinate permutations for homogeneous linear equivalence.
\Cref{cor:affine-gamma-classification} treats the distinct affine problem, in
which support-preserving scales can remain equivalent.

The hypotheses enter at different points.  Support controls null-set
admissibility.  The second moment makes every \(\ell^2\) row a convergent random
series, supplies uniform integrability of squared linear forms, and enables the
Gaussian-summand extraction in \cref{lem:unit-row-rigidity}.  Hence the
first-moment ODE result has strictly broader moment scope than
\cref{thm:measure-equivalence}.

Location and scale Fisher information control the Hellinger paths and their
dimension-free generator bounds.  The test functions \(H_A\) require no
higher moments: their role is to pair those bounds with every matrix tangent.
Tail symmetry then avoids a separate mixed-law variance estimate.  This
division of labor explains why the orthogonal theorem drops scale Fisher
information, while the full bounded invertible theorem retains it.
