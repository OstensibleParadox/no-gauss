\section{Introduction and Main Results}
\label{sec:introduction-and-main-results}

\subsection{Historical context: from Kakutani to dense linear mixing}
\label{sec:history}

Kakutani's theorem turns equivalence of infinite products into a scalar
summability problem.
The test is the product of one-coordinate Hellinger affinities.
This product is positive exactly on the equivalence branch,
and zero exactly on the singular branch~\cite{kakutani1948equivalence}.
This is one of the cleanest infinite-dimensional zero--one phenomena.

Its direct scope is coordinatewise.
Sharp criteria for sequences of Euclidean and, more generally, Lie-group
perturbations retain this product structure.
These criteria identify Fisher information as the local Hellinger metric
\cite{steele1986fisher,marques1992quasi}.  If a bounded operator mixes every output
coordinate with infinitely many inputs, the transformed coordinates cease
to be independent and the affinity no longer tensorizes.

The Gaussian law conceals this obstruction.
For the standard Gaussian product, every orthogonal operator on \(\ell^2\) has
a measurable row-series realization and preserves the product law.
Translation and linear measure-class changes are governed by the Cameron--Martin
and Feldman--H\'ajek theories.
Explicit Gaussian density criteria were developed by Shepp.
Ramer's theorem gives a nonlinear extension
\cite{cameron1944transformations,feldman1958equivalence,hajek1958property,
shepp1966radon,ramer1974nonlinear,bogachev1998gaussian}.  A non-Gaussian
product behaves differently.
Exact independence rigidifies the orthogonal group to coordinate permutations and
to those coordinate signs already preserving the one-dimensional law.
The present paper identifies the corresponding measure-class enlargement of
that exact linear stabilizer.
Non-Gaussian quasi-invariant measures on Hilbert and linear topological
spaces have long been known
\cite{feldman1961examples,takahashi1975quasi}; the issue here is instead a
complete classification for dense linear mixing of one fixed i.i.d. product
law.

There is a second obstacle.
Every fixed finite output marginal can have a strictly positive density even
when the full transformed law is singular to the reference product.
Hence neither convergence on cylinder functions nor fixed-dimensional
calculations detect the boundary.
The correct completion must retain the entire directed family of finite-marginal likelihood ratios.
Our Hellinger energy does precisely this: bounded projected energy is
equivalent to positive global affinity, and a translation-ergodic zero--one
law then upgrades positive affinity to equivalence.

\subsection{The phase transition: continuous Gaussian symmetry and discrete rigidity}
\label{sec:phase-transition}

Let \(X\) have a centered probability law \(\rho\) with finite first moment
and characteristic function \(\phi(u)=\E e^{iuX}\).
Define the rotational Fourier defect by
\begin{equation}
 R(\xi,\eta)=\eta\phi'(\xi)\phi(\eta)-\xi\phi(\xi)\phi'(\eta).
 \label{eq:fourier-defect}
\end{equation}
It is the first variation of the expectation of a product of centered
complex exponentials under a planar rotation.  Its determinant representation
uses the weighted Fourier jet, the explicit curve
\begin{equation}
 u\longmapsto\bigl(u\phi(u),\phi'(u)\bigr)\in\mathbb C^2.
 \label{eq:weighted-fourier-jet}
\end{equation}
Up to sign, \(R(\xi,\eta)\) is the determinant of the two vectors
on this curve at \(\xi\) and \(\eta\).
Hence zero rotational Fourier defect means that the curve lies in one fixed complex line
through the origin.
The resulting global linear ODE has only Gaussian characteristic functions
as solutions.
This formulation is important.
For a nondegenerate Gaussian law, the unweighted curve
\(u\mapsto(\phi(u),\phi'(u))\) is not contained in a straight line.

Under the moment and Sobolev assumptions below, a finite set of frequencies
detects every infinitesimal matrix direction for a non-Gaussian coordinate law.
The corresponding Hellinger orbit is uniformly coercive in Frobenius norm
near the exact linear stabilizer, independently of dimension.
In infinite dimensions the surviving continuous thickness is exactly
Hilbert--Schmidt: equivalence holds precisely for Hilbert--Schmidt
perturbations of coordinate permutations whose signs preserve \(\rho\).
This is the non-Gaussian phase transition studied below.

\subsection{Main theorem: equivalence, energy, and the stabilizer}
\label{sec:main-results}

We now state the assumptions used for the full arbitrary-mixing theorem.
Suppose \(\rho(dx)=f(x)\,dx\), put \(g=\sqrt f\), and
assume
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 f>0\quad\text{Lebesgue-almost everywhere},\qquad
 g\in W^{1,2}(\R),\qquad xg'\in L^2(\R),\\
 \E X=0,\qquad \E X^2=1,\qquad
 f\ne(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:main-assumptions}
\end{equation}
All derivatives are weak derivatives.
Cusps are allowed.
No moment of order greater than two is assumed.
The two Sobolev conditions are finite location and scale Fisher information.

Put \(H=\ell^2(\N;\R)\) and
\(\mu=\rho^{\otimes\N}\) on the product Borel space \(\R^{\N}\),
and \(\mu_n=\rho^{\otimes n}\).  The
space \(H\) indexes coefficients.  Write \(\mathcal B(H)\) for its
bounded linear operators, \(\GL(H)\) for the invertible members, and
\((e_i)_{i\ge1}\) for the standard coordinate basis.
A sample from \(\mu\) is not asserted to belong to \(H\).
Define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal P=\{Q:{}&(Qx)_i=\varepsilon_i x_{\pi(i)},\quad
       \pi\text{ a permutation of }\N,\\
       &\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\},\quad\Law(\varepsilon_iX)=\rho
       \text{ for every }i\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:allowed-permutations}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\mathcal P\) is the full signed-permutation group when \(\rho\)
is symmetric, and the ordinary permutation group otherwise.
This definition also applies when \(\rho\) has no density.
For a bounded linear operator \(T\) on \(H\), let \(\Phi_T\) be the measurable row-series realization
constructed in \cref{lem:measurable-action}.
Write \(\nu_T=(\Phi_T)_\#\mu\).

For probability measures \(\lambda,\nu\), set
\begin{equation}
 \Aff(\lambda,\nu)
 =\int\sqrt{\frac{d\lambda}{dm}\frac{d\nu}{dm}}\,dm,
 \qquad
 h(\lambda,\nu)^2=2-2\Aff(\lambda,\nu),
 \label{eq:affinity-convention}
\end{equation}
where \(m\) dominates both measures.  We use the same notation for
densities and their probability measures.  Let \(P_n:H\to H\) be the
orthogonal projection onto the first \(n\) coordinate vectors, and let
\(\pi_n:\R^{\N}\to\R^n\) extract the first \(n\) coordinates.
Write \(\mathcal S_2(H)\) for the Hilbert--Schmidt ideal.  Define the
projected Hellinger energy by
\begin{equation}
 E_n(T)=-2\log\Aff\bigl(\mu_n,(\pi_n)_\#\nu_T\bigr).
 \label{eq:projected-hellinger-energy}
\end{equation}
These are actual output marginals.
They are not laws obtained from principal matrix corners.

\begin{theorem}[Complete criterion for arbitrary coordinate mixing]
\label{thm:measure-equivalence}
Under \eqref{eq:main-assumptions}, for every \(T\in\GL(H)\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \nu_T\sim\mu
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists Q\in\mathcal P:\ TQ^{-1}-I\in\mathcal S_2(H)\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sup_nE_n(T)<\infty.
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:measure-equivalence}
\end{equation}
If these conditions fail, then \(\nu_T\perp\mu\) and
\(E_n(T)\uparrow\infty\).  The exact bounded invertible linear stabilizer
is \(\mathcal P\): \(\nu_T=\mu\) if and only if \(T\in\mathcal P\).
On the equivalence branch, the finite-marginal likelihood ratios and their reciprocals are uniformly integrable in their
respective reference measures.

For any admissible \(Q\), put \(K=TQ^{-1}-I\) and
\(T_n=I+P_nKP_n\).  Then \(T_n\) is eventually invertible and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d\nu_{T_n}}{d\mu}\longrightarrow\frac{d\nu_{TQ^{-1}}}{d\mu}
       \quad\text{in }L^1(\mu),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{d\nu_{T_n}}{d\mu}}
       \longrightarrow
 \sqrt{\frac{d\nu_{TQ^{-1}}}{d\mu}}
       \quad\text{in }L^2(\mu).
 \label{eq:likelihood-convergence}
\end{equation}
Since \(\nu_Q=\mu\), the limiting law is also \(\nu_T\).
\end{theorem}

The theorem contains the global energy formulation through
\(\sup_nE_n(T)\).
The rotational Fourier defect is not an additional scalar condition on \(T\).
It is the calibration mechanism.
It turns small Hellinger displacement into squared Frobenius displacement.
This distinction keeps the one-dimensional Gaussian characterization separate
from the operator-valued equivalence criterion.

Two refinements identify the assumptions needed for the stabilizer and
for orthogonal transformations.

\begin{corollary}[Moment-only stabilizer]
\label{cor:moment-only-stabilizer}
If \(\rho\) is centered, variance one, and non-Gaussian,
then the bounded invertible linear stabilizer of
\(\mu=\rho^{\otimes\N}\) is \(\mathcal P\).  No density
or Fisher-information assumption is needed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Equivalence under orthogonal transformations]
\label{cor:orthogonal-equivalence}
If \(f>0\) almost everywhere, \(\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\R)\), and \(f\) is
centered, variance one, and non-Gaussian, then every orthogonal \(U\) obeys
\[
 \nu_U\sim\mu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 U-Q\in\mathcal S_2\quad\text{for some }Q\in\mathcal P.
\]
Thus scale Fisher information is needed for general linear mixing but not for
the orthogonal subproblem.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Organization and notation}
\label{sec:organization}

\Cref{sec:rotational-fourier-defect-and-gaussian-characterization} proves the Gaussian characterization by
rotational Fourier defect and explains its determinant representation.
\Cref{sec:finite-dimensional-hellinger-coercivity} constructs finite real Fourier sums with
dual moments and establishes dimension-free local Hellinger coercivity; it
also states the global coercivity theorem.  \Cref{sec:measurable-realization-and-dichotomy}
constructs the measurable action and proves the likelihood and zero--one
machinery.  \Cref{sec:proof-of-the-measure-equivalence-theorem} proves the Hilbert--Schmidt criterion, then
uses a block-sum argument to complete the deferred globalization step.
\Cref{sec:scope-and-sharpness-of-the-hypotheses} gives examples satisfying the hypotheses
and explains the roles of support, moments, and Fisher information.
\Cref{sec:density-zeros-and-the-linear-equivalence-group}
classifies the linear equivalence group for densities with arbitrary
zero sets under the Sobolev assumptions, reduces bounded and half-line
laws to explicit permutation classes, and gives block and marginal
criteria separating absolute continuity from positive affinity.

We write \(A^*\) for the adjoint, including the transpose of a real
matrix, and \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) for the operator norm.
The Frobenius inner product is
\(\langle A,B\rangle_F=\tr(A^*B)\), with norm \(\|A\|_F\).
The law \(\rho\) remains fixed unless a different law is explicitly specified.
