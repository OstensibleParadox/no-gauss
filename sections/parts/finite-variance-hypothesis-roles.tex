\subsection{What the Finite-Variance Hypotheses Control}
\label{sec:finite-variance-hypothesis-roles}

It remains to isolate the roles of the analytic hypotheses within the
positive-density regime.

The a.e.-positive-density theorem includes the centered, variance-one Laplace
density despite its cusp, as well as the normalized density proportional to
\(e^{-x^4}\); symmetry is never used.

For examples allowing both asymmetry and heavy tails, take a density
proportional to
\begin{equation}
 (1+y^2)^{-(\tau+1)/2}
                           e^{\vartheta\arctan y},
 \qquad \tau>2,\quad \vartheta\in\R,
 \label{eq:asymmetric-heavy-tail-example}
\end{equation}
and center and rescale to variance one.
Its location score before centering and rescaling is
\[
 \frac{(\tau+1)y-\vartheta}{1+y^2}.
\]
The score and its product with \(y\) are bounded, so centering and rescaling
give an everywhere-positive example satisfying \eqref{eq:main-assumptions}.
For \(2<\tau\le4\) its fourth moment is infinite, and for
\(\vartheta\ne0\) its two tails have unequal leading constants.  Thus the
theorem assumes neither evenness nor a fourth moment; \(\vartheta=0\) recovers
a rescaled Student \(t\) family.

The centered Gamma example has a half-line zero set: its rotational Fourier
defect detects local rotations, while the support theorem leaves only
coordinate permutations for homogeneous linear equivalence.
\Cref{cor:affine-gamma-classification} treats the distinct affine problem, in
which support-preserving scales can remain equivalent.

The hypotheses enter at different points.  Support controls null-set
admissibility.  The second moment makes every \(\ell^2\) row a convergent random
series, supplies uniform integrability of squared linear forms, and enables the
Gaussian-summand extraction in \cref{lem:unit-row-rigidity}.  Hence the
first-moment ODE result has strictly broader moment scope than
\cref{thm:measure-equivalence}.

Location and scale Fisher information control the Hellinger paths and their
dimension-free generator bounds.  The test functions \(H_A\) require no
higher moments: their role is to pair those bounds with every matrix tangent.
Tail symmetry then avoids a separate mixed-law variance estimate.  This
division of labor explains why the orthogonal theorem drops scale Fisher
information, while the full bounded invertible theorem retains it.
