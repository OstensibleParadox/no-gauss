\section{An i.i.d. non-Gaussian equivalence criterion}
\label{sec:ng-linear-models}

For a regular non-Gaussian i.i.d. law, arbitrary bounded invertible linear
mixing preserves measure class exactly when it is a Hilbert--Schmidt
perturbation of a signed permutation.  The proof uses nonlinear Hellinger
estimates, rigidity of the tail cumulants, and a measurable
Radon--Nikodym closure.  Independence of the transformed coordinates is
never assumed.

\subsection{The density class and the endpoint theorem}
\label{sec:ng-iid-endpoint}

The arbitrary-mixing result below uses the following explicit assumptions:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 f(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)},\qquad V\in C^2(\mathbb R),\qquad
 V(-x)=V(x),\qquad \|V''\|_\infty\le L<\infty,\\
 \E_f X^2=1,\qquad \E_f|X|^p<\infty\quad(1\leq p<\infty),\qquad
 f\ne (2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}
\end{equation}
These are conditions on the one-dimensional density, not assumptions of
Hellinger coercivity, tail rigidity, or likelihood closure.  For example,
the densities proportional to
\(\exp(-x^2/2-a\cos x)\), \(0<|a|<1\), after rescaling to variance
one, satisfy them.  The all-moments assumption can be weakened to a finite
moment condition stated in \cref{sec:ng-moment-scope} below.

Put \(H=\ell^2(\mathbb N;\mathbb R)\) and
\(\mu=f^{\otimes\mathbb N}\) on \(\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}\) with its
product Borel sigma-field.  The space \(H\) indexes coefficients; the
random coordinate sequence is not asserted to belong to \(H\).
Write \(\mathcal P_\pm\) for the group of all signed coordinate
permutations of \(H\).  For \(T\in\mathrm{GL}(H)\), meaning bounded
and boundedly invertible, let
\(\nu_T=(\Phi_T)_\#\mu\), where \(\Phi_T\) is the measurable row-series
realization constructed in \cref{lem:ng-measurable-action}.

For any two probability measures define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf A(\lambda,\nu)
 :=\int\sqrt{\frac{d\lambda}{dm}\frac{d\nu}{dm}}\,dm,
 \qquad h(\lambda,\nu)^2:=2-2\mathsf A(\lambda,\nu),
 \label{eq:ng-affinity-convention}
\end{equation}
where \(m\) dominates both measures.  The value is independent of \(m\).
Let \(P_n\) select the first \(n\) coordinates and set
\[
 E_n(T):=-2\log\mathsf A\bigl((P_n)_\#\mu,(P_n)_\#\nu_T\bigr).
\]
These are actual output marginals; they are not the laws obtained by
replacing \(T\) with its principal matrix corner.

\begin{theorem}[Complete i.i.d. criterion for arbitrary coordinate mixing]
\label{thm:ng-iid-endpoint}
Under \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}, for every
\(T\in\mathrm{GL}(H)\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \nu_T\sim\mu
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists Q\in\mathcal P_\pm:\ TQ^{-1}-I\in\mathcal S_2(H)\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_n E_n(T)<\infty.
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:ng-global-candidate}
\end{equation}
The exact linear stabilizer is \(\operatorname{Stab}(\mu)=\mathcal P_\pm\).
If these equivalent conditions fail, then \(\nu_T\perp\mu\) and
\(E_n(T)\uparrow\infty\).  On the equivalence branch, the projected
likelihoods and reciprocal likelihoods are uniformly integrable in their
respective reference measures.  For sufficiently large \(n\), the
operators \(I+P_n(TQ^{-1}-I)P_n\) are invertible.  Their likelihoods
converge to \(d\nu_T/d\mu\) in
\(L^1(\mu)\), and their square roots converge in \(L^2(\mu)\).
\end{theorem}

The proof is given after the block and Fisher calculations.  The
dimension-free comparison in \cref{prop:ng-finite-hellinger-coercivity}
is a nonlinear estimate for Hellinger distance, with its natural saturation
at large distances.  Its local lower bound, applied to arbitrary tail
marginals, is sufficient for necessity.  No additive formula for the
Hellinger energy of a mixed product law is used.

\subsection{The exact block-product theorem}
\label{sec:ng-block-product}

Let
\[
  X=\prod_{j\geq 1}\mathbb R^{d_j},
  \qquad
  \mu=\bigotimes_{j\geq 1}\mu_j,
\]
on the product Borel sigma-field.  Assume that every \(\mu_j\) has an
everywhere positive Lebesgue density.  For invertible matrices
\(T_j\in\mathrm{GL}(d_j,\mathbb R)\), the coordinatewise map
\[
  T=\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}T_j
\]
is measurable on \(X\), and
\[
  T_\#\mu=\bigotimes_{j\geq 1}(T_j)_\#\mu_j.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:ng-affinity-convention}, define the one-block affinity and
action by
\begin{equation}
  \rho_j(T_j)
  :=\mathsf A\bigl(\mu_j,(T_j)_\#\mu_j\bigr),
  \qquad
  e_j(T_j):=-2\log\rho_j(T_j).
  \label{eq:ng-block-action}
\end{equation}
The strict positivity of the block densities implies \(0<\rho_j(T_j)\leq 1\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact block-product criterion]
\label{thm:ng-block-kakutani}
For every block-diagonal invertible \(T\) as above,
\begin{equation}
  T_\#\mu\sim\mu
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e_j(T_j)<\infty.
  \label{eq:ng-block-criterion}
\end{equation}
If the series diverges, then \(T_\#\mu\perp\mu\).  More precisely, for every
finite set \(F\Subset\mathbb N\),
\begin{align}
  \mathsf A_F(T)
    &=\prod_{j\in F}\rho_j(T_j),
  &
  E_F(T)
    &:=-2\log\mathsf A_F(T)
      =\sum_{j\in F}e_j(T_j),
  \label{eq:ng-block-tensorization}
\end{align}
and, whenever \(F\subset G\),
\begin{equation}
  E_G(T)-E_F(T)=\sum_{j\in G\setminus F}e_j(T_j).
  \label{eq:ng-block-increment}
\end{equation}
Thus singularity is exactly the escape \(E_F(T)\uparrow\infty\) along the
directed family of finite coordinate sets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both formulas in \eqref{eq:ng-block-tensorization} follow from tensorization of
Hellinger affinity.  Kakutani's theorem~\cite{kakutani1948equivalence}
says that two products of pairwise
equivalent probability measures are equivalent precisely when their infinite
product affinity is positive, and otherwise they are mutually singular.  In
the present notation,
\[
  \prod_j\rho_j(T_j)>0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  -\sum_j\log\rho_j(T_j)<\infty,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:ng-block-criterion}.  The increment identity is immediate
from additivity.
\end{proof}
The transformation parameter is not identifiable before quotienting by exact
symmetries of the block laws.  Put
\begin{equation}
  H_j:=\operatorname{Stab}(\mu_j)
  =\{Q\in\mathrm{GL}(d_j):Q_\#\mu_j=\mu_j\}.
  \label{eq:ng-block-stabilizer}
\end{equation}
Then \(e_j(T_jQ)=e_j(T_j)\) for \(Q\in H_j\); the natural parameter is the
right coset \(T_jH_j\), not the matrix \(T_j\) itself.

We next state a useful Hilbert--Schmidt form of
\cref{thm:ng-block-kakutani}.  Its assumptions are deliberately explicit:
they are the hypotheses which turn exact Hellinger energy into a quadratic
matrix coordinate, rather than conclusions of Kakutani's theorem.

\begin{corollary}[Uniform bounded blocks, modulo exact stabilizers]
\label{cor:ng-uniform-block-hs}
Assume the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(B\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \(\sup_jd_j<\infty\), every \(\mu_j\) is centered with identity
  covariance, and \(H_j\) is its exact linear stabilizer.  Consequently
  \(H_j\subset O(d_j)\) is compact.
\item The root-likelihood orbit
  \[
    \Phi_j:\mathrm{GL}(d_j)/H_j\longrightarrow L^2(\mu_j),
    \qquad
    \Phi_j(gH_j)=
    \sqrt{\frac{d(g_\#\mu_j)}{d\mu_j}},
  \]
  is a topological embedding with closed image.  Equip the quotient with the
  pullback metric
  \begin{equation}
    d_j^{\mathrm{root}}(gH_j,kH_j)
    :=\|\Phi_j(gH_j)-\Phi_j(kH_j)\|_{L^2(\mu_j)}.
    \label{eq:ng-root-quotient-metric}
  \end{equation}
  This metric is complete because its image is closed in $L^2(\mu_j)$.
\item With
  \[
    r_j(gH_j):=\min_{Q\in H_j}\|gQ^{-1}-I\|_F,
  \]
  there are constants \(r_0,c,C>0\), independent of \(j\), such that
  \begin{equation}
    c\,r_j(gH_j)^2\leq e_j(g)\leq C\,r_j(gH_j)^2
    \quad\text{whenever }r_j(gH_j)\leq r_0.
    \label{eq:ng-uniform-dqm}
  \end{equation}
  This is the required uniform quadratic-mean differentiability and Fisher
  ellipticity assumption.
\item The orbit action is uniformly proper at its maximum: for every
  \(\varepsilon>0\),
  \begin{equation}
    \inf_j\inf_{r_j(gH_j)\geq\varepsilon}e_j(g)>0.
    \label{eq:ng-uniform-properness}
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Let \(\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{j\geq1}^{2}\mathbb R^{d_j}\), and suppose
\(T=\bigoplus_jT_j\) is bounded and boundedly invertible on \(\mathcal H\).
Then
\begin{equation}
  T_\#\mu\sim\mu
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \sum_j r_j(T_jH_j)^2<\infty.
  \label{eq:ng-quotient-hs}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, there is a block-orthogonal
\(Q=\bigoplus_jQ_j\in\prod_jH_j\) such that
\begin{equation}
  TQ^{-1}-I\in\mathcal S_2(\mathcal H),
  \qquad
  \|TQ^{-1}-I\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2
   =\sum_j\|T_jQ_j^{-1}-I\|_F^2<\infty.
  \label{eq:ng-block-hs-representative}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \(\sum_je_j(T_j)<\infty\), then \(e_j(T_j)\to0\).
Uniform properness forces \(r_j(T_jH_j)\to0\), after which the two bounds in
\eqref{eq:ng-uniform-dqm} show that the two series are finite simultaneously.
The reverse implication follows from the same local comparison, since only
finitely many blocks lie outside the local chart.  Compactness of \(H_j\)
gives a minimizing \(Q_j\) in the definition of \(r_j\).  Since every \(Q_j\)
is orthogonal, \(Q=\bigoplus_jQ_j\) is a bounded orthogonal operator, and the
last equivalence is the standard block formula for the Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
The last assertion in \textup{(B2)} concerns the explicitly defined
one-block metric \eqref{eq:ng-root-quotient-metric}; it makes no completeness
claim for an infinite product quotient.  The class-preserving Cauchy structure
for the latter is defined and proved complete in
\cref{sec:ng-class-preserving-definition,sec:ng-admissible-completeness-theorem}.
\end{proof}

The exact quantity in this corollary is the accumulated action
\(\sum_je_j\), not a preassigned operator ideal.  The Hilbert--Schmidt
expression appears only after uniform DQM, ellipticity, and properness identify
that action with squared Frobenius distance in the quotient chart.

\subsection{An exact affine-coordinate theorem}
\label{sec:ng-affine-coordinate}

Let \(f>0\) almost everywhere be a nondegenerate density on \(\mathbb R\), put
\(g=\sqrt f\), and assume
\begin{equation}
  g\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R),
  \qquad xg'\in L^2(\mathbb R).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-sobolev}
\end{equation}
For \((u,v)\in\mathbb R^2\), let \(q_{u,v}\) be the density of
\(e^uX+v\) when \(X\) has density \(f\):
\begin{equation}
  q_{u,v}(y)=e^{-u}f\bigl(e^{-u}(y-v)\bigr).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-density}
\end{equation}
If \(\psi=-f'/f\) denotes the location score, differentiation of the square
root at the identity gives
\begin{equation}
  D\sqrt q_{0,0}(u,v)
   =u\left(-\tfrac12g-xg'\right)-vg',
  \qquad
  S_u(x)=x\psi(x)-1,
  \quad S_v(x)=\psi(x).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-scores}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{aff}}
  :=\E_f
  \begin{bmatrix}S_u\\S_v\end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix}S_u&S_v\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \delta(u,v):=-2\log\mathsf A(f,q_{u,v}).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-information}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal dilation and translation]
\label{thm:ng-diagonal-affine}
Assume \eqref{eq:ng-affine-sobolev}, assume that
\(I_{\mathrm{aff}}\) is positive definite, and assume that the affine
root-density orbit is proper, in the concrete sense that
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf A(f,q_{u_n,v_n})\longrightarrow1
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  (u_n,v_n)\longrightarrow(0,0).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-properness}
\end{equation}
For \(\mu=f^{\otimes\mathbb N}\) and the coordinatewise affine map
\[
  T_{u,v}(x)_j=e^{u_j}x_j+v_j,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
  (T_{u,v})_\#\mu\sim\mu
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  u\in\ell^2\ \text{and}\ v\in\ell^2.
  \label{eq:ng-affine-l2-criterion}
\end{equation}
Otherwise the two product measures are mutually singular.  In particular,
for positive diagonal dilations,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{diag}(a_j)_\#\mu\sim\mu
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  (\log a_j)_j\in\ell^2.
  \label{eq:ng-diagonal-dilation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Quadratic-mean differentiability and
\eqref{eq:ng-affine-scores} give
\begin{equation}
  \delta(u,v)
  =\frac14 (u,v)I_{\mathrm{aff}}(u,v)^{\mathsf T}
   +o(u^2+v^2).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-quadratic}
\end{equation}
Positive definiteness supplies two-sided quadratic bounds near the origin.
Condition \eqref{eq:ng-affine-properness} supplies a positive lower bound for
\(\delta\) outside every neighborhood of the origin.  Hence
\[
  \sum_j\delta(u_j,v_j)<\infty
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \sum_j(u_j^2+v_j^2)<\infty.
\]
The result follows from \cref{thm:ng-block-kakutani}, with one-dimensional
blocks.  Setting \(v=0\) and \(a_j=e^{u_j}\) gives
\eqref{eq:ng-diagonal-dilation}.
\end{proof}

For an \(\ell^2\)-valued realization, take
\(\operatorname{Law}((\sigma_jX_j)_j)\) with \(\sigma\in\ell^2\) and
\(\E X_1^2<\infty\), and use \(x_j\mapsto e^{u_j}x_j+\sigma_jv_j\).
Condition \eqref{eq:ng-affine-l2-criterion} then defines an affine
homeomorphism of \(\ell^2\).

\subsection{The Fisher operator for a general finite matrix}
\label{sec:ng-fisher-operator}

We first differentiate an arbitrary matrix acting in a finite block.  The
infinite-dimensional endpoint requires the nonlinear estimates proved below.
Let
\[
  f(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}
\]
be an even, centered density of variance one.  Put \(s=V'=-f'/f\), and
assume enough absolute continuity, boundary decay, and integrability to justify
integration by parts and the identities
\begin{equation}
  \E s(X)=0,
  \qquad \E[s(X)X]=1,
  \qquad
  J:=\E[s(X)^2]<\infty,
  \qquad
  \E[s(X)^2X^2]<\infty.
  \label{eq:ng-score-moments}
\end{equation}
Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\sim f^{\otimes n}\), and consider
\(T_t=I+tA\), for a real \(n\times n\) matrix \(A\) and sufficiently small
\(t\).  Differentiating the Jacobian and the product density gives the score
\begin{equation}
  S_A(X)
  =\sum_{i,j=1}^n A_{ij}
      \bigl(s(X_i)X_j-\delta_{ij}\bigr).
  \label{eq:ng-matrix-score}
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}
  \kappa:=\E[(s(X)X-1)^2].
  \label{eq:ng-kappa}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral decomposition of the non-Gaussian Fisher operator]
\label{thm:ng-fisher-spectrum}
Equip \(M_n(\mathbb R)\) with the Frobenius inner product.  Let
\(P_{\mathrm{diag}}\), \(P_{\mathrm{sym,off}}\), and
\(P_{\mathrm{skew}}\) be the orthogonal projections onto diagonal matrices,
off-diagonal symmetric matrices, and skew-symmetric matrices, respectively.
The Fisher quadratic form \(\mathcal I_f^{(n)}(A)=\E[S_A^2]\) is represented
by the self-adjoint operator
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \mathscr I_f^{(n)}
   =\kappa P_{\mathrm{diag}}
    +(J+1)P_{\mathrm{sym,off}}
    +(J-1)P_{\mathrm{skew}}.}
  \label{eq:ng-fisher-operator-spectrum}
\end{equation}
The diagonal, off-diagonal symmetric, and skew-symmetric eigenspaces have,
respectively, eigenvalues \(\kappa,J+1,J-1\) and multiplicities
\begin{equation}
  n,\qquad \frac{n(n-1)}2,\qquad \frac{n(n-1)}2.
  \label{eq:ng-fisher-multiplicities}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, writing
\[
  D=\operatorname{diag}A,
  \qquad
  S_0=\frac{A+A^{\mathsf T}}2-D,
  \qquad
  K=\frac{A-A^{\mathsf T}}2,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal I_f^{(n)}(A)
   =\kappa\|D\|_F^2
    +(J+1)\|S_0\|_F^2
    +(J-1)\|K\|_F^2.
  \label{eq:ng-fisher-decomposition}
\end{equation}
For the standard Gaussian, \(J=1\) and \(\kappa=2\), so the entire
skew-symmetric subspace is the kernel.  Under the stated regularity,
\(J\geq1\), with equality only for the Gaussian.  Hence, for a genuinely
non-Gaussian \(f\), if also \(\kappa>0\),
\begin{equation}
  \min\{\kappa,J-1\}\|A\|_F^2
  \leq \mathcal I_f^{(n)}(A)
  \leq \max\{\kappa,J+1\}\|A\|_F^2,
  \label{eq:ng-fisher-ellipticity}
\end{equation}
with constants independent of \(n\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Independence, parity, and \eqref{eq:ng-score-moments} give
\begin{equation}
  \E[S_A^2]
  =\kappa\sum_iA_{ii}^2
   +\sum_{i<j}
      \left\{J(A_{ij}^2+A_{ji}^2)+2A_{ij}A_{ji}\right\}.
  \label{eq:ng-fisher-pair-form}
\end{equation}
For each \(i<j\), put
\(S_{ij}=(A_{ij}+A_{ji})/2\) and
\(K_{ij}=(A_{ij}-A_{ji})/2\).  The corresponding summand becomes
\[
  2(J+1)S_{ij}^2+2(J-1)K_{ij}^2.
\]
Summing proves \eqref{eq:ng-fisher-decomposition} and the claimed spectrum.
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \(\E[s(X)X]=1\) and \(\E X^2=1\) gives
\(J\geq1\); equality forces \(s(X)=X\) almost surely and hence the standard
Gaussian density.  The final bounds follow by taking the smallest and largest
of the three eigenvalues.
\end{proof}

Gaussian orthogonal invariance makes every infinitesimal rotation invisible.
For a genuinely non-Gaussian product density, the skew eigenvalue is
\(J-1>0\), so the Fisher metric detects all matrix entries.  This remains a
tangent-space statement, not an infinite-dimensional endpoint criterion.

For rotations placed in independent two-dimensional blocks, the endpoint
statement is again exact by \cref{thm:ng-block-kakutani}: the local block action
is \(e(R_\theta)=\frac12(J-1)\theta^2+o(\theta^2)\), and the global criterion
is square summability of block distance to the exact rotation stabilizer.

\subsection{Measurable linear realizations and likelihood closure}
\label{sec:ng-measurable-closure}

Throughout this subsection, \(f\) satisfies
\eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}, \(\mu=f^{\otimes\mathbb N}\) on
\(X=\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}\), and \(\mu_n=f^{\otimes n}\).
The sample sequence is not an \(\ell^2\)-valued random variable.  We
therefore first specify how an operator on \(\ell^2\) acts on its law.

\begin{lemma}[Measurable realization on each countable operator orbit]
\label{lem:ng-measurable-action}
For every \(T\in\mathcal B(\ell^2)\), there is a Borel map
\(\Phi_T:X\to X\) whose coordinates are the \(L^2(\mu)\) row sums
\[
  (\Phi_Tx)_i=\sum_{j\geq1}T_{ij}x_j.
\]
These maps can be chosen so that the following statements hold.  If a
probability measure \(\lambda\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \int\left|\sum_j a_jx_j\right|^2\,d\lambda(x)
  \leq C_\lambda\|a\|_2^2
  \quad\text{for every finitely supported }a,
  \label{eq:ng-covariance-domination}
\end{equation}
then \((\Phi_T)_\#\lambda\) also satisfies this condition, with constant
at most \(C_\lambda\|T\|^2\), and
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_S\circ\Phi_T=\Phi_{ST}
  \quad\lambda\text{-almost surely}
  \qquad(S,T\in\mathcal B(\ell^2)).
  \label{eq:ng-measurable-composition}
\end{equation}
For each fixed countable subgroup \(\Gamma\leq\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\),
there is a \(\Gamma\)-invariant Borel set \(D_\Gamma\subset X\) of full
measure for every \(\lambda\) satisfying
\eqref{eq:ng-covariance-domination}, on which these restrictions form an
action by bimeasurable automorphisms.  In particular, this applies to
\(\mu\) and all its \(\Gamma\)-orbit laws, including singular orbit laws.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(a\in\ell^2\), the finite sums \(\sum_{j\leq n}a_jx_j\) have a
unique \(L^2(\lambda)\) limit \(L_a\), by
\eqref{eq:ng-covariance-domination}.  Choose
\[
  n_k(a)=\min\left\{n\geq k:
                \sum_{j>n}|a_j|^2\leq2^{-4k}\right\}.
\]
Define \(\widehat L_a(x)\) to be the limit of
\(\sum_{j\leq n_k(a)}a_jx_j\) when it exists, and zero otherwise.
The estimate
\[
  \left\|\sum_{j\leq n_k(a)}a_jx_j-L_a\right\|_{L^2(\lambda)}^2
  \leq C_\lambda2^{-4k}
\]
and Borel--Cantelli show that \(\widehat L_a=L_a\),
\(\lambda\)-almost surely.  This one definition therefore works for every
law satisfying \eqref{eq:ng-covariance-domination}; it does not depend on
knowing whether that law is equivalent to \(\mu\).  Set
\(\Phi_T(x)_i=\widehat L_{T^*e_i}(x)\).  The construction is Borel,
and the same is true jointly in the matrix coefficients of \(T\) and in
\(x\), since each tail norm and the integer choice \(n_k\) is Borel.

For finitely supported \(b\), the finite sum of output coordinates equals
\(L_{T^*b}\) almost surely.  This proves the asserted second-moment bound
for the pushforward.  For the \(i\)-th row of \(S\), finite output sums
satisfy
\[
  \sum_{j\leq n}S_{ij}\widehat L_{T^*e_j}
     =L_{T^*P_nS^*e_i}
     \longrightarrow L_{T^*S^*e_i}
     \quad\text{in }L^2(\lambda).
\]
The selected subsequence on the left also converges almost surely to
\(\Phi_S(\Phi_T(x))_i\), because the output law has the required
second-moment bound.  Uniqueness of the limit proves
\eqref{eq:ng-measurable-composition}.

For a countable \(\Gamma\), let \(B\) be the Borel set on which all its
row limits, identity relations, and pairwise composition relations hold.
The preceding argument gives \(\lambda(B)=1\) for every law under
consideration.  Put
\[
  D_\Gamma=\bigcap_{T\in\Gamma}\Phi_T^{-1}(B).
\]
Every orbit law satisfies the same second-moment condition, so
\(\lambda(D_\Gamma)=1\).  The composition identities imply that
\(D_\Gamma\) is invariant and that \(\Phi_{T^{-1}}\) is the inverse of
\(\Phi_T\) there.  This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

Write \(\nu_T=(\Phi_T)_\#\mu\).  The preceding lemma makes assertions
about a common carrier only for a fixed countable subgroup; no single
pointwise action of all of \(\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\) on a common conull
set is required.  The subsequences in its proof are also essential: a
covariance bound gives convergence of arbitrary row sums in \(L^2\), but
does not by itself give almost-sure convergence of all ordinary partial
sums under a correlated orbit law.  On the original independent product
law, the versions agree with the usual almost-sure row sums.

Write \(D(\lambda\Vert\nu)=\int\log(d\lambda/d\nu)\,d\lambda\)
for relative entropy, with value \(+\infty\) when absolute continuity
fails.

\begin{lemma}[A dimension-free entropy upper bound]
\label{lem:ng-finite-entropy}
Let \(L=\|V''\|_\infty\).  For every \(A\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb R)\)
with smallest singular value at least \(m>0\),
\begin{equation}
  D(A_\#\mu_n\Vert\mu_n)
  \leq C_L(m)\|A-I\|_F^2,
  \qquad
  C_L(m)=\frac L2+\frac{1}{2\min\{1,m\}^2}.
  \label{eq:ng-finite-entropy}
\end{equation}
The constant is independent of \(n\).  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  D(\mu_n\Vert A_\#\mu_n)
    =D((A^{-1})_\#\mu_n\Vert\mu_n).
  \label{eq:ng-finite-reverse-entropy}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(K=A-I\) and \(s=V'\).  The standing assumptions justify
integration by parts and give
\(\mathbb E[s(X_i)X_j]=\delta_{ij}\).  In particular, bounded
\(V''\), evenness, and finite second moment make the relevant integrands
integrable; cutoff integration by parts gives the diagonal identity.
The change-of-variables formula and Taylor's upper bound yield
\begin{align*}
  D(A_\#\mu_n\Vert\mu_n)
   &=\mathbb E\sum_i\{V((AX)_i)-V(X_i)\}-\log|\det A|\\
   &\leq\operatorname{tr}K-\log|\det A|
                  +\frac L2\|K\|_F^2.
\end{align*}
If \(\sigma_i\) are the singular values of \(A\), then
\(\operatorname{tr}A\leq\sum_i\sigma_i\) and
\[
  \sum_i(\sigma_i-1)^2\leq\|A-I\|_F^2.
\]
The latter follows by expanding both sides and using the former trace
inequality.  Taylor's formula for \(u-1-\log u\) gives
\[
  u-1-\log u
     \leq\frac{(u-1)^2}{2\min\{1,m\}^2}
     \qquad(u\geq m).
\]
Consequently,
\[
  \operatorname{tr}K-\log|\det A|
  \leq\sum_i(\sigma_i-1-\log\sigma_i)
  \leq\frac{\|K\|_F^2}{2\min\{1,m\}^2},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:ng-finite-entropy}.  Applying the invertible map
\(A^{-1}\) to both arguments of relative entropy proves
\eqref{eq:ng-finite-reverse-entropy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hilbert--Schmidt sufficiency and RN closure]
\label{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency}
Suppose \(T=I+K\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\) and
\(K\in\mathcal S_2\).  Then \(\nu_T\sim\mu\).  Set
\(T_n=I+P_nKP_n\), discarding the finitely many terms before these
operators become invertible.  Their densities
\(Z_n=d\nu_{T_n}/d\mu\) converge in \(L^1(\mu)\) to the strictly
positive density \(Z_T=d\nu_T/d\mu\), and their square roots converge in
\(L^2(\mu)\).  The inverse approximants have the analogous closure:
\[
  \frac{d\nu_{T_n^{-1}}}{d\mu}
     \longrightarrow\frac{d\nu_{T^{-1}}}{d\mu}
     \quad\text{in }L^1(\mu).
\]
Both \(D(\nu_T\Vert\mu)\) and \(D(\mu\Vert\nu_T)\) are finite.
If \(\nu_S\sim\mu\) and \(\nu_T\sim\mu\), the RN derivatives obey
\begin{align}
  Z_{ST}(y)
    &=Z_S(y)Z_T(\Phi_{S^{-1}}y),
       \label{eq:ng-rn-cocycle}\\
  Z_T(y)Z_{T^{-1}}(\Phi_{T^{-1}}y)
    &=1.
       \label{eq:ng-rn-inverse}
\end{align}
If \(Q\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\) is an exact linear symmetry of \(\mu\),
the same equivalence
conclusion holds whenever \(TQ^{-1}-I\in\mathcal S_2\) and \(T\) is
boundedly invertible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(P_nKP_n\to K\) in Hilbert--Schmidt norm, \(T_n\to T\) in
operator norm.  Thus \(T_n\) is eventually invertible and both
\(\|T_n\|\) and \(\|T_n^{-1}\|\) are uniformly bounded.  If
\(A_n\) denotes its first \(n\)-coordinate block, its density is the
explicit finite-dimensional likelihood
\begin{equation}
  Z_n(x)=|\det A_n|^{-1}
     \prod_{i=1}^n
       \frac{f((A_n^{-1}x_{1:n})_i)}{f(x_i)}.
  \label{eq:ng-finite-approximant-density}
\end{equation}
These approximant densities need not form a martingale.

The operators \(T_n^{-1}T_m\) act in a finite coordinate block and have
uniformly bounded norms and inverses.  The entropy lemma and finite
change of variables therefore give, with a constant independent of
\(m,n\),
\begin{align}
  D(\nu_{T_m}\Vert\nu_{T_n})
    &=D(\nu_{T_n^{-1}T_m}\Vert\mu)
      \leq C\|T_m-T_n\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2,
       \label{eq:ng-approximant-relative-entropy}\\
  \|Z_m-Z_n\|_{L^1(\mu)}
    &\leq\sqrt{2C}\|T_m-T_n\|_{\mathcal S_2}.
       \label{eq:ng-approximant-l1-cauchy}
\end{align}
The second line is Pinsker's inequality.  Hence \(Z_n\to Z\) in
\(L^1(\mu)\), with \(Z\geq0\) and \(\int Z\,d\mu=1\).
For each fixed finite set of output coordinates, \(\Phi_{T_n}X\)
converges to \(\Phi_TX\) in \(L^2\), by the row construction.
Bounded continuous cylinder tests consequently identify
\(Z\mu=\nu_T\).  In particular, this limit is the law of the stated
operator and cannot be an unrelated limiting density.  The inequality
\((\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\leq|a-b|\) gives convergence of square roots.

The same argument applies to the finite-block operators \(T_n^{-1}\):
\[
 \|T_n^{-1}-T^{-1}\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \leq\|T_n^{-1}\|\,\|T_n-T\|_{\mathcal S_2}\,\|T^{-1}\|
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
It identifies their \(L^1\) density limit as \(\nu_{T^{-1}}\), which is
therefore absolutely continuous with respect to \(\mu\).  On the common
carrier for the countable group generated by \(T,T_n\),
\cref{lem:ng-measurable-action} gives the inverse relations independently
of any absolute-continuity assertion.  Thus
\[
  \mu=(\Phi_T)_\#\nu_{T^{-1}}
        \ll(\Phi_T)_\#\mu=\nu_T.
\]
This proves equivalence and strict positivity of \(Z\).

The finite entropy lemma bounds
\(D(\nu_{T_n}\Vert\mu)\) and
\(D(\mu\Vert\nu_{T_n})\) uniformly.  Along an almost-surely convergent
subsequence of the densities, Fatou's lemma applied respectively to
\(z\log z-z+1\) and \(z-1-\log z\) proves finiteness of both limiting
entropies.  Change of variables on the common carrier proves
\eqref{eq:ng-rn-cocycle}; the case \(S=T\), with the second factor
indexed by \(T^{-1}\), gives \eqref{eq:ng-rn-inverse}.
Finally, \(T=(TQ^{-1})Q\) and \(\nu_Q=\mu\) give the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dichotomy for every bounded invertible orbit]
\label{lem:ng-orbit-dichotomy}
For every \(T\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\),
\begin{equation}
  \nu_T\sim\mu\quad\text{or}\quad\nu_T\perp\mu.
  \label{eq:ng-orbit-dichotomy}
\end{equation}
This conclusion uses only positivity of \(f\), its centered finite second
moment, and \(\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R)\); in particular, it holds
under \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(g=\sqrt f\).  The translation estimate
\[
  1-\mathsf A(f,f(\cdot-a))
     =\tfrac12\|g(\cdot-a)-g\|_2^2
     \leq\tfrac12\|g'\|_2^2a^2
\]
and Kakutani's theorem imply
\((\tau_h)_\#\mu\sim\mu\) for every \(h\in\ell^2\).
Under the standing assumptions, \(g'=-(V'/2)g\in L^2\), since
\(|V'(x)|\leq L|x|\).  The row realizations satisfy
\[
  \Phi_T(x+T^{-1}h)=\Phi_T(x)+h
  \quad\mu\text{-almost surely};
\]
this follows directly by adding the absolutely convergent scalar row
pairing with the \(\ell^2\) vector \(T^{-1}h\).
Consequently, \(\nu_T\) is also quasi-invariant under every
\(\ell^2\) translation.

Let \(H\) be the countable additive group generated by
\(\{qe_j,qTe_j:q\in\mathbb Q,\ j\geq1\}\).
Both \(\mu\) and \(\nu_T\) are quasi-invariant and ergodic under
translation by \(H\).  Indeed, an event invariant modulo \(\mu\) under
all rational finite-coordinate translations has, by positivity of the
finite-dimensional densities, almost-everywhere constant sections in each
finite coordinate block.  It is therefore a tail event modulo \(\mu\),
and has probability zero or one.  Here one uses the elementary fact that
a measurable function on \(\mathbb R^n\) invariant almost everywhere
under rational translations is constant almost everywhere, obtained, for
example, by convolution with smooth approximate identities.
For \(\nu_T\), pull an invariant event back by \(\Phi_T\); invariance
under \(qTe_j\) becomes invariance under \(qe_j\), giving the same
zero--one conclusion.

Write \(\nu_T=\nu_a+\nu_s\) for its Lebesgue decomposition relative to
\(\mu\).  Choose a Borel \(\mu\)-null set \(N_0\) supporting
\(\nu_s\), and let \(N=\bigcup_{h\in H}(N_0+h)\).
Quasi-invariance of \(\mu\) makes \(N\) a \(\mu\)-null set;
it is exactly \(H\)-invariant and still supports \(\nu_s\).
Thus \(\nu_T(N)=\nu_s(X)\), and ergodicity of \(\nu_T\) makes this
number zero or one.  The latter case gives singularity.  In the former
case, write \(\nu_T=Z\mu\).  Quasi-invariance of both measures implies
that \(\{Z>0\}\) is invariant modulo \(\mu\) under \(H\).
Its \(\mu\)-measure is positive because \(\int Z\,d\mu=1\), and
ergodicity of \(\mu\) makes that measure one.  This gives equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projected likelihoods and two-sided uniform integrability]
\label{lem:ng-projected-likelihoods}
For \(T\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\), every finite output law
\(\nu_{T,N}=(P_N)_\#\nu_T\) is equivalent to \(\mu_N\).
Writing \(\mathcal F_N=\sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\), define
\begin{equation}
  R_N(x)=\frac{d\nu_{T,N}}{d\mu_N}(x_{1:N}),
  \qquad
  \mathsf A_N(T)=\mathbb E_\mu\sqrt{R_N},
  \qquad E_N(T)=-2\log\mathsf A_N(T).
  \label{eq:ng-projected-likelihoods}
\end{equation}
Then \((R_N)\) is a nonnegative mean-one \(\mu\)-martingale and
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf A_N(T)\downarrow\mathsf A(\mu,\nu_T),
  \qquad
  \nu_T\sim\mu
    \Longleftrightarrow\sup_NE_N(T)<\infty.
  \label{eq:ng-projected-affinity-endpoint}
\end{equation}
Otherwise \(E_N(T)\uparrow\infty\) and \(\nu_T\perp\mu\).
If \(\nu_T\sim\mu\) with density \(Z_T\), then
\begin{align}
  R_N&=\mathbb E_\mu[Z_T\mid\mathcal F_N]
       \longrightarrow Z_T
       &&\text{in }L^1(\mu),
       \label{eq:ng-forward-likelihood-ui}\\
  R_N^{-1}&=\mathbb E_{\nu_T}[Z_T^{-1}\mid\mathcal F_N]
       \longrightarrow Z_T^{-1}
       &&\text{in }L^1(\nu_T).
       \label{eq:ng-reverse-likelihood-ui}
\end{align}
In particular, both likelihood martingales are uniformly integrable in
their respective reference measures.  In the Hilbert--Schmidt case of
\cref{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency}, they additionally have uniformly bounded
forward and reverse relative entropies.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first \(N\) rows of \(T\) are linearly independent, since \(T^*\)
is injective.  Some \(N\) input columns therefore form an invertible
\(N\times N\) minor \(A\).  Split the input into these coordinates
and their complement.  The first \(N\) outputs have the form
\(AU+W\), where \(U\sim\mu_N\) and \(W\) is independent of \(U\).
Conditioning on \(W\) writes their density as a mixture of translates
of the strictly positive density of \(AU\).  This mixture is positive
and finite almost everywhere, proving the finite-dimensional equivalence.

Consistency of projected laws proves the martingale assertion.  The
martingale limit is
\(R_\infty=d(\nu_T)_a/d\mu\), the density of the absolutely continuous
part of \(\nu_T\).  To see the identification explicitly, Fatou's lemma
on cylinder sets gives \(R_\infty\mu\leq\nu_T\), first on the cylinder
algebra and then on the generated sigma-field.  Conversely, if
\((\nu_T)_a=Z\mu\), then
\(\mathbb E_\mu[Z\mid\mathcal F_N]\leq R_N\), so martingale
convergence gives \(Z\leq R_\infty\).  The two inequalities identify
the limit.
Since \(\mathbb E_\mu(\sqrt{R_N})^2=1\), the square roots are uniformly
integrable in \(L^1(\mu)\).  They thus converge in \(L^1\) to
\(\sqrt{R_\infty}\).  Conditional Jensen's inequality gives the
monotonicity of their expectations, and consequently
\[
  \lim_N\mathsf A_N(T)
      =\int\sqrt{R_\infty}\,d\mu
      =\mathsf A(\mu,\nu_T).
\]
The dichotomy in \cref{lem:ng-orbit-dichotomy} now proves
\eqref{eq:ng-projected-affinity-endpoint} and the singular alternative.

Under equivalence, the forward conditional-expectation identity is the
definition of a projected density.  The reverse identity follows because
for \(B\in\mathcal F_N\),
\[
  \int_B R_N^{-1}\,d\nu_T=\mu(B)
       =\int_B Z_T^{-1}\,d\nu_T.
\]
Conditional-expectation convergence gives both \(L^1\) limits and hence
both uniform-integrability assertions.  The entropy bounds in the
Hilbert--Schmidt case follow from
\cref{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency} by conditional Jensen's inequality, or
equivalently by contraction of relative entropy under projection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dimension-free nonlinear Hellinger coercivity}
\label{sec:ng-nonlinear-coercivity}

We use the Hellinger convention \eqref{eq:ng-affinity-convention}.
All constants below are independent of both the number of observed
coordinates and the number of source coordinates.  The source may be
countably infinite.  Throughout this subsection, $X_j$ are independent with
an even, non-Gaussian density $f$, mean zero, variance one, and all moments
finite.  For the final matrix comparison we use the entropy estimate
\eqref{eq:ng-finite-entropy}, proved from
\eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}.

\begin{lemma}[A Hellinger detector inequality]
\label{lem:ng-hellinger-detector}
For probability measures $P,Q$ and a real statistic $F$ square integrable
under both measures, put $d=\E_PF-\E_QF$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 (1-h(P,Q)^2)d^2
 \leq 2h(P,Q)^2\{\operatorname{Var}_P F+\operatorname{Var}_Q F\}.
 \label{eq:ng-detector-inequality}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $h(P,Q)^2\leq1/2$, then
$d^2\leq4h(P,Q)^2(\operatorname{Var}_P F+\operatorname{Var}_Q F)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take a common dominating measure, write its densities as $p,q$, and set
$c=(\E_PF+\E_QF)/2$.  Cauchy--Schwarz and
$(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2\leq2(p+q)$ give
\[
 d^2
 \leq h(P,Q)^2\int(F-c)^2(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2
 \leq2h(P,Q)^2\left\{\operatorname{Var}_P F+
 \operatorname{Var}_Q F+\frac{d^2}{2}\right\}.
\]
Rearrange.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Covariance coercivity for arbitrary row maps]
\label{lem:ng-covariance-coercivity}
Let $A:\ell^2(J)\to\mathbb R^n$ be a bounded row map, where $J$ is finite
or countable, with $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq b$.  Define $AX$ by the $L^2$
limits of its coordinate series, put $\Sigma=AA^{\mathsf T}$, and set
$h=h(f^{\otimes n},\operatorname{Law}(AX))$.  There is a constant
$C_{f,b}<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}
 (1-h^2)\|\Sigma-I_n\|_F^2\leq C_{f,b}h^2.
 \label{eq:ng-covariance-coercivity}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a symmetric matrix $B$ and independent standardized coordinates,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X^{\mathsf T}BX)
 =2\|B\|_F^2+(\E X_1^4-3)\sum_j B_{jj}^2
 \leq c_f\|B\|_F^2,
 \qquad c_f=2+|\E X_1^4-3|.
\]
The same bound holds for a self-adjoint Hilbert--Schmidt matrix on a
countable source, with the quadratic form centered and defined in $L^2$;
this follows by truncation.  Apply this to the statistic
$F(y)=y^{\mathsf T}Dy$, where $D=\Sigma-I_n$.  Its expectation difference
under the two laws is $\|D\|_F^2$, while its two variances are bounded by
$c_f\|D\|_F^2$ and $c_fb^4\|D\|_F^2$, respectively.  The latter bound uses
$\|A^{\mathsf T}DA\|_{\mathcal S_2}\leq b^2\|D\|_F$.
Apply \cref{lem:ng-hellinger-detector} and divide by $\|D\|_F^2$ when this
quantity is nonzero.
\end{proof}

The first non-Gaussian moment gives a finite-degree detector for rotations.
Let $r\geq4$ be the least degree at which the moments of $f$ differ from
those of the standard Gaussian.  Such an $r$ exists because the Gaussian
moment sequence determines its probability law; evenness makes $r$ even.
Write $H_k$ for the probabilists' Hermite polynomial, defined by
\[
 e^{tx-t^2/2}=\sum_{k\geq0}H_k(x)\frac{t^k}{k!},
 \qquad \gamma_r:=\E H_r(X_1)\ne0.
\]
Thus $\E H_k(X_1)=0$ for $1\leq k<r$, and $\gamma_r$ is also the first
nonzero non-Gaussian cumulant.  Only the moments through order $2r$ enter
our estimates.

For a symmetric tensor $B\in(\mathbb R^n)^{\odot r}$ define its Gaussian
Wick polynomial by
\begin{equation}
 W_B(x):=\sum_{|\alpha|=r}\frac{r!}{\alpha!}B_\alpha
                  \prod_{i:\alpha_i>0}H_{\alpha_i}(x_i),
 \qquad
 \|B\|^2=\sum_{|\alpha|=r}\frac{r!}{\alpha!}|B_\alpha|^2.
 \label{eq:ng-wick-polynomial}
\end{equation}
Here $B_\alpha$ is the common tensor entry with multiplicity pattern
$\alpha$, and the tensor norm is the ordinary Hilbert tensor norm.

\begin{lemma}[A dimension-free Wick variance bound]
\label{lem:ng-wick-variance}
There is $C_{f,r}<\infty$ such that, for every finite-dimensional symmetric
$r$-tensor $B$,
\begin{equation}
 \E W_B(X)=\gamma_r\sum_iB_{i\cdots i},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}W_B(X)\leq C_{f,r}\|B\|^2.
 \label{eq:ng-wick-variance}
\end{equation}
If $A:\ell^2(J)\to\mathbb R^n$ is a coisometry
($AA^{\mathsf T}=I_n$), the same variance bound holds for $W_B(AX)$.
Its expectation is
\begin{equation}
 \E W_B(AX)
 =\gamma_r\langle B,C_A\rangle,
 \qquad
 C_A:=\sum_{j\in J}a_j^{\otimes r},\quad a_j=Ae_j.
 \label{eq:ng-wick-coisometry}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Group the terms in \eqref{eq:ng-wick-polynomial} by their coordinate support.
For a support containing at least two indices, each positive degree is
strictly less than $r$, so the corresponding Hermite factor has mean zero.
For a singleton support replace $H_r$ by $H_r-\gamma_r$ when centering.
Terms with different supports are therefore orthogonal in $L^2(f^{\otimes n})$.
For a fixed support there are at most $2^{r-1}$ positive compositions of
$r$.  Cauchy--Schwarz within each such group, the finite moments through
order $2r$, and $r!/\alpha!\leq r!$ bound its variance by a constant times
its contribution to $\|B\|^2$.  Summing proves
\eqref{eq:ng-wick-variance}, including the expectation formula.

The generating function for the Wick polynomial gives the tensor identity
\[
 W_B(AX)=W_{(A^{\mathsf T})^{\otimes r}B}(X)
 \quad\text{when }AA^{\mathsf T}=I_n.
\]
Since $A^{\mathsf T}$ is an isometry, the pulled-back tensor has norm
$\|B\|$.  This proves the assertion for a finite source.  For a countable
source, expand first in finitely many source coordinates and pass to the
limit in the centered Wick expansion.  The already proved variance bound
makes this expansion continuous in Hilbert tensor norm.  Equivalently,
one may truncate the coordinate series for $AX$, using
\[
 \E\left|\sum_j a_jX_j\right|^{2r}
     \leq C_{f,r}\left(\sum_j|a_j|^2\right)^r
 \quad\text{for finitely supported }a.
\]
To prove this bound, expand the even power: independence and evenness
remove every term with an odd multiplicity, and the remaining multiplicity
patterns are bounded by a constant depending only on the moments through
$2r$ times the expansion of $(\sum_j|a_j|^2)^r$.
Applying the bound to differences of truncations proves convergence in
$L^{2r}$.  In this truncation the Gaussian
covariance correction also converges to $I_n$.  The expectation series is
absolutely convergent: each tensor coefficient is a finite linear
combination of sums $\sum_j\prod_{k=1}^r A_{i_kj}$, which converge by
H\"older and the $\ell^2$ row bounds.  This proves
\eqref{eq:ng-wick-coisometry} for a countable source as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dimension-free moment detectors]
\label{lem:ng-dimension-free-detectors}
For every bounded row map $A:\ell^2(J)\to\mathbb R^n$, the covariance
estimate \eqref{eq:ng-covariance-coercivity} holds.  If additionally
$AA^{\mathsf T}=I_n$, put
\[
 D_n:=\sum_{i=1}^n e_i^{\otimes r},\qquad
 \Delta_A:=D_n-C_A,\qquad
 h=h(f^{\otimes n},\operatorname{Law}(AX)).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 (1-h^2)\|\Delta_A\|^2\leq C_{f,r}h^2.
 \label{eq:ng-wick-coercivity}
\end{equation}
In particular, near-maximal affinity forces the entire cumulant defect to
zero in Hilbert tensor norm, uniformly in both dimensions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The covariance assertion is \cref{lem:ng-covariance-coercivity}.  For the
second assertion, use $F=W_{\Delta_A}$ in \cref{lem:ng-hellinger-detector}.  Its expectation
difference is $\gamma_r\|\Delta_A\|^2$, and
\cref{lem:ng-wick-variance} bounds each variance by
$C_{f,r}\|\Delta_A\|^2$.  Divide by the latter squared tensor norm if
nonzero, absorbing $\gamma_r^{-2}$ into the constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite nonlinear coercivity modulo signed permutations]
\label{prop:ng-finite-hellinger-coercivity}
Let $\mathfrak P_n$ be the signed permutation matrices.  Under \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}, fix $0<a\leq b<\infty$.  There are
$c,C>0$, depending only on $f,a,b$, such that for every $n$ and every
$T\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb R)$ with singular values in $[a,b]$,
\begin{equation}
 c\min\{d_n(T)^2,1\}
 \leq h(f^{\otimes n},T_\#f^{\otimes n})^2
 \leq C\min\{d_n(T)^2,1\},
 \qquad d_n(T):=\min_{Q\in\mathfrak P_n}\|T-Q\|_F.
 \label{eq:ng-finite-nonlinear-coercivity}
\end{equation}
These are nonlinear estimates uniform in dimension; they do not assert an
additive formula for marginal Hellinger increments.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First let $T=O$ be orthogonal.  Orthogonality of the tensors
$(Oe_j)^{\otimes r}$ gives
\[
 \|\Delta_O\|^2
 =2\left(n-\sum_{i,j}|O_{ij}|^r\right).
\]
The matrix $p_{ij}=O_{ij}^2$ is doubly stochastic.  Its decomposition into
permutation matrices implies that some permutation $\pi$ satisfies
\[
 \sum_i p_{i,\pi(i)}\geq\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}^2
                       \geq\sum_{i,j}|O_{ij}|^r.
\]
Choosing the signs of its permutation matrix to agree with $O$ therefore
shows
\begin{equation}
 d_n(O)^2\leq2\left(n-\sum_i|O_{i,\pi(i)}|\right)
             \leq\|\Delta_O\|^2.
 \label{eq:ng-finite-rounding}
\end{equation}
Consequently \cref{lem:ng-dimension-free-detectors} gives
$d_n(O)\leq C_f h(f^{\otimes n},O_\#f^{\otimes n})$ whenever the latter
Hellinger distance is at most $1/\sqrt2$.

For general $T$ write its left polar decomposition as $T=BO$, with
$B=(TT^{\mathsf T})^{1/2}$.  Put
$h_T=h(f^{\otimes n},T_\#f^{\otimes n})$.  If $h_T\leq1/\sqrt2$,
\cref{lem:ng-covariance-coercivity} gives
\[
 \|B-I\|_F\leq\frac{\|TT^{\mathsf T}-I\|_F}{a+1}
                  \leq C_{f,a,b}h_T.
\]
Hellinger invariance under $O^{\mathsf T}$, the elementary inequality
$h(P,Q)^2\leq D(Q\Vert P)$, and \eqref{eq:ng-finite-entropy} imply
\[
 h(T_\#f^{\otimes n},O_\#f^{\otimes n})
 =h((O^{\mathsf T}BO)_\#f^{\otimes n},f^{\otimes n})
 \leq C_{f,a}\|B-I\|_F.
\]
The triangle inequality now bounds the Hellinger distance of $O$ from the
reference law by $C_{f,a,b}h_T$.  For sufficiently small $h_T$, the
orthogonal case applies, and
\[
 d_n(T)\leq\|T-O\|_F+d_n(O)\leq C_{f,a,b}h_T.
\]
If $h_T$ is not this small, the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:ng-finite-nonlinear-coercivity} follows by decreasing its
constant, since $\min\{d_n(T)^2,1\}\leq1$.

Finally choose a minimizing signed permutation $Q$.  Its action preserves
$f^{\otimes n}$, so the entropy bound applied to $TQ^{\mathsf T}$ yields
$h_T^2\leq C_{f,a}\|TQ^{\mathsf T}-I\|_F^2=C_{f,a}d_n(T)^2$.
Combine this with $h_T^2\leq2$ to obtain the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Tail rigidity and the endpoint theorem}
\label{sec:ng-tail-rigidity}

\begin{lemma}[Equivalent laws have asymptotically identical tails]
\label{lem:ng-reverse-tail}
If \(\nu\sim\mu\), then
\begin{equation}
  \inf_{F\Subset\{N+1,N+2,\ldots\}}
  \mathsf A\bigl((P_F)_\#\mu,(P_F)_\#\nu\bigr)
  \longrightarrow1 \qquad(N\to\infty).
  \label{eq:ng-tail-affinity}
\end{equation}
Here \(P_F\) denotes projection onto an arbitrary finite coordinate set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(p=d\nu/d\mu\) and
\(\mathcal G_N=\sigma(x_i:i>N)\).  The density of the restriction of
\(\nu\) to \(\mathcal G_N\), relative to that of \(\mu\), is
\(p_N=\E_\mu[p\mid\mathcal G_N]\).  The reverse martingale theorem and
triviality of the product tail give \(p_N\to1\) in \(L^1(\mu)\).  Hence
\[
  \E_\mu(\sqrt{p_N}-1)^2
  \leq\E_\mu|p_N-1|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hellinger distance decreases under measurable projection, uniformly for
all finite \(F\subset\{N+1,N+2,\ldots\}\), proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Covariance rigidity]
\label{lem:ng-covariance-rigidity}
If \(T_\#\mu\sim\mu\), then \(TT^*-I\in\mathcal S_2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(C=TT^*-I\).  By \cref{lem:ng-reverse-tail}, all finite-coordinate
affinities sufficiently far out in the tail are at least \(3/4\), so
the squared Hellinger distances in
\cref{lem:ng-dimension-free-detectors} are at most \(1/2\).  The dimension-free estimate
\eqref{eq:ng-covariance-coercivity} consequently bounds
\(\|P_FCP_F\|_F\), uniformly over every finite set \(F\) in that tail.
Increasing \(F\) to the whole tail and summing squared matrix entries shows
that \(P_{>N}CP_{>N}\) is Hilbert--Schmidt.  The remaining rows and columns
form a bounded finite-rank operator, so \(C\) is Hilbert--Schmidt as well.
\end{proof}

Retain the first non-Gaussian order \(r\) and coefficient
\(\gamma_r\) from \cref{lem:ng-wick-variance}.  For an orthogonal operator \(U=(u_{ij})\), define its
order-\(r\) defect array by
\begin{equation}
  D_{i_1\ldots i_r}(U)
  :=\mathbf1_{\{i_1=\cdots=i_r\}}
    -\sum_j u_{i_1j}\cdots u_{i_rj}.
  \label{eq:ng-ica-defect}
\end{equation}
The series converges absolutely by H\"older's inequality and the
\(\ell^2\) bounds on the rows.  The next argument explicitly controls the
indices omitted by a coordinate tail projection.

\begin{lemma}[Tensor rigidity and a single signed permutation]
\label{lem:ng-tail-ica}
If \(U\) is orthogonal and \(U_\#\mu\sim\mu\), then
\begin{equation}
  \sum_i\left(1-\sum_j|u_{ij}|^r\right)<\infty,
  \qquad
  U-Q\in\mathcal S_2
  \quad\hbox{for some signed permutation }Q.
  \label{eq:ng-tail-ica-conclusion}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:ng-reverse-tail} and the dimension-free estimate
\eqref{eq:ng-wick-coercivity} to every finite coordinate set in a sufficiently
far tail.  The squared norms of the corresponding restrictions of
\(D(U)\) are uniformly bounded.  Monotone convergence of the sums of
squared entries therefore shows that the restriction of \(D(U)\) to
\(\{N+1,N+2,\ldots\}^r\) is square summable.

Write \(u_j=Ue_j\).  For each fixed first index \(i\), the tensor series
\[
  \sum_j u_{ij}u_j^{\otimes(r-1)}
\]
converges in the Hilbert tensor product: the tensors
\(u_j^{\otimes(r-1)}\) are orthonormal and \(\sum_j u_{ij}^2=1\).
The squared norm of this slice of the defect is exactly
\begin{equation}
  \left\|e_i^{\otimes(r-1)}
        -\sum_j u_{ij}u_j^{\otimes(r-1)}\right\|^2
  =2\left(1-\sum_j|u_{ij}|^r\right)\leq2.
  \label{eq:ng-defect-slice}
\end{equation}
By symmetry, the part of the array with at least one index at most \(N\)
has squared norm at most \(2rN\).  Adding these finitely many strips proves
that the full array is square summable.  Summing
\eqref{eq:ng-defect-slice} gives the first assertion.

Put \(d_i=1-\sum_j|u_{ij}|^r\) and \(m_i=\max_j|u_{ij}|\).
Since \(r\geq4\),
\[
  \sum_j|u_{ij}|^r\leq m_i^{r-2}\leq m_i^2,
  \qquad 1-m_i^2\leq d_i.
\]
Outside a finite initial set of rows, \(d_i<1/2\).  Each such row has a
unique entry of squared modulus greater than \(1/2\), in a column
\(\pi(i)\).  No two of these rows choose the same column, because every
column has squared norm one.  With
\(\varepsilon_i=\operatorname{sgn}(u_{i,\pi(i)})\),
\[
  \sum_{i>N}\left\|U^*e_i-\varepsilon_i e_{\pi(i)}\right\|^2
  =2\sum_{i>N}(1-m_i)\leq2\sum_{i>N}d_i<\infty.
\]
Define \(Q_0\) by these signed rows on \(i>N\) and by zero rows on
\(i\leq N\).  Then \(U-Q_0\) is Hilbert--Schmidt.  A compact perturbation
of an invertible operator is Fredholm of index zero, so \(Q_0\) has
index zero.  Its cokernel is precisely the first \(N\) output coordinates;
its kernel is the span of the unused input coordinates.  Consequently
there are exactly \(N\) unused columns.  Match them bijectively to the
first \(N\) rows and choose any signs there.  This completes \(Q_0\) to a
single signed permutation \(Q\), with \(U-Q\in\mathcal S_2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact linear stabilizer]
\label{prop:ng-iid-stabilizer}
The bounded invertible linear stabilizer of \(\mu\) is exactly the group
of signed coordinate permutations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Evenness and identical distribution make every signed permutation a
symmetry.  Conversely, if \(T_\#\mu=\mu\), covariance gives \(TT^*=I\),
so \(T\) is orthogonal.  The moment assumptions below order \(r\) imply,
for each row of this orthogonal operator,
\[
  \E\left(\sum_j t_{ij}X_j\right)^r
  =(r-1)!!+\gamma_r\sum_j|t_{ij}|^r.
\]
The same moment under \(\mu\) is \((r-1)!!+\gamma_r\), so
\(\sum_j|t_{ij}|^r=1\).  A unit \(\ell^2\) vector has this property for
\(r>2\) only when exactly one coordinate has modulus one.  Orthogonality
then makes the selected coordinates a bijection.  Thus \(T\) is a signed
permutation.  The moment identity for infinite rows follows by truncation
in \(L^r\); the finite \(r\)-th moment and the standard moment bound for
sums of independent centered variables justify that convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:ng-iid-endpoint}]
Suppose first that \(T_\#\mu\sim\mu\).  By
\cref{lem:ng-covariance-rigidity}, \(C=TT^*-I\) is Hilbert--Schmidt.
The positive invertible operator \(S=(TT^*)^{1/2}\) satisfies
\[
  S-I=(TT^*-I)(S+I)^{-1}\in\mathcal S_2.
\]
The Hilbert--Schmidt sufficiency theorem
\cref{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency} applies to \(S\) and \(S^{-1}\).
Thus, for the orthogonal operator \(U=S^{-1}T\), measurable composition
and preservation of absolute continuity under pushforward give
\[
  U_\#\mu=(S^{-1})_\#(T_\#\mu)
  \sim(S^{-1})_\#\mu\sim\mu.
\]
By \cref{lem:ng-tail-ica}, \(U-Q\in\mathcal S_2\) for a signed
permutation \(Q\).  Therefore
\[
  T-Q=(S-I)U+(U-Q)\in\mathcal S_2.
\]

Conversely, if \(T-Q\in\mathcal S_2\) for a signed permutation \(Q\),
then \(TQ^{-1}=I+(T-Q)Q^{-1}\) is boundedly invertible and differs from
the identity by a Hilbert--Schmidt operator.  Since \(Q_\#\mu=\mu\),
\cref{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency} gives \(T_\#\mu\sim\mu\).
If no such \(Q\) exists, equivalence is impossible, and
\cref{lem:ng-orbit-dichotomy} gives mutual singularity.  Also,
\cref{prop:ng-iid-stabilizer} identifies the quotient symmetry group
appearing in the criterion.  The energy equivalence and its divergent
alternative follow from \cref{lem:ng-projected-likelihoods}.  Applying
\cref{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency} to \(TQ^{-1}\) identifies the
principal-truncation likelihood limit with \(d\nu_T/d\mu\), since
\(\nu_{TQ^{-1}}=\nu_T\).  Finally,
\eqref{eq:ng-forward-likelihood-ui}--\eqref{eq:ng-reverse-likelihood-ui}
give both uniform-integrability assertions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Moment assumptions and scope}
\label{sec:ng-moment-scope}

Only moments through order \(2r\) are used by the detector argument.
Accordingly, the all-moments assumption can be replaced by the existence
of an even \(r\geq4\) such that the moments below \(r\) agree with the
standard Gaussian moments, the moment of order \(r\) differs, and
\(\E|X|^{2r}<\infty\).

The bounded second derivative in \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions} supplies
the dimension-free entropy upper bound; the first non-Gaussian moment
supplies the nonlinear lower bound.  These play different roles and are
both verified above.  The result covers every bounded invertible
cross-coordinate operator for this density class.  It makes no
unrestricted claim for densities without the stated regularity or moment
conditions.  In the Gaussian case the moment detector vanishes at every
order, and the exact stabilizer enlarges to the orthogonal group, as in
\cref{sec:ng-gaussian-quotient}.
