\section{An i.i.d. non-Gaussian equivalence criterion}
\label{sec:ng-linear-models}

We characterize the bounded invertible linear transformations that
preserve the measure class of a non-Gaussian i.i.d. product.  The
one-dimensional hypotheses are a centered second moment and finite
location and scale Fisher information.  No symmetry or moment of order
greater than two is assumed.
The proof combines a logarithm-free Fourier characterization of Gaussian
laws, dimension-independent Hellinger estimates, and a tail-symmetry
argument.  The transformed coordinates need not be independent.

\subsection{The density class and the endpoint theorem}
\label{sec:ng-iid-endpoint}

Our standing assumptions are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 f>0\quad\text{Lebesgue-almost everywhere},\qquad
 g:=\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R),\qquad xg'\in L^2(\mathbb R),\\
 \E_fX=0,\qquad \E_fX^2=1,\qquad
 f\ne(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}
\end{equation}
All derivatives in these hypotheses are weak derivatives.  In
particular, a cusp in the density is allowed.  The two Sobolev
conditions control translations and dilations, respectively.  They do
not impose higher moments on the coordinate law.

Put $H=\ell^2(\mathbb N;\mathbb R)$ and
$\mu=f^{\otimes\mathbb N}$ on the product Borel space
$\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$.  The space $H$ indexes coefficients; a sample
from $\mu$ is not asserted to belong to $H$.  Define
\[
 \Sigma_f:=\{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}:
                       \operatorname{Law}(\varepsilon X)=\operatorname{Law}(X)\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_f:=\{Q:Q\text{ is a coordinate permutation with signs in }\Sigma_f\}.
\]
Thus $\mathcal P_f$ consists of all signed permutations if $f$ is
even, and of ordinary permutations otherwise.  For
$T\in\mathrm{GL}(H)$, meaning bounded and boundedly invertible, write
$\nu_T=(\Phi_T)_\#\mu$, using the measurable row-series realization in
\cref{lem:ng-measurable-action}.

For two probability measures set
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf A(\lambda,\nu)
 :=\int\sqrt{\frac{d\lambda}{dm}\frac{d\nu}{dm}}\,dm,
 \qquad h(\lambda,\nu)^2:=2-2\mathsf A(\lambda,\nu),
 \label{eq:ng-affinity-convention}
\end{equation}
where $m$ dominates both measures.  Let $P_n$ select the first $n$
coordinates and define
\[
 E_n(T):=-2\log\mathsf A\bigl((P_n)_\#\mu,(P_n)_\#\nu_T\bigr).
\]
These are actual output marginals, rather than laws obtained from
principal matrix corners.

\begin{theorem}[Complete criterion for arbitrary coordinate mixing]
\label{thm:ng-iid-endpoint}
Under \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}, for every $T\in\mathrm{GL}(H)$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \nu_T\sim\mu
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists Q\in\mathcal P_f:\ TQ^{-1}-I\in\mathcal S_2(H)\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sup_nE_n(T)<\infty.
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:ng-global-candidate}
\end{equation}
The exact bounded invertible linear stabilizer is
$\operatorname{Stab}(\mu)=\mathcal P_f$.  If these conditions fail,
$\nu_T\perp\mu$ and $E_n(T)\uparrow\infty$.  On the equivalence
branch, the projected likelihoods and reciprocal likelihoods are
uniformly integrable in their respective reference measures.
For any such $Q$, the operators
$T_n=I+P_n(TQ^{-1}-I)P_n$ are eventually invertible and
\[
 \frac{d\nu_{T_n}}{d\mu}\longrightarrow\frac{d\nu_T}{d\mu}
       \quad\text{in }L^1(\mu),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{d\nu_{T_n}}{d\mu}}
       \longrightarrow\sqrt{\frac{d\nu_T}{d\mu}}
       \quad\text{in }L^2(\mu).
\]
\end{theorem}

For orthogonal transformations the scale condition $xg'\in L^2$ can
also be removed; see \cref{thm:ng-orthogonal-endpoint}.  The distinction
between this result and the block-product criterion is that arbitrary
linear mixing generally destroys the product structure.  Necessity
below is proved through tail symmetries of the reference product law,
without tensorizing the affinity of a mixed output law.

\subsection{The rotational Fourier defect and a global ODE}
\label{sec:ng-fourier-defect}

This subsection requires only $\E X=0$ and $\E|X|<\infty$; neither a
density nor a second moment is needed.  Write
$\varphi(u)=\E e^{iuX}$, and for independent copies $X_1,X_2$ put
\[
 O_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
                         \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 W_{\xi,\eta}(x,y)
   =(e^{i\xi x}-\varphi(\xi))(e^{i\eta y}-\varphi(\eta)).
\]
This fixes the sign convention for rotation.  The feature is centered
under the product law and satisfies $|W_{\xi,\eta}|\le4$, so its
variance under any probability law is at most $16$.

\begin{lemma}[Logarithm-free characterization by rotational Fourier defect]
\label{lem:ng-fourier-ode}
Let
$G_{\xi,\eta}(\theta)=\E W_{\xi,\eta}(O_\theta(X_1,X_2))$.
Then $G_{\xi,\eta}(0)=0$ and
\begin{equation}
 R_f(\xi,\eta):=G'_{\xi,\eta}(0)
   =\eta\varphi'(\xi)\varphi(\eta)
          -\xi\varphi(\xi)\varphi'(\eta).
 \label{eq:ng-rotational-fourier-defect}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 R_f\equiv0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Law}(X)\text{ is a centered Gaussian, possibly degenerate}.
 \label{eq:ng-fourier-gaussian-characterization}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first moment implies $\varphi\in C^1(\mathbb R)$.  The joint
characteristic function after rotation is
\[
 \varphi(\xi\cos\theta+\eta\sin\theta)
 \varphi(-\xi\sin\theta+\eta\cos\theta).
\]
The marginal characteristic functions are
$\varphi(\xi\cos\theta)\varphi(-\xi\sin\theta)$ and
$\varphi(\eta\sin\theta)\varphi(\eta\cos\theta)$.
Their derivatives at zero vanish because $\varphi'(0)=i\E X=0$.
Differentiating the joint characteristic function therefore gives
\eqref{eq:ng-rotational-fourier-defect}.

If $R_f$ vanishes everywhere, continuity and $\varphi(0)=1$ allow us
to choose $\eta_0\ne0$ with $\varphi(\eta_0)\ne0$.  Fix this
single frequency.  The defect equation becomes the global ODE
\[
 \varphi'(\xi)=c\xi\varphi(\xi),\qquad
 c=\frac{\varphi'(\eta_0)}{\eta_0\varphi(\eta_0)},\qquad
 \varphi(0)=1.
\]
No division by $\varphi(\xi)$ has occurred.  Uniqueness for this
linear ODE gives $\varphi(\xi)=\exp(c\xi^2/2)$ on all of
$\mathbb R$, including any putative zero of $\varphi$.
The identity $\varphi(-\xi)=\overline{\varphi(\xi)}$ forces
$c\in\mathbb R$, and $|\varphi(\xi)|\le1$ forces $c\le0$.
Writing $c=-\sigma^2$ proves the Gaussian conclusion; $\sigma=0$
gives the point mass at zero.  Substitution proves the converse.
\end{proof}

For a non-Gaussian law, some two distinct frequencies have nonzero
defect.  By continuity a compact neighborhood of this pair already
detects the law.  Thus a compact frequency set may be fixed after the
coordinate law is chosen, independently of the ambient dimension.
The diagonal $\xi=\eta$ always has zero defect and cannot serve this
purpose.  A global scalar energy is, for example,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_w(f)=\iint|R_f(\xi,\eta)|^2w(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi\,d\eta,
 \qquad
 w>0\ \text{a.e.},\quad
 \iint(1+\xi^2+\eta^2)w(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi\,d\eta<\infty.
 \label{eq:ng-fourier-defect-energy}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $|R_f(\xi,\eta)|\le\E|X|(|\xi|+|\eta|)$, so this
energy is finite and is zero exactly for centered Gaussian laws.
Integrability of $w$ alone need not make the energy finite.

The same feature gives a local Hellinger lower bound for one rotation.
Choose a phase $\alpha$ with
$\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\alpha}R_f(\xi,\eta))>0$ and apply
\[
 |\E_\lambda F-\E_\nu F|
 \le h(\lambda,\nu)
             \{2\E_\lambda F^2+2\E_\nu F^2\}^{1/2}
\]
to $F=\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\alpha}W_{\xi,\eta})$.
It follows that
$h((O_\theta)_\#\operatorname{Law}(X)^{\otimes2},
\operatorname{Law}(X)^{\otimes2})\ge c|\theta|$ for small
$|\theta|$.  A first moment suffices for this lower bound.  The
dimension-independent matrix estimates below require the additional
second-moment and Sobolev hypotheses.

\paragraph{Centering, scaling, and phase.}
For an uncentered variable $Y$ with mean $m$, use $R_Y$ for the raw
expression on the right of \eqref{eq:ng-rotational-fourier-defect}.
Differentiating the centered feature now also differentiates its
marginal terms, and the actual dependence response is
\[
 D_Y(a,b)=R_Y(a,b)+im(a-b)\varphi_Y(a)\varphi_Y(b).
\]
If $X=(Y-m)/\sigma$, $\sigma>0$, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 R_X(\xi,\eta)
 =e^{-i(m/\sigma)(\xi+\eta)}
       D_Y(\xi/\sigma,\eta/\sigma).
 \label{eq:ng-centering-phase}
\end{equation}
Thus centering rotates the mean-corrected response by
$-(m/\sigma)(\xi+\eta)$; scaling also changes the frequencies.
At fixed corresponding frequencies its modulus is unchanged.  The
raw expression $R_Y$ alone does not transform by this phase factor.
For a centered symmetric law the defect is real, but at a particular
frequency pair an asymmetric law can also have a real or zero defect.
Consequently its phase is not, by itself, a complete measure of skewness.

For the concrete comparison $Y\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(2,1)$,
$m=2$, $\sigma=\sqrt2$, and $(\xi,\eta)=(1,2)$, one obtains
\[
 D_Y(1/\sqrt2,\sqrt2)=\frac4{27},\qquad
 R_{(Y-2)/\sqrt2}(1,2)=\frac4{27}e^{-3i\sqrt2}.
\]
At this particular pair, the entire phase of the Gamma response is
therefore the centering phase, namely $-3\sqrt2$ modulo $2\pi$.
The variance-one Laplace response is $R_f(1,2)=-4/27$.
Their moduli agree at these frequencies, while their principal phases
are approximately $116.915^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, respectively.

\subsection{Measurable linear realizations}
\label{sec:ng-measurable-closure}

Throughout this subsection, only \(\E_fX=0\) and
\(\E_fX^2=1\) are needed.  Put \(\mu=f^{\otimes\mathbb N}\) on
\(X=\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}\), and \(\mu_n=f^{\otimes n}\).
The sample sequence is not an \(\ell^2\)-valued random variable.  We
therefore first specify how an operator on \(\ell^2\) acts on its law.

\begin{lemma}[Measurable realization on each countable operator orbit]
\label{lem:ng-measurable-action}
For every \(T\in\mathcal B(\ell^2)\), there is a Borel map
\(\Phi_T:X\to X\) whose coordinates are the \(L^2(\mu)\) row sums
\[
  (\Phi_Tx)_i=\sum_{j\geq1}T_{ij}x_j.
\]
These maps can be chosen so that the following statements hold.  If a
probability measure \(\lambda\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \int\left|\sum_j a_jx_j\right|^2\,d\lambda(x)
  \leq C_\lambda\|a\|_2^2
  \quad\text{for every finitely supported }a,
  \label{eq:ng-covariance-domination}
\end{equation}
then \((\Phi_T)_\#\lambda\) also satisfies this condition, with constant
at most \(C_\lambda\|T\|^2\), and
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_S\circ\Phi_T=\Phi_{ST}
  \quad\lambda\text{-almost surely}
  \qquad(S,T\in\mathcal B(\ell^2)).
  \label{eq:ng-measurable-composition}
\end{equation}
For each fixed countable subgroup \(\Gamma\leq\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\),
there is a \(\Gamma\)-invariant Borel set \(D_\Gamma\subset X\) of full
measure for every \(\lambda\) satisfying
\eqref{eq:ng-covariance-domination}, on which these restrictions form an
action by bimeasurable automorphisms.  In particular, this applies to
\(\mu\) and all its \(\Gamma\)-orbit laws, including singular orbit laws.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(a\in\ell^2\), the finite sums \(\sum_{j\leq n}a_jx_j\) have a
unique \(L^2(\lambda)\) limit \(L_a\), by
\eqref{eq:ng-covariance-domination}.  Choose
\[
  n_k(a)=\min\left\{n\geq k:
                \sum_{j>n}|a_j|^2\leq2^{-4k}\right\}.
\]
Define \(\widehat L_a(x)\) to be the limit of
\(\sum_{j\leq n_k(a)}a_jx_j\) when it exists, and zero otherwise.
The estimate
\[
  \left\|\sum_{j\leq n_k(a)}a_jx_j-L_a\right\|_{L^2(\lambda)}^2
  \leq C_\lambda2^{-4k}
\]
and Borel--Cantelli show that \(\widehat L_a=L_a\),
\(\lambda\)-almost surely.  This one definition therefore works for every
law satisfying \eqref{eq:ng-covariance-domination}; it does not depend on
knowing whether that law is equivalent to \(\mu\).  Set
\(\Phi_T(x)_i=\widehat L_{T^*e_i}(x)\).  The construction is Borel,
and the same is true jointly in the matrix coefficients of \(T\) and in
\(x\), since each tail norm and the integer choice \(n_k\) is Borel.

For finitely supported \(b\), the finite sum of output coordinates equals
\(L_{T^*b}\) almost surely.  This proves the asserted second-moment bound
for the pushforward.  For the \(i\)-th row of \(S\), finite output sums
satisfy
\[
  \sum_{j\leq n}S_{ij}\widehat L_{T^*e_j}
     =L_{T^*P_nS^*e_i}
     \longrightarrow L_{T^*S^*e_i}
     \quad\text{in }L^2(\lambda).
\]
The selected subsequence on the left also converges almost surely to
\(\Phi_S(\Phi_T(x))_i\), because the output law has the required
second-moment bound.  Uniqueness of the limit proves
\eqref{eq:ng-measurable-composition}.

For a countable \(\Gamma\), let \(B\) be the Borel set on which all its
row limits, identity relations, and pairwise composition relations hold.
The preceding argument gives \(\lambda(B)=1\) for every law under
consideration.  Put
\[
  D_\Gamma=\bigcap_{T\in\Gamma}\Phi_T^{-1}(B).
\]
Every orbit law satisfies the same second-moment condition, so
\(\lambda(D_\Gamma)=1\).  The composition identities imply that
\(D_\Gamma\) is invariant and that \(\Phi_{T^{-1}}\) is the inverse of
\(\Phi_T\) there.  This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

Write \(\nu_T=(\Phi_T)_\#\mu\).  The preceding lemma makes assertions
about a common carrier only for a fixed countable subgroup; no single
pointwise action of all of \(\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\) on a common conull
set is required.  The subsequences in its proof are also essential: a
covariance bound gives convergence of arbitrary row sums in \(L^2\), but
does not by itself give almost-sure convergence of all ordinary partial
sums under a correlated orbit law.  On the original independent product
law, the versions agree with the usual almost-sure row sums.

\subsection{The Fisher operator without a symmetry assumption}
\label{sec:ng-fisher-operator}

Let $f$ be a probability density and write $g=\sqrt f$.  In this
subsection we assume
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 f>0\quad\text{Lebesgue-almost everywhere},\qquad
 g\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R),\qquad xg'(x)\in L^2(\mathbb R),\\
 \mathbb E X=0,\qquad \mathbb E X^2=1,
 \qquad X\sim f(x)\,dx.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:ng-fisher-regularity}
\end{equation}
The derivatives in these assumptions are weak derivatives.  Define the
location score $s=-f'/f=-2g'/g$ almost everywhere, and put
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E s(X)=0,\qquad \mathbb E[Xs(X)]=1,\qquad
 J:=\mathbb E s(X)^2=4\|g'\|_2^2,
 \label{eq:ng-score-moments}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 d(x):=xs(x)-1,\qquad
 \kappa:=\mathbb E d(X)^2=4\|xg'\|_2^2-1.
 \label{eq:ng-kappa}
\end{equation}
The two integration-by-parts identities in
\eqref{eq:ng-score-moments} follow from these assumptions.  Indeed,
$f'=2gg'$ belongs to $L^1$, and $xf'$ belongs to $L^1$ by
Cauchy--Schwarz and the finite second moment.  Applying integration by
parts first to a compactly supported cutoff and then to
$x\chi(x/R)$ gives the identities as $R\to\infty$.

For $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\sim\mu_n:=f^{\otimes n}$ and
$A\in M_n(\mathbb R)$, the score of the pushforward by $I+tA$ at
$t=0$ is
\begin{equation}
 S_A(X)=\sum_{i,j=1}^n A_{ij}
       \bigl(s(X_i)X_j-\delta_{ij}\bigr)
       =\sum_i A_{ii}d(X_i)+\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}s(X_i)X_j.
 \label{eq:ng-matrix-score}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[The matrix Fisher operator]
\label{thm:ng-fisher-spectrum}
Under \eqref{eq:ng-fisher-regularity}, the quadratic form
$\mathcal I_f^{(n)}(A):=\mathbb E S_A^2$ has the decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathscr I_f^{(n)}
 =\kappa P_{\mathrm{diag}}
  +(J+1)P_{\mathrm{sym,off}}
  +(J-1)P_{\mathrm{skew}}.}
 \label{eq:ng-fisher-operator-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Here the projections are orthogonal for the Frobenius inner product.
Equivalently, if
$D=\operatorname{diag}A$,
$S_0=(A+A^{\mathsf T})/2-D$, and
$K=(A-A^{\mathsf T})/2$, then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_f^{(n)}(A)
 =\kappa\|D\|_F^2+(J+1)\|S_0\|_F^2+(J-1)\|K\|_F^2.
 \label{eq:ng-fisher-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The multiplicities are respectively
\begin{equation}
 n,\qquad n(n-1)/2,\qquad n(n-1)/2.
 \label{eq:ng-fisher-multiplicities}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\kappa>0$ and $J\ge1$, with $J=1$ precisely for the
standard Gaussian density.  Thus, for a non-Gaussian $f$,
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_f\|A\|_F^2\le\mathcal I_f^{(n)}(A)
 \le C_f\|A\|_F^2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_f:=\min\{\kappa,J-1\}>0,
 \quad C_f:=\max\{\kappa,J+1\}.
 \label{eq:ng-fisher-ellipticity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The diagonal variables $d(X_i)$ are independent and centered.  Their
covariances with every off-diagonal term $s(X_j)X_k$, $j\ne k$,
vanish: after any shared coordinate is integrated out, the remaining
factor has expectation $\mathbb E X$ or $\mathbb E s(X)$.
Two off-diagonal terms can have a nonzero covariance only when their
ordered index pairs coincide or are reversed.  Those covariances are,
respectively, $J$ and $(\mathbb E Xs(X))^2=1$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E S_A^2
 =\kappa\sum_i A_{ii}^2+
   \sum_{i<j}\{J(A_{ij}^2+A_{ji}^2)+2A_{ij}A_{ji}\}.
 \label{eq:ng-fisher-pair-form}
\end{equation}
Resolving each pair into its symmetric and skew-symmetric parts proves
the decomposition.  In particular, the calculation uses centering and
independence and imposes no parity condition on $f$.

Cauchy--Schwarz applied to $\mathbb E Xs(X)=1$ gives $J\ge1$.
Equality implies $s(X)=X$ almost surely, hence
$g'=-xg/2$ almost everywhere and $f$ is the standard Gaussian.
If $\kappa=0$, then $2xg'+g=0$ almost everywhere on each of the two
half-lines.  The absolutely continuous solutions there are constant
multiples of $|x|^{-1/2}$, which cannot give a positive probability
density.  This proves $\kappa>0$ and the asserted bounds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hellinger paths and Hilbert--Schmidt closure}
\label{sec:ng-hs-hellinger-closure}

For $A\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb R)$ define a unitary operator on
$L^2(\mathbb R^n,dx)$ by
\[
 (U_Av)(y)=|\det A|^{-1/2}v(A^{-1}y),
 \qquad
 \Omega_n(x)=\prod_{i=1}^n g(x_i).
\]
Thus $U_A\Omega_n$ is the square root of the Lebesgue density of
$A_\#\mu_n$, and
$h(A_\#\mu_n,B_\#\mu_n)=\|U_A\Omega_n-U_B\Omega_n\|_2$.
For a fixed real matrix $A$, the generator of $U_{\exp(tA)}$ is
\begin{equation}
 G_Av=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A)v-(Ax)\cdot\nabla v,
 \qquad G_A\Omega_n=\tfrac12S_A\Omega_n.
 \label{eq:ng-half-density-generator}
\end{equation}
These identities hold in $L^2$.  To justify them under
\eqref{eq:ng-fisher-regularity}, observe that
$x_j\partial_i\Omega_n\in L^2$ for all $i,j$: the diagonal case
uses $xg'\in L^2$, and the off-diagonal case uses $g'\in L^2$
and $\mathbb E X^2=1$.  Cutoff and mollification approximate
$\Omega_n$ in the corresponding weighted Sobolev graph norm, where
the usual change-of-variables differentiation is valid.

\begin{lemma}[A dimension-free Hellinger path bound]
\label{lem:ng-finite-hellinger-upper}
If $A_t$, $0\le t\le1$, is a continuously differentiable path in
$\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb R)$, then
\begin{equation}
 h((A_1)_\#\mu_n,(A_0)_\#\mu_n)
 \le\frac{\sqrt{C_f}}2
       \int_0^1\|A_t^{-1}\dot A_t\|_F\,dt.
 \label{eq:ng-finite-hellinger-path}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le r<1$, then
\begin{equation}
 h((I+B)_\#\mu_n,\mu_n)
 \le\frac{\sqrt{C_f}}{2(1-r)}\|B\|_F.
 \label{eq:ng-finite-hellinger-upper}
\end{equation}
All constants are independent of $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The representation law $U_AU_B=U_{AB}$ and
\eqref{eq:ng-half-density-generator} give
\[
 \frac d{dt}U_{A_t}\Omega_n
 =U_{A_t}G_{A_t^{-1}\dot A_t}\Omega_n,
 \qquad
 \left\|\frac d{dt}U_{A_t}\Omega_n\right\|_2
 =\frac12\sqrt{\mathcal I_f^{(n)}(A_t^{-1}\dot A_t)}.
\]
Integrate this identity and use the Fisher upper bound.  For the last
assertion take $A_t=I+tB$ and use
$\|A_t^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le(1-r)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hilbert--Schmidt sufficiency and RN closure]
\label{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency}
Assume \eqref{eq:ng-fisher-regularity}, let
$\mu=f^{\otimes\mathbb N}$, and let
$T=I+K\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)$ with $K\in\mathcal S_2$.
Then $\nu_T\sim\mu$.  For the eventually invertible operators
$T_n=I+P_nKP_n$, their likelihoods
$Z_n=d\nu_{T_n}/d\mu$ converge in $L^1(\mu)$ to the strictly
positive likelihood $Z_T=d\nu_T/d\mu$, and
$\sqrt{Z_n}\to\sqrt{Z_T}$ in $L^2(\mu)$.
The inverse approximants satisfy the same conclusion:
\[
 \frac{d\nu_{T_n^{-1}}}{d\mu}
 \longrightarrow\frac{d\nu_{T^{-1}}}{d\mu}
 \quad\text{in }L^1(\mu).
\]
On the common measurable carrier of the relevant countable operator
group, the likelihoods obey
\begin{align}
 Z_{ST}(y)&=Z_S(y)Z_T(\Phi_{S^{-1}}y),
   \label{eq:ng-rn-cocycle}\\
 Z_T(y)Z_{T^{-1}}(\Phi_{T^{-1}}y)&=1.
   \label{eq:ng-rn-inverse}
\end{align}
If $Q$ is an exact linear stabilizer of $\mu$, the same equivalence
holds whenever $TQ^{-1}-I\in\mathcal S_2$ and $T$ is invertible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $P_nKP_n\to K$ in Hilbert--Schmidt norm, $T_n\to T$ in
operator norm.  The approximants are therefore eventually invertible,
with uniformly bounded inverse norms.  If $A_n$ is the first
$n$-coordinate block of $T_n$, then
\begin{equation}
 Z_n(x)=|\det A_n|^{-1}
     \prod_{i=1}^n\frac{f((A_n^{-1}x_{1:n})_i)}{f(x_i)}.
 \label{eq:ng-finite-approximant-density}
\end{equation}
These densities are positive almost everywhere.  For all sufficiently
large $m,n$, put $B_{nm}=T_n^{-1}T_m-I$; it acts in a finite
coordinate block and has operator norm at most $1/2$.  Hellinger
invariance under the finite invertible map $T_n^{-1}$ and
\eqref{eq:ng-finite-hellinger-upper} give
\begin{equation}
 \|\sqrt{Z_m}-\sqrt{Z_n}\|_2
 \le\sqrt{C_f}\,\|T_n^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
                    \|T_m-T_n\|_{\mathcal S_2}.
 \label{eq:ng-approximant-hellinger-cauchy}
\end{equation}
Thus the square roots are Cauchy in $L^2(\mu)$, with a nonnegative
limit $r$ of norm one.  Cauchy--Schwarz also gives
\begin{equation}
 \|Z_m-Z_n\|_1\le2\|\sqrt{Z_m}-\sqrt{Z_n}\|_2,
 \qquad Z_n\longrightarrow r^2\quad\text{in }L^1(\mu).
 \label{eq:ng-approximant-l1-cauchy}
\end{equation}
For each fixed finite set of output coordinates, the row-series
realizations $\Phi_{T_n}X$ converge to $\Phi_TX$ in $L^2$.
Bounded continuous cylinder tests consequently identify $r^2\mu$
with $\nu_T$.

The inverse approximants converge in Hilbert--Schmidt norm because
\[
 \|T_n^{-1}-T^{-1}\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \le\|T_n^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
       \|T_n-T\|_{\mathcal S_2}\|T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Apply the same argument to these finite-block operators to obtain
$\nu_{T^{-1}}\ll\mu$.  The measurable row-series construction in
\cref{lem:ng-measurable-action} requires only the centered finite
second moment; on its countable-group carrier it gives
\[
 \mu=(\Phi_T)_\#\nu_{T^{-1}}
       \ll(\Phi_T)_\#\mu=\nu_T.
\]
This proves equivalence and strict positivity of $r^2$.  Change of
variables on the same carrier proves the cocycle and inverse
identities.  Finally, $\nu_Q=\mu$ and
$T=(TQ^{-1})Q$ give the stabilizer assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite Fourier detectors and a uniform local lower bound}
\label{sec:ng-local-fourier-fisher}

We now assume that $f$ is non-Gaussian in addition to
\eqref{eq:ng-fisher-regularity}.  We use finite real Fourier sums:
finite real linear combinations of $1$, $\cos(\xi x)$ and
$\sin(\xi x)$, with real frequencies $\xi$.  Their frequencies may
depend on $f$; once chosen, their finite set is independent of the
matrix dimension.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded Fourier functions with exact dual moments]
\label{lem:ng-trig-duals}
There exist centered finite real Fourier sums $p,q,r$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
       &s&x\\ \hline
 p     &1&0\\
 q     &0&1
 \end{array}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathbb E[r(X)d(X)]=1.
 \label{eq:ng-trig-duality}
\end{equation}
The entries in the table denote expectations of the products.
For
\begin{equation}
 H_A(X):=\sum_i A_{ii}r(X_i)
              +\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}p(X_i)q(X_j),
 \label{eq:ng-matched-fourier-detector}
\end{equation}
one has, for all real matrices $A,B$ of the same size,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[H_AS_B]=\langle A,B\rangle_F,
 \qquad \|H_A\|_{L^2(\mu_n)}\le C_0\|A\|_F,
 \label{eq:ng-matched-fourier-pairing}
\end{equation}
where $C_0$ is independent of $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the real vector space of centered finite real Fourier sums
consider the two functionals
\[
 L_1(h)=\mathbb E h'(X)=\mathbb E[h(X)s(X)],
 \qquad L_2(h)=\mathbb E[Xh(X)].
\]
The integration-by-parts identity holds since $h,h'$ are bounded.
Both functionals are unchanged by subtracting a constant.  If this
moment map had rank at most one, all its two-by-two determinants
would vanish, including after complexification.  Writing
$\varphi(\xi)=\mathbb E e^{i\xi X}$ and testing with the centered
exponentials at frequencies $\xi,\eta$ would then give
\[
 \eta\varphi'(\xi)\varphi(\eta)
       -\xi\varphi(\xi)\varphi'(\eta)=0
 \qquad(\xi,\eta\in\mathbb R).
\]
Here $L_1(e^{i\xi x})=i\xi\varphi(\xi)$ and
$L_2(e^{i\xi x})=-i\varphi'(\xi)$.  The no-logarithm ODE
argument in \cref{lem:ng-fourier-ode} would force a centered
Gaussian law, a contradiction.  The map therefore has rank two.
Inverting the moment matrix of two real Fourier witnesses
supplies $p,q$ with the displayed dual
moments.  This construction fixes finitely many frequencies
depending only on $f$.

The diagonal function can be chosen using only the second moment.
For $\xi\ne0$ let
$r_0(x)=\cos(\xi x)-\mathbb E\cos(\xi X)$.  Dominated convergence
gives
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E[Xr_0'(X)]}{\xi^2}
 =-\mathbb E\left[X\frac{\sin(\xi X)}\xi\right]
 \longrightarrow-\mathbb E X^2=-1
 \qquad(\xi\to0).
\]
Choose a sufficiently small nonzero $\xi$ and set
$r=r_0/\mathbb E[Xr_0'(X)]$.  Cutoff integration by parts gives
$\mathbb E[r(X)d(X)]=\mathbb E[Xr'(X)]=1$.

All diagonal/off-diagonal cross terms in $\mathbb E H_AS_B$ vanish
by centering.  Coincident ordered off-diagonal pairs contribute
$\mathbb E[ps]\mathbb E[qX]=1$; reversed pairs contribute
$\mathbb E[pX]\mathbb E[qs]=0$.  This proves the pairing.
For any centered $a,b\in L^2(f)$, independence gives
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}a(X_i)b(X_j)\right\|_2
 \le\sqrt2\,\|a\|_2\|b\|_2\|A\|_F.
 \label{eq:ng-quadratic-chaos-bound}
\end{equation}
Together with the independent diagonal terms, this proves the last
assertion.
\end{proof}

The next lemma records the uniform estimate needed to use these
detectors along a matrix path.  Its proof gives the index contractions
explicitly; in particular, it uses no fourth moment of $X$ or higher
moment of the score.

\begin{lemma}[Generator bound for bounded coordinate detectors]
\label{lem:ng-trig-generator-bound}
Let $p,q,r$ be any fixed centered bounded continuously differentiable
functions with bounded derivatives.  Define $H_A$ by
\eqref{eq:ng-matched-fourier-detector}.  There is a constant $C_1$,
depending on these functions and on $J,\kappa$, such that
\begin{equation}
 \|G_B(H_A\Omega_n)\|_{L^2(dx)}
 \le C_1\|A\|_F\|B\|_F
 \qquad(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb R),\ n\ge1).
 \label{eq:ng-detector-generator-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $n$, both $H_A$ and its first derivatives are bounded.
Consequently
\[
 x_j\partial_i(H_A\Omega_n)
 =H_Ax_j\partial_i\Omega_n
       +x_j(\partial_iH_A)\Omega_n\in L^2(dx)
\]
by the weighted Sobolev regularity of $\Omega_n$ and
$\mathbb E X^2=1$.  The cutoff and mollification argument used for
\eqref{eq:ng-half-density-generator} therefore puts $H_A\Omega_n$
in the domain of $G_B$.
The product rule gives
\begin{equation}
 G_B(H_A\Omega_n)
 =\left\{\tfrac12H_AS_B-(Bx)\cdot\nabla H_A\right\}\Omega_n.
 \label{eq:ng-detector-generator-product}
\end{equation}
Both terms belong to $L^2$ in each finite dimension.  We prove a
dimension-independent bound for each term.

We will repeatedly use the following elementary consequence of
independence.  If $h_1,\ldots,h_m$ are centered $L^2(f)$ functions,
and a coefficient tensor $C$ is indexed by ordered tuples of
\emph{distinct} coordinates, then
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_m}^{\ne}
 C_{i_1\ldots i_m}\prod_{a=1}^m h_a(X_{i_a})\right\|_2
 \le\sqrt{m!}\,\|C\|_{\ell^2}
                      \prod_{a=1}^m\|h_a\|_2.
 \label{eq:ng-canonical-chaos-bound}
\end{equation}
In the square of this norm, a mixed term vanishes unless the two
tuples have the same index set.  There are at most $m!$ matching
permutations; Cauchy--Schwarz for the local moments and then for
the coefficient arrays proves the bound.  We use it only for
$m\le4$.

First consider $H_AS_B$.  For a vector $a$ and a zero-diagonal
matrix $M$, abbreviate
\[
 D_a(u)=\sum_i a_i u(X_i),\qquad
 Q_M(u,v)=\sum_{i\ne j}M_{ij}u(X_i)v(X_j).
\]
The product $H_AS_B$ is the sum of the four products obtained by
multiplying $D_{\mathbf a}(r)+Q_{A_0}(p,q)$
and $D_{\mathbf b}(d)+Q_{B_0}(s,x)$, where
$\mathbf a=(A_{ii})_i$, $\mathbf b=(B_{ii})_i$,
$A_0=A-\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf a)$, and
$B_0=B-\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf b)$.
In every common coordinate, a local product is a bounded function
times one of $d,s,x$ and hence is in $L^2(f)$.  Write each such
local product as its mean plus its centered part.

For a product $D_a(u)D_b(v)$, distinct indices give a coefficient
tensor bounded by $\|a\|_2\|b\|_2$; equal indices give the
Hadamard vector $(a_ib_i)_i$ and the scalar $\sum_i a_ib_i$.
Their norms have the same bound.  For $D_a(u)Q_M(v,w)$, there
are three index patterns: three distinct indices, equality with
the first index of $Q_M$, and equality with its second index.
The first gives the tensor $a\otimes M$.  In either equality
case, centering the local product gives a matrix obtained by
row or column multiplication of $M$ by $a$, and its mean gives
$M^{\mathsf T}a$ or $Ma$.  Each norm is at most
$\|a\|_2\|M\|_F$.  The product $Q_M(u,v)D_a(w)$ has exactly
the same three patterns and bounds.

For clarity, all patterns in a quadratic--quadratic product can
also be enumerated.  Consider
\[
 Q_M(u,v)Q_N(w,z)
 =\sum_{i\ne j,\,k\ne l}M_{ij}N_{kl}
                         u_i v_j w_k z_l,
\]
where $u,v$ are bounded and $w,z$ are in $L^2$.  Four distinct
indices give the restricted tensor $M\otimes N$, of norm at
most $\|M\|_F\|N\|_F$.  There are four patterns with exactly
one shared index: $i=k$, $i=l$, $j=k$, and $j=l$.
For example, the pattern $i=k$ is
\[
 \sum_{i,j,l}^{\ne}M_{ij}N_{il}(uw)_i v_jz_l.
\]
Its centered local product has coefficient norm bounded by
\[
 \left\{\sum_i\left(\sum_j M_{ij}^2\right)
                    \left(\sum_l N_{il}^2\right)\right\}^{1/2}
 \le\|M\|_F\|N\|_F.
\]
Its local mean has coefficient matrix
$(M^{\mathsf T}N)_{jl}$ off the diagonal; excluding $i=j,l$
does not change the sum because $M,N$ have zero diagonal.
The other three shared-index patterns give the same estimate
with a transpose or with the two coordinates exchanged, and
their mean contractions are matrix products of $M,N$ or their
transposes.

Finally, there are two patterns with both indices shared:
$(k,l)=(i,j)$ and $(k,l)=(j,i)$.  Their coefficients are
$M_{ij}N_{ij}$ and $M_{ij}N_{ji}$.  After centering the two
local products, the resulting coefficients are a Hadamard
matrix, its row sums, its column sums, and its total sum.
For instance
\[
 \sum_i\left|\sum_j M_{ij}N_{ij}\right|^2
 \le\sum_i\left(\sum_j M_{ij}^2\right)
            \left(\sum_j N_{ij}^2\right)
 \le\|M\|_F^2\|N\|_F^2,
\]
and the other three estimates follow from the same
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.  These cases exhaust the possible
index coincidences.  Applying
\eqref{eq:ng-canonical-chaos-bound} to the centered pieces
therefore proves
\begin{equation}
 \|H_AS_B\|_2\le C\|A\|_F\|B\|_F.
 \label{eq:ng-detector-score-product-bound}
\end{equation}

It remains to bound $(Bx)\cdot\nabla H_A$.  We give the
contractions separately, since derivatives of a centered
function need not be centered.  For the diagonal part the sum
is
\[
 \sum_{i,k}A_{ii}B_{ik}r'(X_i)X_k.
\]
When $i\ne k$, split $r'$ into its mean and centered part.
The latter has coefficient matrix
$\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf a)B$, with its diagonal
deleted.  The mean has coefficient vector
$B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf a-\mathbf a\mathbin{\odot}\mathbf b$.
Their norms are bounded by, respectively, one and two times
$\|A\|_F\|B\|_F$.  When $i=k$, center the local function
$xr'(x)$, which is in $L^2$ since $r'$ is bounded.  The
coefficient vector and the mean are bounded by the same
product of matrix norms.

Let $M=A_0$.  The derivative of the
off-diagonal part is the sum of
\[
 F(M,B;p',q):=
 \sum_{i\ne j,k}M_{ij}B_{ik}p'(X_i)q(X_j)X_k
\]
and $F(M^{\mathsf T},B;q',p)$.  In $F$, first take $k$ distinct
from $i,j$ and split $p'$ into its mean and centered part.
The centered part has three-index coefficient norm bounded by
\[
 \left\{\sum_i\left(\sum_jM_{ij}^2\right)
                    \left(\sum_kB_{ik}^2\right)\right\}^{1/2}
 \le\|M\|_F\|B\|_F.
\]
The mean part has, off the diagonal, the matrix
$M^{\mathsf T}B-M^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf b)$;
the second term removes $i=k$, and $i=j$ already contributes
zero.  Its Frobenius norm is at most $2\|M\|_F\|B\|_F$.
When $k=i$, center $xp'(x)$; the resulting matrix has entries
$B_{ii}M_{ij}$ and its mean has vector
$M^{\mathsf T}\mathbf b$.  When $k=j$, split both
$p'$ and $xq(x)$ into means and centered parts; the coefficients
are the Hadamard matrix $M\mathbin{\odot}B$, its row and column
sums, and its total sum.  The preceding Hadamard estimates
bound all of them.  All the local functions used here belong
to $L^2$, since $p',q$ are bounded and $\mathbb E X^2=1$.
The same proof applies to $F(M^{\mathsf T},B;q',p)$.
Using \eqref{eq:ng-canonical-chaos-bound} again proves
\[
 \|(Bx)\cdot\nabla H_A\|_2\le C\|A\|_F\|B\|_F.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:ng-detector-score-product-bound} and
\eqref{eq:ng-detector-generator-product}, this proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform local Hellinger coercivity]
\label{prop:ng-local-fisher-hellinger}
For every non-Gaussian density satisfying
\eqref{eq:ng-fisher-regularity}, there are constants
$c,C,\delta>0$, independent of $n$, such that
\begin{equation}
 c\|B\|_F\le h((I+B)_\#\mu_n,\mu_n)
             \le C\|B\|_F
 \qquad\text{whenever }\|B\|_F\le\delta.
 \label{eq:ng-local-matrix-hellinger}
\end{equation}
The lower bound can be obtained using the fixed finite set of
coordinate frequencies from \cref{lem:ng-trig-duals}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix the dual functions from \cref{lem:ng-trig-duals}.
First use exponential coordinates.  Unitarity and the
one-parameter group derivative give
\begin{align*}
 \langle H_A\Omega_n,(U_{\exp A}-I)\Omega_n\rangle
 &=\int_0^1
   \langle U_{\exp(-tA)}(H_A\Omega_n),G_A\Omega_n\rangle\,dt\\
 &=\tfrac12\|A\|_F^2+\mathcal R_A.
\end{align*}
Here the exact leading term is
$\tfrac12\mathbb E H_AS_A$.
For a vector in the domain of $G_A$, the fundamental theorem of
calculus gives
$\|(U_{\exp(-tA)}-I)v\|_2\le t\|G_Av\|_2$.
Thus \cref{lem:ng-trig-generator-bound} and the Fisher upper
bound imply
\[
 |\mathcal R_A|
 \le\frac{C_1\sqrt{C_f}}4\|A\|_F^3.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{eq:ng-matched-fourier-pairing} now
give, for all sufficiently small $\|A\|_F$,
\[
 h((\exp A)_\#\mu_n,\mu_n)
 \ge\frac{1}{4C_0}\|A\|_F.
\]
For $\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1/2$, the real power-series logarithm
$A=\log(I+B)$ exists and satisfies
\[
 \|A-B\|_F
 \le\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{k-1}}{k}\|B\|_F
 \le C\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|B\|_F.
\]
Its norm is therefore uniformly comparable to $\|B\|_F$ in a
fixed small chart.  This proves the lower bound for $I+B$;
the upper bound follows from
\cref{lem:ng-finite-hellinger-upper}.
\end{proof}

The preceding pairing is a half-density pairing: it is
$\int H_A\sqrt{d\mu_n\,d((\exp A)_\#\mu_n)}-
\int H_A\,d\mu_n$.  It provides a uniform local lower bound
without asserting a dimension-uniform variance bound for
$H_A$ under every arbitrarily mixed output law.  Such a bound,
or an independent tail argument, is a separate requirement
when passing to necessity for arbitrary output marginals.

\subsection{The orthogonal case requires only location Fisher information}
\label{sec:ng-orthogonal-location-fisher}

For orthogonal transformations the assumptions can be reduced to
\begin{equation}
 f>0\ \text{almost everywhere},\qquad
 g=\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R),\qquad
 \mathbb E X=0,\qquad\mathbb E X^2=1.
 \label{eq:ng-orthogonal-fisher-regularity}
\end{equation}
In particular, $xg'\in L^2$ is not required.  The cutoff
identities $\mathbb E s=0$ and $\mathbb E Xs=1$ still follow
from $J<\infty$ and the second moment.  For a skew-symmetric
matrix $A$, the score has only off-diagonal terms and
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E S_A^2=(J-1)\|A\|_F^2.
 \label{eq:ng-orthogonal-fisher}
\end{equation}
The generator formula remains valid: only
$x_j\partial_i\Omega_n$, $i\ne j$, occur.  One can justify
it by radial cutoff and radial mollification, which commute
with the orthogonal one-parameter group.

\begin{proposition}[Orthogonal local bounds and HS sufficiency]
\label{prop:ng-orthogonal-fisher-hs}
Under \eqref{eq:ng-orthogonal-fisher-regularity}, every
orthogonal $U$ on $\ell^2$ with $U-I\in\mathcal S_2$ satisfies
$\nu_U\sim\mu$.  Its likelihoods have the same $L^1$ and
square-root $L^2$ closure along finite-coordinate orthogonal
approximants, as do the inverse likelihoods.
If $f$ is non-Gaussian, there also exist dimension-independent
$c,C,\delta>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
 c\|Q-I\|_F\le h(Q_\#\mu_n,\mu_n)
                   \le C\|Q-I\|_F
 \quad(Q\in O(n),\ \|Q-I\|_F\le\delta).
 \label{eq:ng-orthogonal-local-hellinger}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a skew-symmetric $A$, integration along $\exp(tA)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 h((\exp A)_\#\mu_n,\mu_n)
 \le\tfrac12\sqrt{J-1}\,\|A\|_F.
 \label{eq:ng-orthogonal-path-upper}
\end{equation}
If $f$ is non-Gaussian, the construction of the two dual
functions $p,q$ in \cref{lem:ng-trig-duals} uses only $s,X\in L^2$
and $J-1>0$.  On skew-symmetric matrices use
$H_A=\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}p(X_i)q(X_j)$, with no diagonal
function.  The score $S_B$ likewise has no diagonal term.
Consequently the proof of
\cref{lem:ng-trig-generator-bound} contains only bounded
functions times $s$ or $X$, and proves the same estimate under
\eqref{eq:ng-orthogonal-fisher-regularity}.  The half-density
pairing argument gives the local lower bound in exponential
coordinates.  For an orthogonal $Q$ sufficiently close to $I$,
its power-series logarithm is real and skew-symmetric, with
$\|\log Q\|_F$ comparable to $\|Q-I\|_F$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:ng-orthogonal-local-hellinger}.

For completeness, finite-coordinate orthogonal approximants
can be constructed without applying a nonorthogonal path
estimate.  Set $T_n=I+P_n(U-I)P_n$ and, for large $n$, take its
polar factor
\[
 Q_n=T_n(T_n^{\mathsf T}T_n)^{-1/2}.
\]
It is orthogonal and acts identically outside the first $n$
coordinates.  Since $T_n\to U$ in Hilbert--Schmidt norm,
\[
 \|T_n^{\mathsf T}T_n-I\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \le(\|T_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}+1)
                         \|T_n-U\|_{\mathcal S_2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The smallest singular values of $T_n$ are bounded below by
some $m>0$.  Functional calculus for $|T_n|$ then gives
\[
 \|Q_n-T_n\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 =\|I-|T_n|\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \le\frac{\|T_n^{\mathsf T}T_n-I\|_{\mathcal S_2}}{1+m}.
\]
Thus $Q_n\to U$ in Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  For sufficiently
large $n,m$, $Q_n^{\mathsf T}Q_m$ is close to the identity
and has a small skew-symmetric logarithm.  Applying
\eqref{eq:ng-orthogonal-path-upper} to this logarithm proves
that the likelihood square roots are Cauchy.  Their $L^1$
limits are identified by the same cylinder tests as in
\cref{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency}.  Apply the argument also to
$Q_n^{-1}\to U^{-1}$; the measurable inverse action then
proves equivalence and the asserted closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The local bound for Hilbert--Schmidt operators]
\label{lem:ng-local-hs-coercivity}
Let $f$ be non-Gaussian and satisfy
\eqref{eq:ng-fisher-regularity}.  There are constants
$c,C,\delta>0$ such that every $K\in\mathcal S_2(\ell^2)$ with
$\|K\|_{\mathcal S_2}\le\delta$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 c\|K\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \le h(\nu_{I+K},\mu)
 \le C\|K\|_{\mathcal S_2}.
 \label{eq:ng-local-hs-hellinger}
\end{equation}
The constants can be chosen with $\delta<1$, so $I+K$ is
automatically invertible.  Under the weaker assumptions
\eqref{eq:ng-orthogonal-fisher-regularity}, the same conclusion
holds for orthogonal $I+K$ whenever $f$ is non-Gaussian.
This includes finite-rank perturbations whose ranges have
infinite coordinate support.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the general linear case, take $K_n=P_nKP_n$ and apply
\eqref{eq:ng-local-matrix-hellinger} to its finite coordinate
block.  Its untouched tail factors contribute affinity one,
so the finite-dimensional Hellinger distance is also
$h(\nu_{I+K_n},\mu)$.  The likelihood closure in
\cref{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency} gives
\[
 h(\nu_{I+K_n},\mu)
 =\left\|\sqrt{\frac{d\nu_{I+K_n}}{d\mu}}-1\right\|_2
 \longrightarrow
 \left\|\sqrt{\frac{d\nu_{I+K}}{d\mu}}-1\right\|_2
 =h(\nu_{I+K},\mu).
\]
Also $\|K_n\|_{\mathcal S_2}\to\|K\|_{\mathcal S_2}$.
Passing to the limit proves both bounds.

In the orthogonal case, use the finite-coordinate polar
approximants $Q_n$ and their likelihood closure from
\cref{prop:ng-orthogonal-fisher-hs}.  They satisfy
$Q_n\to I+K$ in Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  Choose the radius in
this assertion to be half the finite-dimensional radius in
\eqref{eq:ng-orthogonal-local-hellinger}.  Then all sufficiently
large $Q_n$ lie in that finite-dimensional chart, and
\[
 h(\nu_{Q_n},\mu)\longrightarrow h(\nu_{I+K},\mu),
 \qquad
 \|Q_n-I\|_{\mathcal S_2}\longrightarrow\|K\|_{\mathcal S_2}.
\]
Pass to the limit in \eqref{eq:ng-orthogonal-local-hellinger}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Orbit dichotomy and projected likelihoods}
\label{sec:ng-orbit-likelihoods}

\begin{lemma}[Dichotomy for every bounded invertible orbit]
\label{lem:ng-orbit-dichotomy}
For every \(T\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\),
\begin{equation}
  \nu_T\sim\mu\quad\text{or}\quad\nu_T\perp\mu.
  \label{eq:ng-orbit-dichotomy}
\end{equation}
This conclusion uses only positivity of \(f\), its centered finite second
moment, and \(\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R)\); in particular, it holds
under \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(g=\sqrt f\).  The translation estimate
\[
  1-\mathsf A(f,f(\cdot-a))
     =\tfrac12\|g(\cdot-a)-g\|_2^2
     \leq\tfrac12\|g'\|_2^2a^2
\]
and Kakutani's theorem imply
\((\tau_h)_\#\mu\sim\mu\) for every \(h\in\ell^2\).
The row realizations satisfy
\[
  \Phi_T(x+T^{-1}h)=\Phi_T(x)+h
  \quad\mu\text{-almost surely};
\]
this follows directly by adding the absolutely convergent scalar row
pairing with the \(\ell^2\) vector \(T^{-1}h\).
Consequently, \(\nu_T\) is also quasi-invariant under every
\(\ell^2\) translation.

Let \(H\) be the countable additive group generated by
\(\{qe_j,qTe_j:q\in\mathbb Q,\ j\geq1\}\).
Both \(\mu\) and \(\nu_T\) are quasi-invariant and ergodic under
translation by \(H\).  Indeed, an event invariant modulo \(\mu\) under
all rational finite-coordinate translations has, by positivity of the
finite-dimensional densities, almost-everywhere constant sections in each
finite coordinate block.  It is therefore a tail event modulo \(\mu\),
and has probability zero or one.  Here one uses the elementary fact that
a measurable function on \(\mathbb R^n\) invariant almost everywhere
under rational translations is constant almost everywhere, obtained, for
example, by convolution with smooth approximate identities.
For \(\nu_T\), pull an invariant event back by \(\Phi_T\); invariance
under \(qTe_j\) becomes invariance under \(qe_j\), giving the same
zero--one conclusion.

Write \(\nu_T=\nu_a+\nu_s\) for its Lebesgue decomposition relative to
\(\mu\).  Choose a Borel \(\mu\)-null set \(N_0\) supporting
\(\nu_s\), and let \(N=\bigcup_{h\in H}(N_0+h)\).
Quasi-invariance of \(\mu\) makes \(N\) a \(\mu\)-null set;
it is exactly \(H\)-invariant and still supports \(\nu_s\).
Thus \(\nu_T(N)=\nu_s(X)\), and ergodicity of \(\nu_T\) makes this
number zero or one.  The latter case gives singularity.  In the former
case, write \(\nu_T=Z\mu\).  Quasi-invariance of both measures implies
that \(\{Z>0\}\) is invariant modulo \(\mu\) under \(H\).
Its \(\mu\)-measure is positive because \(\int Z\,d\mu=1\), and
ergodicity of \(\mu\) makes that measure one.  This gives equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projected likelihoods and two-sided uniform integrability]
\label{lem:ng-projected-likelihoods}
For \(T\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)\), every finite output law
\(\nu_{T,N}=(P_N)_\#\nu_T\) is equivalent to \(\mu_N\).
Writing \(\mathcal F_N=\sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\), define
\begin{equation}
  R_N(x)=\frac{d\nu_{T,N}}{d\mu_N}(x_{1:N}),
  \qquad
  \mathsf A_N(T)=\mathbb E_\mu\sqrt{R_N},
  \qquad E_N(T)=-2\log\mathsf A_N(T).
  \label{eq:ng-projected-likelihoods}
\end{equation}
Then \((R_N)\) is a nonnegative mean-one \(\mu\)-martingale and
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf A_N(T)\downarrow\mathsf A(\mu,\nu_T),
  \qquad
  \nu_T\sim\mu
    \Longleftrightarrow\sup_NE_N(T)<\infty.
  \label{eq:ng-projected-affinity-endpoint}
\end{equation}
Otherwise \(E_N(T)\uparrow\infty\) and \(\nu_T\perp\mu\).
If \(\nu_T\sim\mu\) with density \(Z_T\), then
\begin{align}
  R_N&=\mathbb E_\mu[Z_T\mid\mathcal F_N]
       \longrightarrow Z_T
       &&\text{in }L^1(\mu),
       \label{eq:ng-forward-likelihood-ui}\\
  R_N^{-1}&=\mathbb E_{\nu_T}[Z_T^{-1}\mid\mathcal F_N]
       \longrightarrow Z_T^{-1}
       &&\text{in }L^1(\nu_T).
       \label{eq:ng-reverse-likelihood-ui}
\end{align}
In particular, both likelihood martingales are uniformly integrable in
their respective reference measures.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first \(N\) rows of \(T\) are linearly independent, since \(T^*\)
is injective.  Some \(N\) input columns therefore form an invertible
\(N\times N\) minor \(A\).  Split the input into these coordinates
and their complement.  The first \(N\) outputs have the form
\(AU+W\), where \(U\sim\mu_N\) and \(W\) is independent of \(U\).
Conditioning on \(W\) writes their density as a mixture of translates
of the strictly positive density of \(AU\).  This mixture is positive
and finite almost everywhere, proving the finite-dimensional equivalence.

Consistency of projected laws proves the martingale assertion.  The
martingale limit is
\(R_\infty=d(\nu_T)_a/d\mu\), the density of the absolutely continuous
part of \(\nu_T\).  To see the identification explicitly, Fatou's lemma
on cylinder sets gives \(R_\infty\mu\leq\nu_T\), first on the cylinder
algebra and then on the generated sigma-field.  Conversely, if
\((\nu_T)_a=Z\mu\), then
\(\mathbb E_\mu[Z\mid\mathcal F_N]\leq R_N\), so martingale
convergence gives \(Z\leq R_\infty\).  The two inequalities identify
the limit.
Since \(\mathbb E_\mu(\sqrt{R_N})^2=1\), the square roots are uniformly
integrable in \(L^1(\mu)\).  They thus converge in \(L^1\) to
\(\sqrt{R_\infty}\).  Conditional Jensen's inequality gives the
monotonicity of their expectations, and consequently
\[
  \lim_N\mathsf A_N(T)
      =\int\sqrt{R_\infty}\,d\mu
      =\mathsf A(\mu,\nu_T).
\]
The dichotomy in \cref{lem:ng-orbit-dichotomy} now proves
\eqref{eq:ng-projected-affinity-endpoint} and the singular alternative.

Under equivalence, the forward conditional-expectation identity is the
definition of a projected density.  The reverse identity follows because
for \(B\in\mathcal F_N\),
\[
  \int_B R_N^{-1}\,d\nu_T=\mu(B)
       =\int_B Z_T^{-1}\,d\nu_T.
\]
Conditional-expectation convergence gives both \(L^1\) limits and hence
both uniform-integrability assertions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Characteristic-function rigidity and arbitrary mixing}
\label{sec:ng-characteristic-mixing-rigidity}

Throughout this subsection, $X_j$ are independent copies of a real random
variable $X$ with
\[
  \E X=0,\qquad \E X^2=1,
\]
and the law of $X$ is not Gaussian.  The characteristic-function arguments
below require no moments of order greater than two.  Write
\[
  \varphi(t)=\E e^{itX},\qquad
  \Sigma_f:=\{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}:
                  \operatorname{Law}(\varepsilon X)=\operatorname{Law}(X)\}.
\]
Let $\mathcal P_f$ be the group of coordinate permutations with row
signs in $\Sigma_f$.  When the common law has density $f$, this is
$\mathcal P_\pm$ if $f$ is even, and the ordinary permutation group
if $f$ is not even.

\begin{lemma}[A product approximation for small coefficients]
\label{lem:ng-characteristic-product-tail}
There is a nonnegative function $\eta$, with $\eta(\delta)\to0$ as
$\delta\downarrow0$, such that, for every finite or countable real family
$(b_j)$ with $\sum_j b_j^2<\infty$,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\prod_j\varphi(tb_j)
       -\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2}\sum_jb_j^2\right)\right|
 \leq \eta(\delta)t^2\sum_jb_j^2
 \quad\text{if}\quad \sup_j|tb_j|\leq\delta.
 \label{eq:ng-characteristic-product-tail}
\end{equation}
For a countable family, the product denotes the characteristic function
of the $L^2$ sum $\sum_jb_jX_j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The centered second-moment assumption gives
\[
 \varphi(u)=1-\frac{u^2}{2}+o(u^2).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \eta(\delta):=
 \sup_{0<|u|\leq\delta}
 \frac{|\varphi(u)-e^{-u^2/2}|}{u^2}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Both $\varphi(u)$ and $e^{-u^2/2}$ have modulus at most one.  Telescoping
the difference of their finite products therefore bounds it by the sum
of the differences of the factors, proving
\eqref{eq:ng-characteristic-product-tail} for a finite family.
For a countable family, pass to the limit through finite partial sums in
$L^2$ and hence in distribution.  This argument does not take a logarithm
of the characteristic function.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Qualitative rigidity of a unit row]
\label{lem:ng-unit-row-rigidity}
Let $a^{(n)}\in\ell^2$ satisfy $\|a^{(n)}\|_2=1$.  If
\[
 \operatorname{Law}\!\left(\sum_j a_j^{(n)}X_j\right)
       \Longrightarrow\operatorname{Law}(X),
\]
then
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{j\geq1,\ \varepsilon\in\Sigma_f}
       \|a^{(n)}-\varepsilon e_j\|_2\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:ng-unit-row-qualitative}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first describe the limits of such coefficient arrays.  Permuting
coefficients does not change the law of their sum.  Arrange each row in
nonincreasing order of absolute value, retaining its signs and padding
with zeros if necessary.  Its $j$th coefficient then has modulus at most
$j^{-1/2}$.  From any subsequence we may extract a further subsequence
on which each coefficient converges, say $a_j^{(n)}\to a_j$.
Fatou's lemma gives
\[
 q:=\sum_j a_j^2\leq1.
\]
For a fixed $m$, apply
\cref{lem:ng-characteristic-product-tail} to the coefficients with
$j>m$.  For every fixed $t$, the error in replacing their characteristic
function by
\[
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{t^2}{2}
        \left(1-\sum_{j\leq m}(a_j^{(n)})^2\right)\right]
\]
is at most
$t^2\eta(|t|/\sqrt{m+1})$, uniformly in $n$.  First let $n\to\infty$
and then let $m\to\infty$.  The limiting characteristic function is
\begin{equation}
 e^{-(1-q)t^2/2}\prod_j\varphi(a_jt).
 \label{eq:ng-unit-row-gaussian-residual}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the limiting law is that of
\[
 \sum_j a_jX_j+\sqrt{1-q}\,Z,
\]
where $Z$ is standard Gaussian and independent of the $X_j$.

Under the convergence hypothesis this limiting law is the law of $X$.
Suppose that no $|a_j|$ is one.  If all $a_j$ vanish,
\eqref{eq:ng-unit-row-gaussian-residual} already makes $X$ Gaussian.
Otherwise the maximum
$\rho:=\max_j|a_j|$ exists, since $(a_j)\in\ell^2$, and satisfies
$0<\rho<1$.  Iterating the characteristic-function identity
\eqref{eq:ng-unit-row-gaussian-residual} $k$ times gives
\begin{equation}
 \varphi(t)=e^{-(1-q^k)t^2/2}
       \prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb N^k}\varphi(tb_\alpha),
 \qquad
 b_\alpha=a_{\alpha_1}\cdots a_{\alpha_k}.
 \label{eq:ng-smoothing-iteration}
\end{equation}
Here
\[
 \sum_\alpha b_\alpha^2=q^k,
 \qquad \sup_\alpha|b_\alpha|\leq\rho^k.
\]
The identity may be obtained by substituting independent copies into
the $L^2$ series; at each finite depth all series have summable
coefficient squares.  Applying
\cref{lem:ng-characteristic-product-tail} to the last product in
\eqref{eq:ng-smoothing-iteration} yields
\[
 |\varphi(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|
 \leq t^2q^k\eta(|t|\rho^k)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This again makes $X$ Gaussian, a contradiction.  Thus some
$a_j=\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}$; all the remaining coefficients and
the Gaussian residual vanish.  The limiting identity then says
$\operatorname{Law}(\varepsilon X)=\operatorname{Law}(X)$, so
$\varepsilon\in\Sigma_f$.

To conclude, if \eqref{eq:ng-unit-row-qualitative} failed, take a
subsequence whose distance from all the indicated unit vectors is
bounded below.  The preceding extraction produces a coefficient
converging to an allowed sign of modulus one.  Since the row norms are
one, its squared distance from that signed unit vector tends to zero:
\[
 \|a^{(n)}-\varepsilon e_j\|_2^2
       =2-2\varepsilon a_j^{(n)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Undoing the coefficient permutations gives the required contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform invariance under tail symmetries]
\label{lem:ng-tail-symmetry-uniform}
Let $\mu$ be the i.i.d. product law and let $\nu\ll\mu$.  Let
$\mathcal S_N$ be the finite coordinate permutations, with allowed
coordinate signs, that act identically on coordinates $1,\ldots,N$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{S\in\mathcal S_N}h(\nu,S_\#\nu)\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:ng-tail-symmetry-uniform}
\end{equation}
Also, the one-coordinate marginals of $\nu$ converge to the common
coordinate law of $\mu$, uniformly over coordinates greater than $N$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $p=d\nu/d\mu$ and
$p_N=\E_\mu[p\mid\sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_N)]$.  The martingale
convergence theorem gives $\|p-p_N\|_{L^1(\mu)}\to0$.
Every $S\in\mathcal S_N$ preserves $\mu$ and satisfies
$p_N\circ S^{-1}=p_N$.  Thus
\[
 h(\nu,S_\#\nu)^2
 \leq\|p-p\circ S^{-1}\|_{L^1(\mu)}
 \leq2\|p-p_N\|_{L^1(\mu)},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:ng-tail-symmetry-uniform}.
For $i>N$, the marginal density is
$\E_\mu[p\mid x_i]$.  Independence and $\E_\mu p_N=1$ imply
$\E_\mu[p_N\mid x_i]=1$, so
\[
 \left\|\E_\mu[p\mid x_i]-1\right\|_{L^1(\mu)}
 \leq\|p-p_N\|_{L^1(\mu)}.
\]
This proves the uniform marginal assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact covariance rigidity from a second moment]
\label{lem:ng-compact-covariance-rigidity}
Let $T$ be bounded on $\ell^2$.  If $T_\#\mu\ll\mu$, then
\[
 TT^*-I\quad\text{is compact}.
\]
If $T$ is boundedly invertible, $T^*T-I$ is compact as well.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first record uniform integrability of the squares of the linear sums
\begin{equation}
 Z_b:=\sum_jb_jX_j,\qquad \|b\|_2\leq B,
 \label{eq:ng-uniform-linear-square-family}
\end{equation}
for each fixed $B<\infty$.  Let
\[
 X^{(M)}=X\mathbf1_{\{|X|\leq M\}}
           -\E[X\mathbf1_{\{|X|\leq M\}}],
 \qquad Z_b^{(M)}=\sum_jb_jX_j^{(M)}.
\]
The variables $X^{(M)}$ are bounded and centered, and converge to $X$
in $L^2$.  Independence gives
\[
 \sup_{\|b\|_2\leq B}\|Z_b-Z_b^{(M)}\|_2
 \leq B\|X-X^{(M)}\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
For fixed $M$, expanding the fourth moment of a sum of independent
centered bounded variables gives
\[
 \sup_{\|b\|_2\leq B}\E|Z_b^{(M)}|^4\leq C_M B^4<\infty.
\]
The bound first holds for finite sums; it also makes their truncations
Cauchy in $L^4$, proving the countable version.  Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{\|b\|_2\leq B}
 \bigl\|Z_b^2-(Z_b^{(M)})^2\bigr\|_1
 \leq B^2\|X-X^{(M)}\|_2
                  (\|X\|_2+\|X^{(M)}\|_2)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the squares in \eqref{eq:ng-uniform-linear-square-family}
are uniformly approximated in $L^1$ by uniformly integrable families,
and are themselves uniformly integrable.  This uses fourth moments of
bounded truncations, not a fourth-moment assumption on $X$.

Write $\nu=T_\#\mu$, $p=d\nu/d\mu$, and
$p_N=\E_\mu[p\mid\sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_N)]$.  For a finitely supported
unit vector $a$ supported on coordinates greater than $N$, put
$F_a(x)=(\sum_i a_ix_i)^2$.  Under $\mu$, these are squares from
\eqref{eq:ng-uniform-linear-square-family} with $B=1$; under $\nu$,
they are squares of $Z_{T^*a}$ under $\mu$, with
$\|T^*a\|_2\leq\|T\|$.  They are therefore uniformly integrable
under both laws.  The truncated statistic $F_a\wedge R$ depends only
on tail coordinates.  Independence yields
\[
 \left|\E_\nu(F_a\wedge R)-\E_\mu(F_a\wedge R)\right|
 \leq R\|p-p_N\|_1.
\]
First let $N\to\infty$ at fixed $R$, and then let $R\to\infty$.
Uniform integrability gives
\[
 \sup_{\substack{\|a\|_2=1,\ a\text{ finitely supported}\\
                  \operatorname{supp}a\subset\{N+1,N+2,\ldots\}}}
 \left|\langle(TT^*-I)a,a\rangle\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since $TT^*-I$ is self-adjoint and bounded, density of finite vectors
in the tail subspace implies
\[
 \|(I-P_N)(TT^*-I)(I-P_N)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The complementary rows and columns form a finite-rank operator.  Hence
$TT^*-I$ is a norm limit of finite-rank operators and is compact.
For invertible $T$, use
$T^*T-I=T^{-1}(TT^*-I)T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tail rigidity without higher moments]
\label{lem:ng-tail-ica}
Assume that the common law has a positive density $f$, that
$g=\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R)$, and that $xg'\in L^2(\mathbb R)$.
If $T\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2)$ and $T_\#\mu\sim\mu$, then
\begin{equation}
 T-Q\in\mathcal S_2
 \qquad\text{for some }Q\in\mathcal P_f.
 \label{eq:ng-tail-ica-conclusion}
\end{equation}
When $T$ is orthogonal, the condition $xg'\in L^2$ can be omitted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $r_i=T^*e_i$.  By
\cref{lem:ng-compact-covariance-rigidity}, $\|r_i\|_2\to1$.
The law of $\sum_j(r_i)_jX_j$ converges to the common coordinate law
by \cref{lem:ng-tail-symmetry-uniform}.  Normalizing $r_i$ to a unit
vector changes this sum by a quantity tending to zero in $L^2$.
Thus \cref{lem:ng-unit-row-rigidity} gives indices $\pi(i)$ and
signs $\varepsilon_i\in\Sigma_f$, outside a finite initial set, with
\begin{equation}
 s_i:=\varepsilon_i e_{\pi(i)},\qquad
 \eta_i:=\|T^*e_i-s_i\|_2^2\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:ng-qualitative-tail-matching}
\end{equation}
The indices $\pi(i)$ are distinct sufficiently far out.  Indeed, if
$\pi(i)=\pi(j)$ for two such rows, then
$\varepsilon_iT^*e_i-\varepsilon_jT^*e_j$ has arbitrarily small norm
by \eqref{eq:ng-qualitative-tail-matching}, whereas bounded
invertibility gives the lower bound
$\sqrt2/\|T^{-1}\|$.  Fix a cutoff after which $\pi$ is injective.

There is also column approximation along this matching.  Compactness
of $T^*T-I$ and the fact that $\pi(i)$ leaves every finite set imply
\[
 \|Ts_i\|_2^2\longrightarrow1.
\]
Since $\langle Ts_i,e_i\rangle=\langle s_i,r_i\rangle\to1$,
we obtain
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_i:=\|Ts_i-e_i\|_2^2\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \rho_i:=\eta_i+\zeta_i\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:ng-row-column-tail-errors}
\end{equation}

We prove that $\sum_i\rho_i<\infty$ on this tail.  By
\cref{lem:ng-local-hs-coercivity}, there are $c_0,\delta_0>0$ such
that
\begin{equation}
 h(\mu,W_\#\mu)\geq c_0\|W-I\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \quad\text{when }W\in\mathrm{GL}(\ell^2),\quad
 \|W-I\|_{\mathcal S_2}\leq\delta_0.
 \label{eq:ng-local-lower-used}
\end{equation}
Suppose instead that $\sum_i\rho_i=\infty$.
Fix $\delta>0$ small enough that
$3\|T^{-1}\|\delta<\delta_0$.  In any sufficiently remote tail,
where $\rho_i\leq\delta^2$, choose a finite set $E$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \delta^2\leq\sum_{i\in E}\rho_i\leq2\delta^2.
 \label{eq:ng-active-tail-error}
\end{equation}
Let $q=|E|$.  Pair each $i\in E$ with a distinct auxiliary index
$k_i$ beyond $E$, and set $G=\{k_i:i\in E\}$.
Since $\rho_i\to0$, these auxiliary indices may be chosen with
$\sum_{k\in G}\rho_k$ arbitrarily small, while $E$ remains fixed.

Let $S$ exchange each output pair $(e_i,e_{k_i})$, and let $P$ exchange
the aligned signed input pairs $(s_i,s_{k_i})$, acting identically on
their orthogonal complements.  Both are involutions in
$\mathcal P_f$, so they preserve $\mu$.  Define
\[
 W=P T^{-1} S T.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 W-I=P T^{-1}(ST-TP),\qquad
 \frac{\|ST-TP\|_{\mathcal S_2}}{\|T\|}
 \leq\|W-I\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \leq\|T^{-1}\|\,\|ST-TP\|_{\mathcal S_2}.
 \label{eq:ng-swap-relative-norm}
\end{equation}
These are finite-rank differences, since $S-I$ and $P-I$ have finite
rank.  Hellinger invariance and $P_\#\mu=\mu$ give
\begin{equation}
 h(\mu,W_\#\mu)=h(T_\#\mu,S_\#T_\#\mu).
 \label{eq:ng-swap-hellinger-identity}
\end{equation}
All maps in use lie in a countable operator group, so
\cref{lem:ng-measurable-action} justifies this identity on a common
carrier.

We next estimate the commutator in
\eqref{eq:ng-swap-relative-norm}.  Let $R_G$ denote projection onto
the output vectors $(e_k)_{k\in G}$, let $C_G$ denote projection onto
the input vectors $(s_k)_{k\in G}$, and let
\[
 J_Gx=\sum_{k\in G}\langle x,s_k\rangle e_k,\qquad
 T_0=(I-R_G)T(I-C_G)+J_G.
\]
Thus $T_0$ has exact matched rows and columns at all the auxiliary
coordinates.  Orthogonal block decomposition gives
\begin{equation}
 \|T-T_0\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2
 \leq\sum_{k\in G}
       \bigl(\|T^*e_k-s_k\|_2^2+\|Ts_k-e_k\|_2^2\bigr)
 =\sum_{k\in G}\rho_k.
 \label{eq:ng-exact-dummy-approximation}
\end{equation}
For completeness, the left side counts the squared entries of the
auxiliary row and column errors once, while their intersection is
counted twice on the right.

Order the output subspaces as $E$, its complement outside $E\cup G$,
and $G$; order the input subspaces as the aligned signed coordinates
for $E$, their complement outside the aligned $E\cup G$, and the
aligned coordinates for $G$.  In these orthonormal decompositions,
\[
 T_0=\begin{pmatrix}A&B&0\\ C&D&0\\0&0&I_q\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 ST_0-T_0P=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&-B&I_q-A\\
 C&0&-C\\
 A-I_q&B&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The blocks $B,C$ have finite-dimensional range or domain and are
Hilbert--Schmidt.  Put
\[
 \rho_E(T_0):=\sum_{i\in E}
 \bigl(\|T_0^*e_i-s_i\|_2^2+\|T_0s_i-e_i\|_2^2\bigr)
 =2\|A-I_q\|_F^2+\|B\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2+\|C\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2.
\]
The displayed commutator gives the exact identity
\[
 \|ST_0-T_0P\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2
 =2\bigl(\|A-I_q\|_F^2+\|B\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2
                         +\|C\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2\bigr),
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}
 \rho_E(T_0)\leq\|ST_0-T_0P\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2
                         \leq2\rho_E(T_0).
 \label{eq:ng-swap-block-energy}
\end{equation}
As the auxiliary error in
\eqref{eq:ng-exact-dummy-approximation} tends to zero,
$\rho_E(T_0)\to\sum_{i\in E}\rho_i$.  Also,
\[
 \|(ST-TP)-(ST_0-T_0P)\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \leq2\|T-T_0\|_{\mathcal S_2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
By \eqref{eq:ng-active-tail-error} and
\eqref{eq:ng-swap-block-energy}, actual finite auxiliary indices can
thus be chosen so that
\[
 \frac{\delta}{2}\leq\|ST-TP\|_{\mathcal S_2}\leq3\delta.
\]
It follows from \eqref{eq:ng-swap-relative-norm} that
\[
 \frac{\delta}{2\|T\|}
 \leq\|W-I\|_{\mathcal S_2}
 \leq3\|T^{-1}\|\delta<\delta_0.
\]
The local bound \eqref{eq:ng-local-lower-used} now forces
$h(\mu,W_\#\mu)\geq c_0\delta/(2\|T\|)$.
On the other hand, $S$ is supported in the original remote tail, and
\cref{lem:ng-tail-symmetry-uniform} makes the right side of
\eqref{eq:ng-swap-hellinger-identity} uniformly smaller than this
positive number when that tail is far enough out.  This contradiction
proves $\sum_i\rho_i<\infty$.

Define $Q_0$ by the matched rows $s_i$ after the fixed initial cutoff
$N_0$, and by zero rows for $i\leq N_0$.  The summability just proved
implies
\[
 \|T-Q_0\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2
 =\sum_{i\leq N_0}\|T^*e_i\|_2^2
                      +\sum_{i>N_0}\eta_i<\infty.
\]
Thus $Q_0$ is a compact perturbation of an invertible operator and is
Fredholm of index zero.  Its cokernel consists precisely of the first
$N_0$ output coordinates, while its kernel is spanned by the unused
input coordinates.  There are exactly $N_0$ unused columns.  Match
them to the first $N_0$ rows with sign $+1$.  This completes $Q_0$ to
a single $Q\in\mathcal P_f$ and proves
\eqref{eq:ng-tail-ica-conclusion}.

If $T$ is orthogonal, both covariance defects vanish, and every
relative commutator $W=P T^*ST$ constructed above is orthogonal.
Consequently only the orthogonal part of
\cref{lem:ng-local-hs-coercivity} is needed.  That part uses location
Fisher information alone, so the condition $xg'\in L^2$ may be
omitted in this case.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact linear stabilizer under a second-moment hypothesis]
\label{prop:ng-iid-stabilizer}
For a centered, variance-one, non-Gaussian i.i.d. product law,
the bounded invertible linear stabilizer is exactly $\mathcal P_f$.
This assertion requires neither a density nor Fisher information.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every member of $\mathcal P_f$ preserves the product law.
Conversely, if $T_\#\mu=\mu$, covariance gives $TT^*=I$.
Since $T$ is boundedly invertible, it is orthogonal.  Each of its unit
rows gives a linear combination with the same law as $X$.
Applying \cref{lem:ng-unit-row-rigidity} to the constant sequence
consisting of this row shows that the row is an allowed signed unit
vector.  Orthogonality makes the selected coordinates a bijection.
Thus $T\in\mathcal P_f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:ng-iid-endpoint}]
Necessity follows from \cref{lem:ng-tail-ica}.  If
$T-Q\in\mathcal S_2$ for $Q\in\mathcal P_f$, then
$TQ^{-1}=I+(T-Q)Q^{-1}$ is invertible and differs from the identity
by a Hilbert--Schmidt operator.  By \cref{prop:ng-hs-sufficiency},
$\nu_{TQ^{-1}}\sim\mu$.  Since $Q_\#\mu=\mu$, this is
$\nu_T\sim\mu$.  The same proposition gives the finite-corner
likelihood and square-root limits stated in the theorem.
The exact stabilizer is identified in \cref{prop:ng-iid-stabilizer}.
Finally, \cref{lem:ng-orbit-dichotomy,lem:ng-projected-likelihoods}
give the projected-affinity characterization, the singular alternative,
and both uniform-integrability statements.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete orthogonal criterion with only location Fisher information]
\label{thm:ng-orthogonal-endpoint}
Let $f>0$ almost everywhere be a centered, variance-one, non-Gaussian
density, and assume only $\sqrt f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R)$ in addition
to its second moment.  For every orthogonal $U$ on $\ell^2$,
\[
 U_\#\mu\sim\mu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 U-Q\in\mathcal S_2
 \quad\text{for some }Q\in\mathcal P_f.
\]
If this condition fails, $U_\#\mu\perp\mu$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity is \cref{lem:ng-tail-ica}.  For sufficiency, write
$UQ^{-1}=I+(U-Q)Q^{-1}$.  This is orthogonal and differs from the
identity by a Hilbert--Schmidt operator.  Apply
\cref{prop:ng-orthogonal-fisher-hs}, and then use
$Q_\#\mu=\mu$.  The alternative follows from
\cref{lem:ng-orbit-dichotomy}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact block-product theorem}
\label{sec:ng-block-product}

Let
\[
  X=\prod_{j\geq 1}\mathbb R^{d_j},
  \qquad
  \mu=\bigotimes_{j\geq 1}\mu_j,
\]
on the product Borel sigma-field.  Assume that every \(\mu_j\) has a
Lebesgue density positive almost everywhere.  For invertible matrices
\(T_j\in\mathrm{GL}(d_j,\mathbb R)\), the coordinatewise map
\[
  T=\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}T_j
\]
is measurable on \(X\), and
\[
  T_\#\mu=\bigotimes_{j\geq 1}(T_j)_\#\mu_j.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:ng-affinity-convention}, define the one-block affinity and
action by
\begin{equation}
  \rho_j(T_j)
  :=\mathsf A\bigl(\mu_j,(T_j)_\#\mu_j\bigr),
  \qquad
  e_j(T_j):=-2\log\rho_j(T_j).
  \label{eq:ng-block-action}
\end{equation}
The strict positivity of the block densities implies \(0<\rho_j(T_j)\leq 1\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact block-product criterion]
\label{thm:ng-block-kakutani}
For every block-diagonal invertible \(T\) as above,
\begin{equation}
  T_\#\mu\sim\mu
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e_j(T_j)<\infty.
  \label{eq:ng-block-criterion}
\end{equation}
If the series diverges, then \(T_\#\mu\perp\mu\).  More precisely, for every
finite set \(F\Subset\mathbb N\),
\begin{align}
  \mathsf A_F(T)
    &=\prod_{j\in F}\rho_j(T_j),
  &
  E_F(T)
    &:=-2\log\mathsf A_F(T)
      =\sum_{j\in F}e_j(T_j),
  \label{eq:ng-block-tensorization}
\end{align}
and, whenever \(F\subset G\),
\begin{equation}
  E_G(T)-E_F(T)=\sum_{j\in G\setminus F}e_j(T_j).
  \label{eq:ng-block-increment}
\end{equation}
Thus singularity is exactly the escape \(E_F(T)\uparrow\infty\) along the
directed family of finite coordinate sets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both formulas in \eqref{eq:ng-block-tensorization} follow from tensorization of
Hellinger affinity.  Kakutani's theorem~\cite{kakutani1948equivalence}
says that two products of pairwise
equivalent probability measures are equivalent precisely when their infinite
product affinity is positive, and otherwise they are mutually singular.  In
the present notation,
\[
  \prod_j\rho_j(T_j)>0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  -\sum_j\log\rho_j(T_j)<\infty,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:ng-block-criterion}.  The increment identity is immediate
from additivity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact affine-coordinate theorem}
\label{sec:ng-affine-coordinate}

Let \(f>0\) almost everywhere be a nondegenerate density on \(\mathbb R\), put
\(g=\sqrt f\), and assume
\begin{equation}
  g\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R),
  \qquad xg'\in L^2(\mathbb R).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-sobolev}
\end{equation}
For \((u,v)\in\mathbb R^2\), let \(q_{u,v}\) be the density of
\(e^uX+v\) when \(X\) has density \(f\):
\begin{equation}
  q_{u,v}(y)=e^{-u}f\bigl(e^{-u}(y-v)\bigr).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-density}
\end{equation}
If \(\psi=-f'/f\) denotes the location score, differentiation of the square
root at the identity gives
\begin{equation}
  D\sqrt q_{0,0}(u,v)
   =u\left(-\tfrac12g-xg'\right)-vg',
  \qquad
  S_u(x)=x\psi(x)-1,
  \quad S_v(x)=\psi(x).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-scores}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{aff}}
  :=\E_f
  \begin{bmatrix}S_u\\S_v\end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix}S_u&S_v\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \delta(u,v):=-2\log\mathsf A(f,q_{u,v}).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-information}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal dilation and translation]
\label{thm:ng-diagonal-affine}
Assume \eqref{eq:ng-affine-sobolev}, assume that
\(I_{\mathrm{aff}}\) is positive definite, and assume that the affine
root-density orbit is proper, in the concrete sense that
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf A(f,q_{u_n,v_n})\longrightarrow1
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  (u_n,v_n)\longrightarrow(0,0).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-properness}
\end{equation}
For \(\mu=f^{\otimes\mathbb N}\) and the coordinatewise affine map
\[
  T_{u,v}(x)_j=e^{u_j}x_j+v_j,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
  (T_{u,v})_\#\mu\sim\mu
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  u\in\ell^2\ \text{and}\ v\in\ell^2.
  \label{eq:ng-affine-l2-criterion}
\end{equation}
Otherwise the two product measures are mutually singular.  In particular,
for positive diagonal dilations,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{diag}(a_j)_\#\mu\sim\mu
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  (\log a_j)_j\in\ell^2.
  \label{eq:ng-diagonal-dilation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Quadratic-mean differentiability and
\eqref{eq:ng-affine-scores} give
\begin{equation}
  \delta(u,v)
  =\frac14 (u,v)I_{\mathrm{aff}}(u,v)^{\mathsf T}
   +o(u^2+v^2).
  \label{eq:ng-affine-quadratic}
\end{equation}
Positive definiteness supplies two-sided quadratic bounds near the origin.
Condition \eqref{eq:ng-affine-properness} supplies a positive lower bound for
\(\delta\) outside every neighborhood of the origin.  Hence
\[
  \sum_j\delta(u_j,v_j)<\infty
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \sum_j(u_j^2+v_j^2)<\infty.
\]
The result follows from \cref{thm:ng-block-kakutani}, with one-dimensional
blocks.  Setting \(v=0\) and \(a_j=e^{u_j}\) gives
\eqref{eq:ng-diagonal-dilation}.
\end{proof}

For an \(\ell^2\)-valued realization, take
\(\operatorname{Law}((\sigma_jX_j)_j)\) with \(\sigma\in\ell^2\) and
\(\E X_1^2<\infty\), and use \(x_j\mapsto e^{u_j}x_j+\sigma_jv_j\).
Condition \eqref{eq:ng-affine-l2-criterion} then defines an affine
homeomorphism of \(\ell^2\).

\subsection{Scope, examples, and the role of the second moment}
\label{sec:ng-moment-scope}

The hypotheses in \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions} include centered,
variance-normalized Laplace densities, despite their cusp at the
origin.  They also include variance-normalized densities proportional
to $e^{-x^4}$.  Symmetry is not needed in any step of the proof.

For a family allowing both asymmetry and heavy tails, take
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde f_{\nu,\beta}(y)
   =c_{\nu,\beta}(1+y^2)^{-(\nu+1)/2}
                           e^{\beta\arctan y},
 \qquad \nu>2,\quad \beta\in\mathbb R,
 \label{eq:ng-asymmetric-heavy-tail-example}
\end{equation}
and center and rescale to variance one.  The unstandardized location
score is
\[
 \widetilde s(y)=\frac{(\nu+1)y-\beta}{1+y^2}.
\]
Both $\widetilde s$ and $y\widetilde s$ are bounded.  The density
is positive and smooth and has a finite second moment.  Centering and
rescaling preserve finiteness of the location and scale information,
so the standardized density satisfies \eqref{eq:ng-iid-assumptions}.
For $2<\nu\le4$ its fourth moment is infinite.  When $\beta\ne0$,
the two tails have unequal leading constants, so centering does not
make the density even.  The case $\beta=0$ is a rescaled Student
family.  These examples show that neither evenness nor a fourth
moment is implicit in the theorem.

The Gamma examples used to illustrate the Fourier response belong to
\cref{lem:ng-fourier-ode}; their centered densities still vanish on
a half-line and therefore do not satisfy the full-support hypothesis
of \cref{thm:ng-iid-endpoint}.  A nonzero Fourier response establishes
local detection, and alone does not establish the general
measure-equivalence theorem for a density with restricted support.

The second moment has a specific role beyond differentiating one
Fourier feature.  It makes every $\ell^2$ row into a convergent
random series, supplies uniform integrability of squares of linear
forms, and gives the Gaussian-residual compactness argument.  Thus
the first-moment ODE result is stronger in its moment scope than the
arbitrary-operator equivalence theorem.  Extending the latter to
infinite-variance coordinates requires a different domain of
admissible rows and a corresponding operator criterion.  For example,
for symmetric nondegenerate $\alpha$-stable coordinates, $0<\alpha<2$,
convergence of a row series requires
$\sum_j|a_j|^\alpha<\infty$: its partial-sum characteristic
functions are
\[
 \exp\left(-c|t|^\alpha\sum_{j\le n}|a_j|^\alpha\right).
\]
An arbitrary $\ell^2$ row need not meet that requirement.  The
condition on admissible rows must also be satisfied when using bounded
or fractional Fourier features.

The Hellinger path estimates give $L^1$ convergence and uniform
integrability of likelihoods.  The finite-frequency functions are bounded in
each coordinate; their dimension-independent bounds are the
$L^2(\mu_n)$ and generator estimates proved above.  No variance
bound for their aggregate under an arbitrary mixed output law is
needed in the tail-symmetry argument.
