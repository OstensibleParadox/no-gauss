\section{Projection increments and class-preserving completion}
\label{sec:ng-projection-completion}

The preceding product calculation suggests an order of construction that does
not mention an operator ideal.  One first asks what every finite observation
can see, then records the information added by refinement, and only afterwards
completes the finite-dimensional core.  This order is important outside the
Gaussian chart: an infinite-dimensional norm chosen in advance can confuse a
feature of one parametrization with an invariant of the underlying laws.

Throughout this section, $(X,\mathcal A)$ is a standard Borel space,
$\mathcal G$ is a group of bimeasurable automorphisms of $X$, and
$\mu_0$ is a probability law on $X$.  We write
\[
   \mathcal O(\mu_0)
   :=\{g_\#\mu_0:g\in\mathcal G\}.
\]
Linearity of $X$ or of the action will not be used until a Hilbert information
chart is introduced.  Thus the measure-theoretic construction below applies
to arbitrary automorphism orbits.

\subsection{Finite tests and joint observations}
\label{sec:ng-finite-tests}

Let $\mathscr T$ be a family of measurable maps
$\tau:X\to X_\tau$, where each $X_\tau$ is a finite-dimensional standard
Borel space.  For a finite set $F\Subset\mathscr T$, put
\[
   Q_F:X\longrightarrow\prod_{\tau\in F}X_\tau,
   \qquad
   Q_F(x):=(\tau(x))_{\tau\in F},
   \qquad
   \mathcal A_F:=\sigma(Q_F).
\]
If $F\subset F'$, then $Q_F$ is a measurable factor of $Q_{F'}$ and
$\mathcal A_F\subset\mathcal A_{F'}$.  We assume that the tests are
closed under finite joint observations: after replacing $\mathscr T$ by its
finite-joint closure, each $Q_F$ is itself an allowed test (up to a Borel
isomorphism of its target).  This replacement does not change the generated
sigma-field, and it ensures that convergence for all cylinder gauges controls
the joint laws $(Q_F)_\#\mu$, not merely the separate one-test marginals.  We
also assume that the tests are generating in the sense that
\begin{equation}
   \sigma\!\left(\bigcup_{F\Subset\mathscr T}\mathcal A_F\right)
   =\mathcal A
   \quad\text{modulo }\mu+\nu
   \label{sec:ng-generating-tests}
\end{equation}
for every pair of laws under consideration.  A fixed increasing sequence of
finite tests suffices when $\mathcal A$ is countably generated, but the
directed formulation avoids choosing coordinates.

For probability laws $a,b$, let
\[
  \operatorname{Aff}(a,b)
  :=\int\sqrt{\frac{da}{d\lambda}\frac{db}{d\lambda}}\,d\lambda,
  \qquad
  h(a,b)^2:=2\{1-\operatorname{Aff}(a,b)\},
  \qquad \lambda:=a+b.
\]
The expression is independent of the dominating law.  There are two natural
finite-test quantities, and they have different jobs.

\begin{definition}[Cylinder gauge]
For $F\Subset\mathscr T$, define
\begin{equation}
 d_F^{\mathrm{cyl}}(\mu,\nu)
 :=\max_{\tau\in F}h(\tau_\#\mu,\tau_\#\nu).
 \label{sec:ng-cylinder-gauge}
\end{equation}
The family $(d_F^{\mathrm{cyl}})_F$ generates the initial, or cylinder,
uniformity on probability laws.
\end{definition}

The gauges are monotone under inclusion:
\[
 F\subset F'
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_F^{\mathrm{cyl}}\le d_{F'}^{\mathrm{cyl}}.
\]
Their role is topological.  They say that finitely many prescribed marginals
are close, and finite maxima allow different menus of tests to be combined.

\begin{definition}[Joint projection energy]
For $F\Subset\mathscr T$, define
\begin{equation}
 E_F(\mu,\nu)
 :=-2\log\operatorname{Aff}\bigl((Q_F)_\#\mu,(Q_F)_\#\nu\bigr),
 \label{sec:ng-joint-energy}
\end{equation}
with the convention $-\log0=+\infty$.
\end{definition}

The energy is the order-$1/2$ R\'enyi divergence of the joint observation.
It is not the maximum of the one-test divergences.  The latter discards
dependence among the tests and therefore cannot represent information in the
new shell $\mathcal A_{F'}\ominus\mathcal A_F$.  Nor do we assert that $E_F$
is a metric: it is a monotone action used to exhaust a measure class.  In
short,
\[
 \boxed{
 \ d_F^{\mathrm{cyl}}\ \text{generates the finite-test uniformity},
 \qquad
 E_F\ \text{measures joint information and its increments}.\ }
\]

\subsection{Projection limits and singularity explosion}
\label{sec:ng-projection-limits}

Write $\mu_F=(Q_F)_\#\mu$, $\nu_F=(Q_F)_\#\nu$, and
$\rho_F=\operatorname{Aff}(\mu_F,\nu_F)$.  Data processing immediately gives
$\rho_{F'}\le\rho_F$, and hence $E_{F'}\ge E_F$, whenever $F\subset F'$.
The limit has an exact global meaning.

\begin{theorem}[Projection affinity theorem]
\label{sec:ng-projection-affinity-theorem}
Under \eqref{sec:ng-generating-tests}, as a directed net,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_F\downarrow\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\nu),
 \qquad
 E_F(\mu,\nu)\uparrow
 -2\log\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\nu).
 \label{sec:ng-affinity-limit}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \mu\perp\nu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 E_F(\mu,\nu)\longrightarrow+\infty.
 \label{sec:ng-singularity-explosion}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $\lambda=(\mu+\nu)/2$, $p=d\mu/d\lambda$, and
$q=d\nu/d\lambda$.  Pulling finite-stage densities back to $X$ gives
\[
 p_F=\E_\lambda[p\mid\mathcal A_F],
 \qquad
 q_F=\E_\lambda[q\mid\mathcal A_F].
\]
The generating assumption and martingale approximation imply
$p_F\to p$ and $q_F\to q$ in $L^1(\lambda)$.  Since
\[
 \|\sqrt a-\sqrt b\|_{L^2}^2\le\|a-b\|_{L^1},
\]
we obtain
$\int\sqrt{p_Fq_F}\,d\lambda\to\int\sqrt{pq}\,d\lambda$.
Monotonicity follows from data processing.  Finally, affinity is zero exactly
when the two laws are mutually singular.
\end{proof}

There is also an exact conditional increment.  If $F\subset F'$ and
$\rho_F>0$, disintegrate the two $F'$-stage laws over the common $F$-stage
observation.  Let $r_{F'\mid F}(x)$ be the conditional affinity and let
$\eta_F$ be the normalized Hellinger midpoint law, defined by
\[
 \frac{d\eta_F}{d(\mu_F+\nu_F)}
 =\frac{
 \sqrt{d\mu_F/d(\mu_F+\nu_F)}
 \sqrt{d\nu_F/d(\mu_F+\nu_F)}}{\rho_F}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{F'}=\rho_F\int r_{F'\mid F}\,d\eta_F,
 \qquad
 E_{F'}-E_F
 =-2\log\int r_{F'\mid F}\,d\eta_F\ge0.
 \label{sec:ng-conditional-shell}
\end{equation}
For independent product blocks the conditional affinity is independent of
the old observation, and the energy increment becomes literally additive.
In a general orbit, \eqref{sec:ng-conditional-shell} is the replacement for
an orthogonal sum of coordinate energies.

The R\'enyi--$1/2$ energy is preferable to relative entropy as a global class
detector.  Infinite relative entropy may occur for equivalent measures
because a log density is not integrable.  By contrast,
\eqref{sec:ng-singularity-explosion} detects singularity with no integrability
hypothesis.

\subsection{Equivalence requires two-sided likelihood closure}
\label{sec:ng-two-sided-ui}

Finite energy says that two laws are not mutually singular; without a
dichotomy it does not say that they are equivalent.  The missing condition is
the preservation of likelihood mass in both directions.

Assume that $\mu_F\sim\nu_F$ at every finite stage and lift
\[
 Z_F:=\frac{d\mu_F}{d\nu_F}\circ Q_F
\]
to $X$.  If $F\subset F'$, then
\begin{equation}
 \E_\nu[Z_{F'}\mid\mathcal A_F]=Z_F.
 \label{sec:ng-likelihood-martingale}
\end{equation}
Thus $(Z_F)_F$ is a nonnegative $\nu$-martingale of mean one, while
$(Z_F^{-1})_F$ is the corresponding $\mu$-martingale.

\begin{theorem}[Two-sided likelihood criterion]
\label{sec:ng-two-sided-ui-theorem}
Under \eqref{sec:ng-generating-tests} and finite-stage equivalence,
\begin{align}
 \mu\ll\nu
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{Z_F:F\Subset\mathscr T\}
 \quad\text{is uniformly integrable in }L^1(\nu),
 \label{sec:ng-forward-ui}\\
 \nu\ll\mu
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{Z_F^{-1}:F\Subset\mathscr T\}
 \quad\text{is uniformly integrable in }L^1(\mu).
 \label{sec:ng-reverse-ui}
\end{align}
In particular, $\mu\sim\nu$ if and only if both conditions hold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $Z=d\mu/d\nu$ exists in $L^1(\nu)$, then
$Z_F=\E_\nu[Z\mid\mathcal A_F]$; conditional expectations of one
$L^1$ variable form a uniformly integrable family.  Conversely, uniform
integrability makes the likelihood martingale relatively weakly compact in
$L^1(\nu)$.  Select a weakly convergent cofinal subnet
$Z_{F_\alpha}\rightharpoonup Z$ in $L^1(\nu)$.  Cofinality means that for
each fixed $F_0$ the indices eventually satisfy $F_\alpha\supset F_0$.
Consequently, if $A\in\mathcal A_{F_0}$, then eventually
\[
 \int_A Z_{F_\alpha}\,d\nu
 =\int_A Z_{F_0}\,d\nu
 =\mu(A)
\]
by \eqref{sec:ng-likelihood-martingale}; weak convergence against
$\mathbf 1_A\in L^\infty(\nu)$ gives
$\int_A Z\,d\nu=\mu(A)$.  The directedness of the finite test sets implies
that $\bigcup_F\mathcal A_F$ is an algebra: if
$A\in\mathcal A_F$ and $B\in\mathcal A_{F'}$, then both sets belong to
$\mathcal A_{F\cup F'}$, which is closed under complements and finite unions.
By \eqref{sec:ng-generating-tests}, this algebra generates $\mathcal A$
modulo $\mu+\nu$.  The monotone-class theorem therefore extends the identity
to every $A\in\mathcal A$, so $d\mu=Z\,d\nu$.  Interchanging the laws proves
the reverse assertion.
\end{proof}

The reverse condition is genuinely a lower-tail condition for the forward
likelihood:
\begin{equation}
 \E_\mu\!\left[Z_F^{-1}\mathbf 1_{\{Z_F^{-1}>M\}}\right]
 =\nu\{Z_F<M^{-1}\}.
 \label{sec:ng-lower-tail-ui}
\end{equation}
It cannot be recovered from an upper-tail estimate on $Z_F$.  A convenient
quantitative version uses convex superlinear functions $\Phi,\Psi$:
\begin{equation}
 \sup_F\E_\nu\Phi(Z_F)<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sup_F\E_\mu\Psi(Z_F^{-1})<\infty.
 \label{sec:ng-dvp-block}
\end{equation}
Together with compatible finite-stage limits and an honest realization, fixed
de la Vall\'ee--Poussin bounds give the two-sided uniform integrability needed
to close both Radon--Nikodym directions.  They do not by themselves supply the
countably additive realization.

\begin{lemma}[Hellinger lower semicontinuity of the Orlicz bounds]
\label{sec:ng-orlicz-hellinger-lsc}
Let $\nu$ be a probability law, let $\lambda_i=z_i\nu$ be a net of
probability laws with $z_i>0$ $\nu$-almost everywhere, and suppose that
$\lambda_i\to\lambda$ in Hellinger distance.  Then $\lambda=z\nu$ for some
$z\geq0$,
\[
 \sqrt{z_i}\longrightarrow\sqrt z\quad\text{in }L^2(\nu),
 \qquad
 z_i\longrightarrow z\quad\text{in }L^1(\nu).
\]
Let $\Phi,\Psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ be lower-semicontinuous convex
functions, both superlinear at infinity.
Define the lower-semicontinuous perspective
\begin{equation}
 \Psi^\leftarrow(r):=
 \begin{cases}
   r\Psi(r^{-1}),&r>0,\\
   +\infty,&r=0.
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \Psi^\leftarrow(0)
 =\lim_{r\downarrow0}r\Psi(r^{-1})
 =\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\Psi(u)}u=+\infty.
 \label{sec:ng-reverse-orlicz-perspective}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 \int\Phi(z)\,d\nu
 &\leq\liminf_i\int\Phi(z_i)\,d\nu,
 \label{sec:ng-forward-orlicz-lsc}\\
 \int\Psi^\leftarrow(z)\,d\nu
 &\leq\liminf_i\int\Psi^\leftarrow(z_i)\,d\nu
 =\liminf_i\int\Psi(z_i^{-1})\,d\lambda_i.
 \label{sec:ng-reverse-orlicz-lsc}
\end{align}
In particular, a finite uniform bound on the second functional forces
$z>0$ $\nu$-almost everywhere and passes the reverse Orlicz bound to the
limit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Hellinger convergence implies convergence in total variation.  Since every
$\lambda_i$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\nu$, so is $\lambda$.
The Hellinger identity gives the asserted $L^2$ convergence of the square
roots, and Cauchy--Schwarz then gives the $L^1$ convergence of the densities.
The function $\Psi^\leftarrow$ is convex by the perspective construction and
is lower-semicontinuous at zero by superlinearity.  The integral of a
nonnegative lower-semicontinuous convex function is lower-semicontinuous for
$L^1$ convergence.  This proves both inequalities.  The equality in
\eqref{sec:ng-reverse-orlicz-lsc} is change of measure, and the value
$\Psi^\leftarrow(0)=+\infty$ proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why the bare projective uniformity is too weak}
\label{sec:ng-cylinder-counterexample}

Let $\ell(dx)=\frac12e^{-|x|}\,dx$ and
$\mu_0=\bigotimes_{j\ge1}\ell$ on $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$.  Fix
$c>0$, $c\ne1$, and define
\[
 T_n=\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{c,\ldots,c}_{n},1,1,\ldots).
\]
Put $\mu_n=(T_n)_\#\mu_0$ and $\mu_\infty=(cI)_\#\mu_0$.  Every $\mu_n$ is
equivalent to $\mu_0$, because only finitely many coordinates have changed.
For each fixed finite coordinate set $F$, however,
\[
 (Q_F)_\#\mu_n=(Q_F)_\#\mu_\infty
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.
\]
Thus $(\mu_n)$ is Cauchy, and in fact convergent to $\mu_\infty$, for the
bare cylinder uniformity.  The one-coordinate affinity
$\rho=2\sqrt c/(1+c)$ is strictly less than one, so
\begin{equation}
 E_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}(\mu_n,\mu_0)
 =-2n\log\rho
 =2n\log\frac{1+c}{2\sqrt c}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \label{sec:ng-laplace-energy}
\end{equation}
Kakutani's theorem gives $\mu_\infty\perp\mu_0$.  Moreover, if $m>n$, then
$\operatorname{Aff}(\mu_m,\mu_n)=\rho^{m-n}$; in particular, consecutive
terms remain a fixed positive Hellinger distance apart.  Hence the sequence
is not Cauchy for the admissible metric defined below.  There is no conflict:
the completion of the bare cylinder uniformity contains the singular law
$\mu_\infty$, whereas the stronger Cauchy structure below deliberately puts
that boundary at nonzero distance.

\subsection{The admissible metric completion}
\label{sec:ng-admissible-completion}

The example shows that selecting some cylinder-Cauchy threads after the fact
would not produce a completion of the cylinder uniformity.  We instead define
a stronger, class-preserving Cauchy structure before taking a completion.
Let
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mu_0}:=\{\mu:\mu\text{ is a probability law and }\mu\sim\mu_0\}.
\]
For $\mu\in\mathcal P_{\mu_0}$, write
\[
 Z_\mu:=\frac{d\mu}{d\mu_0},
 \qquad L_\mu:=\log Z_\mu,
\]
as equivalence classes modulo $\mu_0$-null sets.  On $L^0(\mu_0)$ use the
complete metric
\[
 \delta_0(f,g):=\int_X\min\{1,|f-g|\}\,d\mu_0,
\]
which induces convergence in $\mu_0$-measure.

\begin{definition}[Admissible Cauchy structure]
\label{sec:ng-class-preserving-definition}
Define the likelihood-graph metric on $\mathcal P_{\mu_0}$ by
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0}(\mu,\nu)
 :=\delta_0(L_\mu,L_\nu)
   +\|Z_\mu-Z_\nu\|_{L^1(\mu_0)}.
 \label{sec:ng-likelihood-graph-metric}
\end{equation}
If $H=\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathcal G}(\mu_0)$, the injective orbit map
$\mathcal G/H\to\mathcal P_{\mu_0}$, $gH\mapsto g_\#\mu_0$, pulls
\eqref{sec:ng-likelihood-graph-metric} back to a Hausdorff metric and hence a
specified uniformity on the orbit quotient.
Fix a core $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathcal P_{\mu_0}$.  A net in
the core is \emph{admissibly Cauchy} when it is Cauchy for
$d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0}$.  Two admissibly Cauchy nets $(\mu_i)_i$ and
$(\nu_k)_k$ are equivalent when
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{(i,k)}d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0}(\mu_i,\nu_k)=0
 \label{sec:ng-admissible-net-equivalence}
\end{equation}
on the product directed set.  Denote the set of equivalence classes by
$\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\,\mathrm{LG}}$ and put
\begin{equation}
 \widehat d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0}
   \bigl([\mu_i],[\nu_k]\bigr)
 :=\lim_{(i,k)}d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0}(\mu_i,\nu_k).
 \label{sec:ng-completed-likelihood-graph-metric}
\end{equation}
The double limit exists because both nets are Cauchy.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness of the admissible Cauchy structure]
\label{sec:ng-admissible-completeness-theorem}
The metric space
$(\mathcal P_{\mu_0},d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0})$ is complete.  The formula
\eqref{sec:ng-completed-likelihood-graph-metric} is therefore a well-defined
complete metric, and the endpoint map identifies
$\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\,\mathrm{LG}}$ isometrically with
\begin{equation}
 \overline{\mathcal O_{\mathrm{fin}}}^{\,d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0}}
 \subset\mathcal P_{\mu_0}.
 \label{sec:ng-admissible-closure}
\end{equation}
Under this identification, the constant-net map is the inclusion of the core.
In particular, the core is dense in its completion and every completion point
is an honest countably additive probability law equivalent to $\mu_0$.
Moreover, every admissibly Cauchy net is Cauchy for each cylinder gauge
$d_F^{\mathrm{cyl}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map
\[
 \mathcal J:\mathcal P_{\mu_0}
 \longrightarrow L^0(\mu_0)\times L^1(\mu_0),
 \qquad \mathcal J(\mu)=(L_\mu,Z_\mu),
\]
is an isometry when the product is given the sum of $\delta_0$ and the
$L^1$ metric.  Its image is closed.  Indeed, suppose
$L_{\mu_n}\to L$ in measure and $Z_{\mu_n}\to Z$ in $L^1$.  A common
subsequence converges almost everywhere in both coordinates, and
$Z_{\mu_n}=e^{L_{\mu_n}}$ yields $Z=e^L>0$ almost everywhere.  Also
$\int Z\,d\mu_0=1$.  Hence $\mu:=Z\mu_0$ belongs to
$\mathcal P_{\mu_0}$ and $\mathcal J(\mu)=(L,Z)$.  Completeness of
$L^0(\mu_0)$ for $\delta_0$ and of $L^1(\mu_0)$ now proves completeness of
$\mathcal P_{\mu_0}$.

The quotient of Cauchy nets by
\eqref{sec:ng-admissible-net-equivalence}, equipped with the double-limit
metric, is the standard metric completion.  The endpoint map sends a class
$[\mu_i]$ to the $d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0}$-limit of $(\mu_i)$ in
$\mathcal P_{\mu_0}$; it is a well-defined isometry onto the closed set
\eqref{sec:ng-admissible-closure}.  This proves the completion assertions.

Finally, data processing and
$h(a,b)^2\leq\|da/d\lambda-db/d\lambda\|_{L^1(\lambda)}$ give
\[
 d_F^{\mathrm{cyl}}(\mu,\nu)^2
 \leq \|Z_\mu-Z_\nu\|_{L^1(\mu_0)}
 \leq d_{\mathrm{LG},\mu_0}(\mu,\nu).
\]
Thus admissible Cauchy nets are cylinder-Cauchy.  Their finite-stage limits
are compatible and are the projections of their unique global endpoint; by
the generating assumption, equality of all those limits is equivalent to
equality in the completed metric quotient.
\end{proof}

The Cauchy structure depends only on the reference measure class.  Indeed, if
$\nu_0\sim\mu_0$, then changing reference subtracts the same fixed
$\log(d\nu_0/d\mu_0)$ from every log-likelihood difference, the two $L^1$
terms agree by change of measure, and equivalent probability laws induce the
same convergence-in-measure uniformity on $L^0$.

Projection energy and two-sided Orlicz bounds remain useful endpoint
criteria, but they do not themselves define this completion.  On an orbit
with pairwise equivalence--singularity dichotomy, a uniform bound
$\sup_F E_F(\mu,\mu_0)<\infty$ forces an already realized law $\mu$ into the
class of $\mu_0$.  Without the dichotomy, the two-sided conditions
\eqref{sec:ng-forward-ui}--\eqref{sec:ng-reverse-ui} do the same, while
\cref{sec:ng-orlicz-hellinger-lsc} passes fixed Orlicz bounds through
finite-stage Hellinger limits.  None of these criteria, without a Cauchy
metric and a closed realization space, justifies calling a selected quotient
of cylinder threads a completion.

\subsection{An abstract projective Feldman--H\'ajek theorem}
\label{sec:ng-abstract-projective-fh}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional projective Feldman--H\'ajek principle]
\label{sec:ng-projective-fh-theorem}
Suppose that every finite joint projection of every pair in
$\mathcal O(\mu_0)$ is equivalent, the tests generate $\mathcal A$, and the
orbit has the pairwise dichotomy
\[
 \mu_g\sim\mu_k\quad\text{or}\quad\mu_g\perp\mu_k,
 \qquad \mu_g:=g_\#\mu_0.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mu_g\sim\mu_k
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_F E_F(\mu_g,\mu_k)<\infty,
 \qquad
 \mu_g\perp\mu_k
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 E_F(\mu_g,\mu_k)\uparrow\infty.
 \label{sec:ng-abstract-fh-dichotomy}
\end{equation}
If the dichotomy is absent, the first equivalence must be replaced by the
two-sided UI conditions \eqref{sec:ng-forward-ui}--\eqref{sec:ng-reverse-ui}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The projection affinity theorem identifies bounded energy with positive
global affinity.  Under the dichotomy, positive affinity rules out the
singular branch and therefore forces equivalence.  Divergence of the energy
is exactly \eqref{sec:ng-singularity-explosion}.  The final statement is the
two-sided likelihood criterion.
\end{proof}

The theorem is deliberately stated on the law side.  It produces a finite
energy component before any coordinates on $\mathcal G$ are chosen.  An
operator ideal can appear only after the energy has been calculated in a
particular chart.

\subsection{Hilbert information charts as a consequence}
\label{sec:ng-hilbert-chart}

Suppose now that finite stages admit Hilbert coordinates.  Let
$(\mathsf H_F)_F$ be a directed system with isometric inclusions
\[
 \iota_{FF'}:\mathsf H_F\longrightarrow\mathsf H_{F'},
 \qquad F\subset F',
\]
and orthogonal bonding projections
$\pi_{F'F}=\iota_{FF'}^*:\mathsf H_{F'}\to\mathsf H_F$, and let
$\Gamma_F(g,k)\in\mathsf H_F$ be compatible pair coordinates.  Assume
\[
 \pi_{F'F}\Gamma_{F'}(g,k)=\Gamma_F(g,k),
 \qquad F\subset F'.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Information-chart completion]
\label{sec:ng-information-chart-theorem}
Assume the hypotheses of the conditional projective theorem.  If, on the
orbit region under consideration, constants $0<c\le C<\infty$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 c\|\Gamma_F(g,k)\|_{\mathsf H_F}^2
 \le E_F(\mu_g,\mu_k)
 \le C\|\Gamma_F(g,k)\|_{\mathsf H_F}^2
 \label{sec:ng-chart-energy-comparison}
\end{equation}
uniformly in $F$, then
\begin{equation}
 \mu_g\sim\mu_k
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_F\|\Gamma_F(g,k)\|_{\mathsf H_F}<\infty.
 \label{sec:ng-chart-finite-length}
\end{equation}
Whenever these equivalent conditions hold, the compatible net converges in
the Hilbert direct-limit completion $\mathsf H_{\mathrm{info}}$.  Indeed,
suppressing $(g,k)$, compatibility and orthogonality give
\begin{equation}
 \|\Gamma_{F'}-\iota_{FF'}\Gamma_F\|_{\mathsf H_{F'}}^2
 =\|\Gamma_{F'}\|_{\mathsf H_{F'}}^2
  -\|\Gamma_F\|_{\mathsf H_F}^2,
 \qquad F\subset F'.
 \label{sec:ng-chart-pythagoras}
\end{equation}
Thus the squared norms form a bounded increasing net with limit
$L:=\sup_F\|\Gamma_F\|_{\mathsf H_F}^2$.  If $F_0\subset F$, $F_0\subset G$,
and $H\supset F\cup G$, then
\[
 \|\iota_{FH}\Gamma_F-\iota_{GH}\Gamma_G\|_{\mathsf H_H}
 \leq
 \sqrt{\|\Gamma_H\|_{\mathsf H_H}^2-\|\Gamma_F\|_{\mathsf H_F}^2}
 +\sqrt{\|\Gamma_H\|_{\mathsf H_H}^2-\|\Gamma_G\|_{\mathsf H_G}^2}
 \leq 2\sqrt{L-\|\Gamma_{F_0}\|_{\mathsf H_{F_0}}^2},
\]
which tends to zero as $F_0$ increases.  Hence the net is Cauchy and converges
in $\mathsf H_{\mathrm{info}}$.  If, more strongly,
\begin{equation}
 c\|\Gamma_{F'}-\iota_{FF'}\Gamma_F\|_{\mathsf H_{F'}}^2
 \le E_{F'}-E_F
 \le C\|\Gamma_{F'}-\iota_{FF'}\Gamma_F\|_{\mathsf H_{F'}}^2,
 \qquad F\subset F',
 \label{sec:ng-shell-comparison}
\end{equation}
then projection energy and Hilbert shell energy define the same Cauchy
structure on the finite-stage core.
\end{theorem}

The Gaussian Hilbert--Schmidt space and the weighted $\ell^2$ spaces of
product models are instances of $\mathsf H_{\mathrm{info}}$; they are not
inputs to the theorem.  In a non-Gaussian matrix chart, the corresponding
completion is determined by the Fisher operator computed in the preceding
section.

One standard route to \eqref{sec:ng-shell-comparison} is quadratic-mean
differentiability.  For a differentiable path $t\mapsto\mu_t$ with score
$S_t\in L^2_0(\mu_t)$, the score visible to $\mathcal A_F$ is
\[
 S_{t,F}=\E_{\mu_t}[S_t\mid\mathcal A_F].
\]
Orthogonal projection in $L^2(\mu_t)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \|S_{t,F'}\|_2^2-\|S_{t,F}\|_2^2
 =\|S_{t,F'}-S_{t,F}\|_2^2,
 \qquad F\subset F'.
 \label{sec:ng-fisher-pythagoras}
\end{equation}
This identifies the infinitesimal information shell.  Uniform DQM remainder
bounds are still required to pass from
\eqref{sec:ng-fisher-pythagoras} to the finite Hellinger comparison
\eqref{sec:ng-shell-comparison}; local Fisher calculus by itself does not
close a global measure class.

\subsection{Changing the finite test system}
\label{sec:ng-changing-tests}

The cylinder uniformity is intrinsic provided two generating test systems
compare cofinally.  If $p_F$ and $q_G$ are their finite-stage gauges, assume that for
every finite $F$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there are a finite $G$ and
$\delta>0$ such that
\[
 q_G(\mu,\nu)<\delta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 p_F(\mu,\nu)<\varepsilon,
\]
and assume the converse implication with $p$ and $q$ interchanged.  Then the
two families generate the same projective uniformity.  The admissible metric
\eqref{sec:ng-likelihood-graph-metric} depends only on the reference law and
the global Radon--Nikodym graph, not on the generating test system.  Thus, for
the same core, both test systems have exactly the same admissibly Cauchy nets
and the same completion.  If energy or two-sided UI blocks are used to verify
endpoint equivalence, cofinality of those blocks transfers that verification
between the two systems as well.

This is the nonstatistical content of finite-experiment comparison.  A finite
net reduces a compact menu of tests to finitely many representatives; a
pullback compares those representatives; and the triangle inequality returns
to the full menu.  Uniform integrability supplies the closed realization of
the limiting likelihoods.  No Markov-kernel quotient is needed, and the null
ideal of the original sample space is retained.
