\section{Symmetric Stable Products}

For symmetric stable products, both the coefficient domain and the mixing cost
are \(\alpha\)-dependent.  Marques and Cambanis treat product-preserving affine
maps~\cite{marques1991dichotomies}; for Cauchy products, coordinatewise
location and scale changes are characterized in
\cite{lie2018cauchy,okamura2020cauchy}.  Off-diagonal dependence instead leads
to the column cost below.
Fix \(0<\alpha<2\), let \(\rho_\alpha\) be the standard symmetric
\(\alpha\)-stable law, and put
\begin{equation}
 \int_\R e^{itx}\,\rho_\alpha(dx)=e^{-|t|^\alpha},
 \qquad
 \mu_\alpha=\rho_\alpha^{\otimes\N},
 \qquad
 \mu_{\alpha,n}=\rho_\alpha^{\otimes n}.
 \label{eq:standard-symmetric-stable-law}
\end{equation}
For this symmetric law, \(\mathcal P\) is the full signed-permutation group and
\(\mathcal P_n\) its \(n\)-dimensional version.
Let \((X_j)_{j\geq1}\) be independent with common law \(\rho_\alpha\), and
write \(\mathbf X=(X_j)_{j\geq1}\).
We use the symmetric unit-scale convention of
\cite{nolan2020univariate}.  The density \(f_\alpha\) is smooth and positive and
has finite location and scale Fisher information despite its infinite second
moment~\cite{bobkov2014stable}: the density and derivative asymptotics in
\cite{zolotarev1986stable} give
\(f_\alpha(x)\asymp(1+|x|)^{-1-\alpha}\) and location score
\(O((1+|x|)^{-1})\).
For \(\alpha\le1\), the normalization in
\eqref{eq:standard-symmetric-stable-law} specifies a zero location parameter,
not a finite mean.

A stable series \(\sum_ja_jX_j\) converges almost surely exactly for
\(a\in\ell^\alpha\)~\cite{samorodnitsky1994stable}.  For a matrix \(T\) with
such rows, let \(\Phi_{\alpha,T}\) be its row-series map and
\(\nu_{\alpha,T}=(\Phi_{\alpha,T})_\#\mu_\alpha\).  The realization is
\emph{compatible} if some matrix \(S\), also with \(\ell^\alpha\) rows, has
measurable row-series maps satisfying
\[
 \Phi_{\alpha,S}\circ\Phi_{\alpha,T}=I,
 \qquad
 \Phi_{\alpha,T}\circ\Phi_{\alpha,S}=I
\]
on a common full-measure carrier for the corresponding orbit laws.  Unlike
\cref{lem:measurable-row-series-realization}, this is an assumption, not a
consequence of boundedness on \(\ell^2\).

For a matrix \(K\), put \(F_\alpha(K)=\sum_j\|Ke_j\|_2^\alpha\).  For
\(M=(m_{ij})\), define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak e_\alpha(M)
 =\sum_i|m_{ii}-1|^2
  +F_\alpha(M-\diag M).
 \label{eq:stable-mixing-cost}
\end{equation}
Thus scales cost quadratically, whereas off-diagonal input columns cost their
\(\ell^2\)-norms to the \(\alpha\)-th power.
For the finite output marginals, set
\begin{equation}
 E_{\alpha,n}(T)
 =-2\log\Aff\bigl(
       \mu_{\alpha,n},(\pi_n)_\#\nu_{\alpha,T}\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Complete criterion for symmetric stable coordinates]
\label{thm:complete-criterion-symmetric-stable-coordinates}
Let \(0<\alpha<2\) and suppose that \(T\) admits a compatible stable
row-series realization.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \nu_{\alpha,T}\sim\mu_\alpha
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T|_H\in\GL(H)\ \text{and}\
 \mathfrak e_\alpha(TQ^{-1})<\infty
       \ \text{for some }Q\in\mathcal P\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_nE_{\alpha,n}(T)<\infty,
 \end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
If these conditions fail, then
\(\nu_{\alpha,T}\perp\mu_\alpha\) and
\(E_{\alpha,n}(T)\uparrow\infty\).
For \(T\in\GL(H)\), the displayed summability condition itself supplies
compatible stable row-series realizations of \(T\) and \(T^{-1}\).
The exact linear stabilizer in this class is \(\mathcal P\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof separates the equivalence--singularity dichotomy, a Gaussian-mixture upper
bound, and Fourier/rare-event arguments for necessity.

\emph{Dichotomy, energy, and the coefficient domain.}
Put \(g_\alpha=\sqrt{f_\alpha}\) and define
\[
 a_\alpha(u)=\int_\R g_\alpha(x)g_\alpha(x-u)\,dx.
\]
Finite positive location Fisher information and decay of translations in
\(L^2\) give
\begin{equation}
 1-a_\alpha(u)\asymp_\alpha\min\{u^2,1\}.
 \label{eq:stable-translation-affinity}
\end{equation}
Kakutani's theorem therefore identifies \(\ell^2\) as the translation space
of \(\mu_\alpha\).  Under equivalence, conjugating translations through the
compatible realization shows that \(T\) and its inverse matrix \(S\) preserve
\(\ell^2\); the closed graph theorem gives \(T|_H\in\GL(H)\).

The inverse also makes every finite family of output rows linearly independent;
a nonsingular input minor convolved with the remaining inputs gives a positive
finite output marginal density.  Using compatibility for composition,
\eqref{eq:stable-translation-affinity} for translation quasi-invariance, and
these marginal densities, the proofs of
\cref{lem:equivalence-singularity-dichotomy,lem:finite-marginal-likelihood-ratios} adapt to
\(\mu_\alpha\).  Together with the equivalence--singularity theorem of
\cite{okazaki1985dichotomy}, they give
\begin{equation}
 \nu_{\alpha,T}\sim\mu_\alpha
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_nE_{\alpha,n}(T)<\infty,
 \label{eq:stable-energy-dichotomy}
\end{equation}
with singularity and divergent energy on the other branch.

\emph{Sufficiency.}
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 F_\alpha(AK)\le\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^\alpha F_\alpha(K),
 \qquad
 F_\alpha(KD)\le\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}^\alpha F_\alpha(K),\\
    \|K^*\gamma\|_\alpha^\alpha
    \le F_\alpha(K)\|\gamma\|_2^\alpha,
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:stable-column-class-bounds}
\end{equation}
for bounded \(A\), bounded diagonal \(D\), and \(\gamma\in\ell^2\).  In
particular, finite column cost gives admissible stable rows.

The finite-dimensional estimate that replaces the finite-variance Hellinger
path bound is
\begin{equation}
 h^2(A_\#\mu_{\alpha,n},\mu_{\alpha,n})
 \le C_\alpha\min\{\mathfrak e_\alpha(A),1\}
 \label{eq:stable-finite-dimensional-upper-bound}
\end{equation}
for every invertible real \(n\times n\) matrix \(A\), with a constant
independent of \(n\).
Put \(\beta=\alpha/2\) and use the positive-stable representation
\(X_i=\sqrt{2V_i}Z_i\), where
\(\E e^{-tV_i}=e^{-t^\beta}\) and the \(Z_i\) are independent standard
Gaussians~\cite{sato1999levy,samorodnitsky1994stable}.
Conditioning on \(V=\diag(V_i)\), Hellinger data processing and the Gaussian
affinity formula bound the left side of
\eqref{eq:stable-finite-dimensional-upper-bound} by a constant times
\[
 \E\min\{\|V^{-1/2}(A-I)V^{1/2}\|_F^2,1\}.
\]
For \(c_j^2=\sum_{i\ne j}|a_{ij}|^2\), source column \(j\) contributes
\(W_j=V_j\sum_{i\ne j}|a_{ij}|^2/V_i\).  The positive-stable tail and
truncated-moment bounds, together with \(\E V_1^{-1}<\infty\), give
\[
 \mathbb P(c_j^2V_j>1)+\E[W_j;c_j^2V_j\le1]
 \le C_\alpha c_j^{2\beta}=C_\alpha c_j^\alpha.
\]
Summation over \(j\), followed by the quadratic diagonal-scale estimate, proves
\eqref{eq:stable-finite-dimensional-upper-bound}.

Remove the measure-preserving \(Q\) and write
\(M=D+K\), where \(D=\diag M\) has entries \(d_i\),
\((d_i-1)\in\ell^2\), and
\(F_\alpha(K)<\infty\).
Only finitely many \(d_i\) are nonpositive.  Replace each of them by \(1\) in
\(D\) and add \(d_i-1\) to the corresponding diagonal entry of \(K\); this
preserves both summability conditions and makes \(D\) positive and boundedly
invertible.
Set
\[
 D_n=I+P_n(D-I)P_n,
 \qquad K_n=P_nKP_n,
 \qquad M_n=D_n+K_n,
\]
so that \(M_n\to M\) in operator norm and the \(M_n\) are eventually
invertible with uniformly bounded inverses.  The identity
\(M_n^{-1}=D_n^{-1}-M_n^{-1}K_nD_n^{-1}\) and
\eqref{eq:stable-column-class-bounds} give these inverses uniformly bounded
diagonal parts and column costs.  Applied to
\(M_n^{-1}(M_m-M_n)\), the same bounds show that its off-diagonal column cost
and diagonal square cost tend to zero; here
\(F_\alpha(K_m-K_n)\to0\), \(\|D_m-D_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to0\), and finite column
cost implies Hilbert--Schmidt membership.  Hence
\eqref{eq:stable-finite-dimensional-upper-bound} makes the square-root
likelihoods of \(\nu_{\alpha,M_n}\) Cauchy in \(L^2\).  The limit has norm
one, and \(\ell^\alpha\)-convergence of every row identifies its square with
\(d\nu_{\alpha,M}/d\mu_\alpha\).  This proves absolute continuity, which
\eqref{eq:stable-energy-dichotomy} upgrades to equivalence.
The identity
\((D+K)^{-1}=D^{-1}-(D+K)^{-1}KD^{-1}\) gives the same admissible form for the
inverse.  Continuity of stable random functionals from \(\ell^\alpha\) to
convergence in probability then passes finite-matrix composition to the
row-series limits and supplies compatible versions.

\emph{Reduction to a Hilbert--Schmidt perturbation.}
For necessity, suppose \(\nu_{\alpha,T}\sim\mu_\alpha\).  We first transfer the
half-density through Fourier transform to obtain the Hilbert--Schmidt part;
the sharper column cost will be recovered separately.

Let \(\widehat g_\alpha\) be the unitary Fourier transform of \(g_\alpha\),
and set
\[
 \rho_\alpha^\sharp(d\xi)=|\widehat g_\alpha(\xi)|^2\,d\xi.
\]
The law \(\rho_\alpha^\sharp\) is symmetric, has variance
\(\|g_\alpha'\|_2^2\in(0,\infty)\), and has characteristic function
\(a_\alpha\).  Since \(\widehat g_\alpha*\widehat g_\alpha\) is a positive
multiple of \(e^{-|t|^\alpha}\), we also have
\(|\widehat g_\alpha|^2*|\widehat g_\alpha|^2>0\) almost everywhere.
Polynomial stable tails give two-sided polynomial bounds for \(a_\alpha\), and
\(a_\alpha'\) is bounded.
Choose an integer \(r\) so large that
\[
 \int_\R t^2\bigl(|a_\alpha(t)|^{2r}
                 +|(a_\alpha^r)'(t)|^2\bigr)\,dt<\infty.
\]
Fourier inversion gives \((\rho_\alpha^\sharp)^{*r}\) a bounded-variation density, whose
third convolution has finite location Fisher information by the smoothing
estimate in \cite{bobkov2014stable}.  If \(f_{3r}\) is the density of
\((\rho_\alpha^\sharp)^{*3r}\), \(U_1,U_2\) are independent with this density, and
\(s_{3r},s_{6r}\) are the scores of \(f_{3r}\) and
\(f_{3r}*f_{3r}\), then
\[
 x s_{6r}(x)-1
 =2\E[U_1s_{3r}(U_2)\mid U_1+U_2=x].
\]
Thus finite variance and location Fisher information give finite scale Fisher
information after one further convolution.
Set \(k=6r\) and
\(\mu_{\alpha,k}^\sharp=((\rho_\alpha^\sharp)^{*k})^{\otimes\N}\).
After rescaling to variance one, \((\rho_\alpha^\sharp)^{*k}\) has an a.e.-positive density
satisfying \eqref{eq:main-assumptions}; it is non-Gaussian because its
characteristic function \(a_\alpha^k\) has a polynomial lower bound.

Let \(f_{\alpha,T,n}\) be the density of the first \(n\) output marginal and put
\[
 \ell_{\alpha,T,n}=\frac{f_{\alpha,T,n}}{f_\alpha^{\otimes n}},
 \qquad
 \ell_{\alpha,T}=\frac{d\nu_{\alpha,T}}{d\mu_\alpha}.
\]
Here \(\ell_{\alpha,T,n}\) is lifted to the product space and
\(\sqrt{f_{\alpha,T,n}}
  =g_\alpha^{\otimes n}\sqrt{\ell_{\alpha,T,n}}\).
Equivalence gives
\(\sqrt{\ell_{\alpha,T,n}}\to\sqrt{\ell_{\alpha,T}}\)
in \(L^2(\mu_\alpha)\).

Fourier transformation turns half-density translations into phases;
tensorization and frequency addition then produce the desired convolution.
Precisely, take \(k\) tensor copies of \(\sqrt{f_{\alpha,T,n}}\), Fourier
transform, square the modulus, and add the \(k\) frequencies at each site.
Let \(\Xi_{\alpha,T,n}\) be the product of the resulting law on \(\R^n\)
with \(((\rho_\alpha^\sharp)^{*k})^{\otimes\{i>n\}}\).
The first factor has a Lebesgue density and the tail density is positive
almost everywhere; hence
\(\Xi_{\alpha,T,n}\ll\mu_{\alpha,k}^\sharp\).

For \(m>n\), pad \(\sqrt{f_{\alpha,T,n}}\) by \(m-n\) factors of
\(g_\alpha\).  Unitarity, contraction under pushforward, and
\(\||u|^2-|v|^2\|_1\le2\|u-v\|_2\) give
\[
 \|\Xi_{\alpha,T,m}-\Xi_{\alpha,T,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le2\|(\sqrt{\ell_{\alpha,T,m}})^{\otimes k}
           -(\sqrt{\ell_{\alpha,T,n}})^{\otimes k}\|_{L^2(\mu_\alpha^{\otimes k})}
 \le2k\|\sqrt{\ell_{\alpha,T,m}}
           -\sqrt{\ell_{\alpha,T,n}}\|_{L^2(\mu_\alpha)}.
\]
Hence \(\Xi_{\alpha,T,n}\) converges in total variation to a probability
\(\Xi_{\alpha,T}\ll\mu_{\alpha,k}^\sharp\).

Let \(\nu_{\alpha,T}^{[n]}=\ell_{\alpha,T,n}\mu_\alpha\).  For every finitely
supported \(z\), Fourier autocorrelation and square-root convergence give
\begin{align*}
 \widehat\Xi_{\alpha,T}(z)
 &=\lim_n\Aff\bigl(\nu_{\alpha,T}^{[n]},
             (x\mapsto x+z)_\#\nu_{\alpha,T}^{[n]}\bigr)^k\\
 &=\Aff\bigl(\nu_{\alpha,T},
        (x\mapsto x+z)_\#\nu_{\alpha,T}\bigr)^k\\
 &=\prod_i a_\alpha((T^{-1}z)_i)^k.
\end{align*}
The last expression is the characteristic function of
\((\Phi_{(T^{-1})^*})_\#\mu_{\alpha,k}^\sharp\); agreement for finitely supported \(z\)
identifies all finite marginals.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 (\Phi_{(T^{-1})^*})_\#\mu_{\alpha,k}^\sharp\ll\mu_{\alpha,k}^\sharp.
\end{equation}
The dichotomy and classification in \cref{thm:measure-equivalence} upgrade
this to equivalence and give
\(Q\in\mathcal P\) such that
\((T^{-1})^*-Q\in\mathcal S_2\).
Since
\[
 T-Q=-T\bigl((T^{-1})^*-Q\bigr)^*Q,
\]
we obtain \(T-Q\in\mathcal S_2\).
After removing \(Q\), write
\begin{equation}
 TQ^{-1}=I+K,
 \qquad K\in\mathcal S_2.
 \label{eq:stable-hilbert-schmidt-match}
\end{equation}

\emph{Necessity of the off-diagonal column summability.}
For \(\alpha<2\), Hilbert--Schmidt control is weaker than the column cost.  We
detect column \(j\) on the event
\(|X_j|\asymp\|Be_j\|_2^{-1}\), whose probability has the required order.
We prove that there are constants \(\varepsilon_\alpha,c_\alpha>0\) such that,
for every \(n\) and every \(B\in\R^{n\times n}\),
\begin{equation}
 \diag B=0,\quad F_\alpha(B)\le\varepsilon_\alpha
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 h^2((I+B)_\#\mu_{\alpha,n},\mu_{\alpha,n})
 \ge c_\alpha F_\alpha(B).
 \label{eq:stable-off-diagonal-column-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
To prove it, write \(F=F_\alpha(B)\),
\(c_j=\|Be_j\|_2\), and
\(u_j=Be_j/c_j\) when \(c_j\ne0\).
Then \(\|u_j\|_2=1\), \((u_j)_j=0\), and the stable scale contributed to
output row \(i\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \omega_i(B):=\sum_j|b_{ij}|^\alpha\le F.
 \label{eq:stable-row-scale-bound}
\end{equation}

For a finite family of Hilbert-space vectors \(v_j\), excluding the events
\(\{\|v_j\|_2|X_j|>t\}\) and applying Chebyshev to the centered truncated sum
gives a tail of order
\(t^{-\alpha}\sum_j\|v_j\|_2^\alpha\).  Integration yields a modulus
\(\tau_\alpha(s)\downarrow0\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \E\min\left\{\left\|\sum_jv_jX_j\right\|_2,1\right\}
 \le\tau_\alpha\left(\sum_j\|v_j\|_2^\alpha\right).
 \label{eq:stable-hilbert-random-sum-truncation}
\end{equation}

Next, a bounded statistic detects unit displacements uniformly in the
dimension.  Set \(\psi(x)=\tanh x\).  There are a smooth odd increasing
\(\chi\), bounded by one, and \(c_\alpha>0\) such that every unit
vector \(u\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_u(x)=\sum_i u_i\psi(x_i),
 \qquad
 \mathcal M_u(t)=\E\chi(\Theta_u(\mathbf X+tu)),
 \qquad
 \mathcal M_u(t)\ge c_\alpha\quad(1\le t\le2).
 \label{eq:stable-directional-detection}
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(\Theta_u(\mathbf X)\) has mean zero and variance
\(\operatorname{Var}(\psi(X))\), while its shift by \(tu\) changes it by at
most \(2\) for \(|t|\le2\).  A fixed compact interval therefore carries all
but uniformly small mass.  Choose \(\chi'\) positive on that interval enlarged
by two.  The bound
\(\inf_{|\zeta|\le2}\E\psi'(X+\zeta)>0\) then makes
\(\mathcal M_u'(t)\) uniformly positive after the discarded contribution is
absorbed.  Oddness gives \(\mathcal M_u(0)=0\), proving
\eqref{eq:stable-directional-detection}.

Now choose a nonzero smooth odd function \(w\), supported where
\(1\le|t|\le2\), with \(0\le w\le1\) on the positive half-line.
Put
\[
 \Theta_j(x)=\sum_{i\ne j}(u_j)_i\psi(x_i),
\]
and define the bounded test
\[
 \Psi_B(x)=\max\left\{-1,\min\left\{
 \sum_{j:c_j\ne0}w(c_jx_j)\chi(\Theta_j(x)),1\right\}\right\}.
\]
For all sufficiently small \(c_j\), stable tail asymptotics give
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P(1\le|c_jX_j|\le2)\le C_\alpha c_j^\alpha,
 \qquad
 \E|w(c_jX_j)|\ge c_\alpha c_j^\alpha.
 \label{eq:stable-support-event-probability}
\end{equation}
The events \(\{w(c_jX_j)\ne0\}\) are independent, and the probability that at
least two occur is \(O_\alpha(F^2)\).  Before truncation each summand
has mean zero because \(w(c_jX_j)\) is independent of \(\Theta_j(\mathbf X)\);
hence
\(|\E\Psi_B(\mathbf X)|\le C_\alpha F^2\).

Set \(\mathbf Y=(I+B)\mathbf X\) and fix \(0<\delta<1/4\).  By
\eqref{eq:stable-row-scale-bound},
\[
 \mathbb P\{\exists i:\ |c_i(B\mathbf X)_i|>\delta\}
 \le C_\alpha\delta^{-\alpha}\sum_i c_i^\alpha\omega_i(B)
 \le C_\alpha\delta^{-\alpha}F^2.
\]
On the complementary event, Lipschitz continuity of \(w\) and
\eqref{eq:stable-support-event-probability} show that replacing every factor
\(w(c_jY_j)\) by \(w(c_jX_j)\) changes the expectation
by at most \(C_\alpha(\delta F+\delta^{-\alpha}F^2)\); removing the outer
truncation costs \(O_\alpha(F^2)\).  The remaining-coordinate perturbation
\(B(I-e_j\otimes e_j)\mathbf X\) is independent of \(X_j\), and
\eqref{eq:stable-hilbert-random-sum-truncation} shows that replacing
\(\Theta_j(\mathbf Y)\) by
\(\Theta_j(\mathbf X+c_jX_ju_j)\) costs at most
\(C_\alpha F\tau_\alpha(F)\) after summation.
Because \((u_j)_j=0\), conditioning on \(X_j\) and using
\cref{eq:stable-directional-detection,eq:stable-support-event-probability} gives
\[
 \sum_j\E\!\left[
   w(c_jX_j)\chi(\Theta_j(\mathbf X+c_jX_ju_j))\right]\ge c_\alpha F.
\]
Thus
\[
 \E\Psi_B(\mathbf Y)-\E\Psi_B(\mathbf X)
 \ge c_\alpha F-C_\alpha\{\delta F+(1+\delta^{-\alpha})F^2
                              +F\tau_\alpha(F)\}.
\]
Choose \(\delta\), then \(F\), so that the error is at most half the main
response.
The test is nonzero only when at least one such event occurs, and the coordinates of
\(\mathbf Y\) have stable scales at most \((1+F)^{1/\alpha}\); hence
\[
 \E\Psi_B(\mathbf X)^2+\E\Psi_B(\mathbf Y)^2\le C_\alpha F.
\]
Hellinger Cauchy--Schwarz now gives
\eqref{eq:stable-off-diagonal-column-lower-bound}.

We globalize this local estimate using finite tail sign changes.  Such a change
isolates the entries crossing a coordinate cut: equivalence makes every remote
cut Hellinger-invisible, whereas the local estimate detects every cut of a
fixed small cost.

Let \(B\in\mathcal S_2\) have \(\ell^\alpha\) rows, zero diagonal, and
\(\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1/10\).  Suppose
\(I+B\) admits a compatible stable row-series realization and
\(\nu_{\alpha,I+B}\sim\mu_\alpha\).
For a finite coordinate set \(\mathcal J\), let \(Q_{\mathcal J}\) change
the signs in \(\mathcal J\)
and set
\[
 \Delta_{\mathcal J}=Q_{\mathcal J}BQ_{\mathcal J}-B,
 \qquad
 A_{\mathcal J}=(I+B)^{-1}Q_{\mathcal J}(I+B)Q_{\mathcal J}.
\]
The matrix \(\Delta_{\mathcal J}\) consists of the entries crossing the cut.
Since \(\mathcal J\) is finite, \(\Delta_{\mathcal J}\) has finite column
cost.  Applying
\eqref{eq:stable-off-diagonal-column-lower-bound} to
\(P_n\Delta_{\mathcal J}P_n\) and using the sufficiency approximation extends
that bound to \(\Delta_{\mathcal J}\).

Put
\[
 K_{\mathcal J}=(I+B)^{-1}\Delta_{\mathcal J},
 \qquad
 D_{\mathcal J}=I+\diag K_{\mathcal J},
 \qquad
 \widetilde B_{\mathcal J}=D_{\mathcal J}^{-1}(I+K_{\mathcal J})-I.
\]
Then \(A_{\mathcal J}=I+K_{\mathcal J}\) and
\(\diag\widetilde B_{\mathcal J}=0\).  Since
\(\|(I+B)^{-1}-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1/9\), projection away from each diagonal
entry compares the off-diagonal columns of \(K_{\mathcal J}\) and
\(\Delta_{\mathcal J}\).  For small \(F_\alpha(\Delta_{\mathcal J})\), summing
these columnwise comparisons gives
\[
 F_\alpha(\widetilde B_{\mathcal J})\asymp_\alpha F_\alpha(\Delta_{\mathcal J}),
 \qquad
 \|\diag K_{\mathcal J}\|_2
 \le\frac19F_\alpha(\Delta_{\mathcal J})^{1/\alpha}.
\]
The lower bound for \(\widetilde B_{\mathcal J}\), the diagonal scale upper
bound for \(D_{\mathcal J}\), and the Hellinger triangle inequality now give
\begin{equation}
 F_\alpha(\Delta_{\mathcal J})\le\varepsilon_*
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 h(\nu_{\alpha,A_{\mathcal J}},\mu_\alpha)
 \ge c_*F_\alpha(\Delta_{\mathcal J})^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:stable-finite-cut-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
Indeed, by \eqref{eq:stable-off-diagonal-column-lower-bound}, the two terms
have respective orders
\(F_\alpha(\Delta_{\mathcal J})^{1/2}\) and
\(F_\alpha(\Delta_{\mathcal J})^{1/\alpha}\), and the latter is smaller for small
cost because \(\alpha<2\).

The finite-prefix argument in \cref{lem:tail-symmetry-uniform}, applied to the
square-root likelihood, is uniform over finite tail sign changes.  Since each
such change preserves \(\mu_\alpha\), Hellinger invariance gives
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\substack{\mathcal J\subset\{N+1,N+2,\ldots\}\\ |\mathcal J|<\infty}}
 h\bigl(\mu_\alpha,\nu_{\alpha,A_{\mathcal J}}\bigr)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:stable-tail-sign-change-invariance}
\end{equation}
Suppose \(F_\alpha(B)=\infty\).  Deleting finitely many columns or rows
changes the cost finitely, the latter because each row lies in
\(\ell^\alpha\); thus every tail has infinite cost.  For a finite tail set
\(\mathcal I_0\), include each coordinate independently in \(\mathcal J\) with
probability one half.  Columnwise concavity of \(t^{\alpha/2}\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \E_{\mathcal J}F_\alpha(P_{\mathcal I_0}\Delta_{\mathcal J}P_{\mathcal I_0})
 \ge2^{\alpha-1}F_\alpha(P_{\mathcal I_0}BP_{\mathcal I_0}).
 \label{eq:stable-random-finite-cut}
\end{equation}
As \(\mathcal I_0\) exhausts the tail, some finite cuts have arbitrarily large cost.
Equivalence makes the remote one-coordinate output marginals converge to
\(\rho_\alpha\), so
\(\omega_i(B)\to0\) in the notation of
\eqref{eq:stable-row-scale-bound}; Hilbert--Schmidt membership also gives
\(c_i\to0\).  Hence
\[
 F_\alpha(\Delta_{\{i\}})
 =2^\alpha(c_i^\alpha+\omega_i(B))\longrightarrow0.
\]
For \(\alpha<1\), \(F_\alpha^{1/\alpha}\) is a quasi-norm and its
\(\alpha\)-th power \(F_\alpha\) is subadditive; for \(\alpha\ge1\),
\(F_\alpha^{1/\alpha}\) is a norm.  Since adding \(i\) to a cut changes
\(\Delta_{\mathcal J}\) by a sign conjugate of \(\Delta_{\{i\}}\), the
increments of \(F_\alpha\) in the first case and of
\(F_\alpha^{1/\alpha}\) in the second tend uniformly to zero.  Fix
\(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_*/2\), add the elements of a large cut from
\eqref{eq:stable-random-finite-cut}, and stop at the first threshold crossing.
In every sufficiently remote tail this produces \(\mathcal J\) with
\(\varepsilon\le F_\alpha(\Delta_{\mathcal J})\le2\varepsilon\).
Equations
\cref{eq:stable-finite-cut-lower-bound,eq:stable-tail-sign-change-invariance}
then contradict one another.
Hence \(F_\alpha(B)<\infty\).

Finally, for \(M=I+K\) from \eqref{eq:stable-hilbert-schmidt-match}, take
\(M_n=I+P_nKP_n\) and set \(R=M_n^{-1}M\), which is close to \(I\).
Composition with the finite-coordinate map \(M_n^{-1}\) gives
\(\nu_{\alpha,R}\sim\mu_\alpha\).  The diagonal part
\(D_R=\diag R\) is positive, boundedly invertible, and satisfies
\(D_R-I\in\mathcal S_2\), so the one-coordinate scale criterion gives
\(\nu_{\alpha,D_R}\sim\mu_\alpha\).  Thus
\[
 D_R^{-1}R=I+B,
 \qquad
 \diag B=0,\quad B\in\mathcal S_2,\quad
 \|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1/10,\quad
 \nu_{\alpha,I+B}\sim\mu_\alpha.
\]
Finite-coordinate and bounded diagonal composition preserve \(\ell^\alpha\)
rows and compatibility; hence the finite-cut argument gives
\(F_\alpha(B)<\infty\).  Since \(K_n\) is finitely supported,
\[
 M=M_nD_R(I+B)
  =D_R+\bigl(K_nD_R+D_RB+K_nD_RB\bigr).
\]
The parenthesized term has finite column cost by
\eqref{eq:stable-column-class-bounds}.  Restoring the signed permutation gives
\[
 \sum_j\left(\sum_{i\ne j}|k_{ij}|^2\right)^{\alpha/2}<\infty,
\]
while \(K\in\mathcal S_2\) gives \(\sum_i|k_{ii}|^2<\infty\).  Together these
are \(\mathfrak e_\alpha(TQ^{-1})<\infty\).

If \(\nu_{\alpha,T}=\mu_\alpha\), comparison of the joint characteristic
functions, first for $T$ and then for its compatible inverse, makes $T^*$ a
surjective isometry of \(\ell^\alpha\).  For \(0<\alpha<2\), equality in
\[
 2\|u\|_\alpha^\alpha+2\|v\|_\alpha^\alpha
 -\|u+v\|_\alpha^\alpha-\|u-v\|_\alpha^\alpha\ge0
\]
characterizes disjoint support.  Hence the images of the coordinate vectors
have disjoint supports; surjectivity makes each support a singleton, and norm
preservation makes its entry \(\pm1\).  Thus \(T\in\mathcal P\).  The
converse follows from independence and symmetry.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anisotropic stable Hellinger coercivity and sharpness}

The two summands in \eqref{eq:stable-mixing-cost} have genuinely different
homogeneities.  For a finite matrix \(A\), put
\[
 \mathfrak e_{\alpha,n}(A)
 =\min_{Q\in\mathcal P_n}\mathfrak e_\alpha(AQ^{-1}).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Anisotropic stable Hellinger coercivity]
Let \(A\in\GL(n,\R)\).  If all singular values of \(A\) lie in a fixed interval
\([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\), then there are positive constants depending only
on \(\alpha,a,b\), and not on the dimension, such that
\begin{equation}
 c_{\alpha,a,b}\min\{\mathfrak e_{\alpha,n}(A),1\}
 \le h^2(A_\#\mu_{\alpha,n},\mu_{\alpha,n})
 \le C_{\alpha,a,b}\min\{\mathfrak e_{\alpha,n}(A),1\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound is \eqref{eq:stable-finite-dimensional-upper-bound}, after
removing the minimizing signed permutation.
For the even convolution power \(k\) above, Fourier transformation and modulus
contraction, followed by tensorization and pushforward under coordinatewise
frequency addition, give
\begin{equation}
 h^2\bigl(((A^{-1})^*)_\#((\rho_\alpha^\sharp)^{*k})^{\otimes n},
                 ((\rho_\alpha^\sharp)^{*k})^{\otimes n}\bigr)
 \le k\,h^2(A_\#\mu_{\alpha,n},\mu_{\alpha,n}).
\end{equation}
Apply finite-variance coercivity to the rescaled \((\rho_\alpha^\sharp)^{*k}\) product.  The
singular values of
\((A^{-1})^*\) lie in \([b^{-1},a^{-1}]\), and
\[
 A-Q=-A\bigl((A^{-1})^*-Q\bigr)^*Q
\]
compares the two distances up to a factor depending on \(a,b\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
 h^2(A_\#\mu_{\alpha,n},\mu_{\alpha,n})
 \ge c_{\alpha,a,b}
     \min\left\{\min_{Q\in\mathcal P_n}\|A-Q\|_F^2,1\right\}.
 \label{eq:stable-quadratic-lower-bound}
\end{equation}

Near a signed permutation, remove that permutation and write
\[
 d_{\mathrm{diag}}=\sum_i|a_{ii}-1|^2,
 \qquad
 F=\sum_j\left(\sum_{i\ne j}|a_{ij}|^2\right)^{\alpha/2}.
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm{diag}}+F\) small, the identity is the nearest signed
permutation, so \eqref{eq:stable-quadratic-lower-bound} controls
\(d_{\mathrm{diag}}\).  With \(D=\diag A\) and \(D^{-1}A=I+B\),
\(\diag B=0\) and \(F_\alpha(B)\asymp_\alpha F\).
The off-diagonal column lower bound and the diagonal scale upper bound give
\[
 h(A_\#\mu_{\alpha,n},\mu_{\alpha,n})
 \ge c_\alpha\sqrt F-C_\alpha\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{diag}}}.
\]
Choose \(\eta\le(c_\alpha/(2C_\alpha))^2\).  Then
\(d_{\mathrm{diag}}\le\eta F\) implies
\(C_\alpha\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{diag}}}\le(c_\alpha/2)\sqrt F\).  When
$d_{\mathrm{diag}}\ge\eta F$, use
\eqref{eq:stable-quadratic-lower-bound}.  This proves the local lower bound.

If the global lower bound failed, there would be \(\varepsilon>0\), dimensions
\(n_k\), and matrices \(A_k\in\GL(n_k,\R)\), with singular values in \([a,b]\),
such that \(\mathfrak e_{\alpha,n_k}(A_k)\ge\varepsilon\) and, after passing to
a subsequence,
\[
 \sum_k h^2((A_k)_\#\mu_{\alpha,n_k},\mu_{\alpha,n_k})<\infty.
\]
The block-diagonal sum is boundedly invertible; its row map and inverse act
blockwise and are compatible.  Kakutani makes its image product equivalent to
\(\mu_\alpha\), so the classification supplies a signed
permutation with finite anisotropic cost.  A permutation column crossing a
block contributes at least \(a^\alpha\), so all sufficiently late blocks are
preserved.  The corresponding block restrictions then give
\(\sum_k\mathfrak e_{\alpha,n_k}(A_k)<\infty\), contradicting the lower bound
\(\varepsilon\).  This proves the global lower bound.
\end{proof}

The different local powers already appear for the shear
\(T_t(x,y)=(x+ty,y)\).  Conditioning on \(y\) and applying Plancherel gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 h^2((T_t)_\#\mu_{\alpha,2},\mu_{\alpha,2})
 &=2\int_\R(1-e^{-|t\xi|^\alpha})
                  |\widehat g_\alpha(\xi)|^2\,d\xi\\
 &\sim2|t|^\alpha\int_\R|\xi|^\alpha
                  |\widehat g_\alpha(\xi)|^2\,d\xi,\qquad t\to0.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Diagonal scales instead cost \(t^2\), so no single finite-dimensional matrix
Fisher quadratic form generates this anisotropic coercivity.

In particular, Hilbert--Schmidt proximity is necessary but no longer
sufficient.
On independent two-coordinate blocks, let
\[
 T_a(x_k,y_k)_{k\ge1}=(x_k+a_ky_k,y_k)_{k\ge1}.
\]
Then \(T_a-I\in\mathcal S_2\) exactly when \(a\in\ell^2\), while
\cref{thm:complete-criterion-symmetric-stable-coordinates} gives
\(\nu_{\alpha,T_a}\sim\mu_\alpha\) exactly when
\(a\in\ell^\alpha\).
Any \(a\in\ell^2\setminus\ell^\alpha\) therefore gives a
Hilbert--Schmidt perturbation with singular image law, even though the
stable coordinate density is everywhere positive and has finite location
and scale Fisher information.
By contrast, when one source is mixed into many outputs, the criterion
charges the \(\ell^2\)-norm of that whole column only once.
An entrywise \(\ell^\alpha\) condition would therefore be too strong.
The correct stable criterion is the anisotropic mixing cost
\eqref{eq:stable-mixing-cost}, not a Schatten-class replacement for the
finite-variance theorem.
