\section{Rotational Fourier Defect and Hellinger Coercivity}
\label{sec:rotational-fourier-defect-and-hellinger-coercivity}

Non-Gaussianity is first detected by a bounded, logarithm-free rotational
Fourier defect: if it vanishes at every frequency pair, a global linear ODE
forces the coordinate law to be Gaussian
\cite{kac1939characterization,kagan1973characterization}.  That
one-dimensional rigidity step is short.  The harder task is to turn one
nonzero defect into fixed bounded coordinate functions for the off-diagonal
directions, add a separate bounded function for the diagonal scale directions,
and keep the resulting estimates independent of the dimension.  The matrix
Fisher operator identifies the infinitesimal geometry; the coordinate
functions assemble into direction-dependent test functions; and an explicit
index-contraction estimate controls their motion along a Hellinger path.
Together these steps yield local Frobenius coercivity.  We
also state the global form modulo the exact linear stabilizer; its proof follows
the infinite-dimensional classification.
