Scope of the new classification
The common law rho has a non-Gaussian density f, mean zero, variance one,
sqrt(f) in W^{1,2}(R), and x(sqrt(f))' in L2(R). The density need not be
positive almost everywhere. T is a bounded invertible operator on H.
For orthogonal T, the scale condition can be omitted as stated in the theorem.
The result does not assert the same classification for arbitrary irregular
densities without these hypotheses.

Definitions and substitution checks
- U is exactly {f>0}, not the topological support of the coordinate law.
- mu(U^N)=1. U^N introduces no second sample space or square-root density.
- nu_T always denotes the law of the measurable row-series realization.
- G_rho consists of all bounded invertible T with nu_T equivalent to mu.
  Its displayed classification contains Hilbert--Schmidt proximity and only
  one-dimensional row zero-set probabilities for T and its inverse.
- The one-sided theorem uses only the forward row condition. Replacing it
  by the two-sided condition would incorrectly remove strict absolute continuity.
- All row sums have their L2(mu) and almost-sure meanings from the earlier
  realization lemma; the result does not require samples to belong to H.

Proof verification
1. If T-Q is Hilbert--Schmidt with Q in the exact stabilizer P, then
   K=TQ^{-1}-I is Hilbert--Schmidt and nu_T=nu_{I+K}.
2. The eventually invertible finite-coordinate approximants I+P_n K P_n
   converge in Hellinger distance, hence total variation, to nu_T. This
   follows from the restricted-density local estimate and its proof.
3. Each approximant changes only its first n coordinates. Its law has a
   Lebesgue density there and leaves an independent reference product tail.
   On U^n the reference density is positive, so Fubini shows that the
   approximant restricted to U^N is absolutely continuous relative to mu.
   No absolute continuity of the unrestricted approximant is assumed.
4. For every mu-null B, all approximants give B intersect U^N mass zero.
   Total variation convergence gives the same assertion for nu_T.
   Thus its restriction to U^N is its absolutely continuous part; its
   restriction to the complement is singular.
5. Therefore nu_T is absolutely continuous exactly when every forward row
   lies in U almost surely, once Hilbert--Schmidt proximity is known.
   The previously proved necessity supplies the reverse operator implication.
6. The inverse identity T^{-1}-Q^{-1}=-T^{-1}(T-Q)Q^{-1} preserves the
   Hilbert--Schmidt condition. Applying the one-sided criterion to both
   operators and using the measurable inverse action proves equivalence.
7. Measurable composition gives nu_{ST}=(Phi_S)_#nu_T, so equivalences are
   closed under products. The inverse argument gives closure under inverses.
8. Every row of an invertible T is nonzero. Conditioning on all but one
   nonzero coefficient gives an absolutely continuous scalar row law. Thus
   modifying f on a Lebesgue-null set does not change either row condition.

Consistency with the other seventh-section results
- Under positivity almost everywhere, the row conditions are automatic,
  recovering the earlier Hilbert--Schmidt equivalence theorem.
- A finite contraction of a bounded-support coordinate satisfies the forward
  row condition and fails the inverse condition: strict absolute continuity.
- The bounded-law proof bounds both inverse matrices' absolute row sums by
  one and hence forces signed permutations; reflection equivalence determines
  whether all, finitely many, or no negative signs are allowed.
- For a one-sided law, the finite conditional sums force nonnegative rows
  for both inverse matrices. The same argument forces ordinary permutations.
- The affine Gamma result concerns an affine transformation. Its endpoint
  identity and logarithmic scale summability are consistent with the separate
  homogeneous linear permutation classification.
- The uniform-square example lacks the Sobolev regularity used by the new
  theorem. The smooth-square example satisfies that regularity but a nontrivial
  rotation fails its row zero-set condition. Neither example contradicts it.
- The square-intersection formula and its linear small-angle term agree;
  the smooth comparison has the stated quadratic Fisher expansion.

Seventh-section notation changes
Coordinate lambda became rho; generic block lambda_k remains a separately
quantified block law. R_n became the lifted finite-marginal likelihood ratio
ell_n. The one-use block-density and Gamma-affinity names were removed.
Undefined r_f(theta) and the temporary uniform-rotation affinity name were
replaced by explicit Hellinger affinities. The sparse affine-map name Psi was
removed. The two absolutely continuous masses are now stated as a corollary,
with explicit dominating measures and the same a-subscript convention as
the Lebesgue decomposition. A missing density assumption for the block formula
was made explicit. Countable operator groups use Gamma, reserving G_rho for
the equivalence group. Misleading 'full support' and 'commutator' descriptions
were replaced with the actual positivity assumption and operator differences.
