Canonical language and macro-expansion checks
- "a.e.-positive density" expands to f>0 Lebesgue-almost everywhere. The
  stronger phrase "everywhere-positive density" is used only where pointwise
  positivity is actually available, notably for symmetric stable densities.
- "centered with variance one" expands to E X=0 and E X^2=1.
- "bounded invertible linear mixing" expands to an element of GL(ell^2) acting
  through the specified measurable row-series realization. "Dense" remains
  only in its literal topological sense, such as a dense translation group.
- "projected Hellinger energy" expands to
  E_n(T)=-2 log Aff(mu_n,(pi_n)_#nu_T).
- "exact linear stabilizer" expands to {T:nu_T=mu}; its identified subgroup is
  \mathcal P in the finite-variance branch and \mathcal P^{\pm} in the symmetric
  stable branch.
- "rotational Fourier defect" expands to
  R(xi,eta)=eta phi'(xi)phi(eta)-xi phi(xi)phi'(eta).
- "matrix Fisher operator" expands to the score-covariance operator I defined
  by <A,I B>_F=E[S_A S_B].
- "compatible stable row-series realization" expands to the measurable
  row-series maps for T and a row inverse S satisfying both composition
  identities on common conull carriers.
- "anisotropic mixing cost" expands to the displayed diagonal-square term plus
  the off-diagonal column cost F_alpha.
- "zero-set row condition" expands to the formal forward and inverse
  almost-sure conditions that every relevant row land in {f>0}.

One word, one meaning, one name
The visible manuscript uses the canonical forms above. "Stable" is reserved for
alpha-stable probability laws and their row series. Convergence is described as
convergent.
"Likelihood ratio" is the open compound noun, while attributive compounds use
"likelihood-ratio", as in likelihood-ratio martingale and likelihood-ratio
convergence. The canonical name for the normalized property is "centered with
variance one"; phrases such as "rescaled to variance one" describe the distinct
operation that produces that normalization.
