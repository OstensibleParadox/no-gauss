Method and limits
Each inventory phrase was submitted to Google Scholar enclosed in quotation
marks. Actual public results pages were inspected in the Codex in-app browser
using accessibility text and, where needed, DOM snapshots. Titles and snippets
were read to classify context. Primary papers were inspected for the substantive
nearby naming decisions identified below. No hit-count-based bibliometrics or
claim of reading every returned paper is made.
The inventory covers named objects, persistent notation, and descriptive
technical phrases identified during the manuscript audit. Related local symbols
are mapped to their definitions. Bare letters, dummy indices, numerical constants
and ordinary logical notation are not meaningful standalone phrase queries.
Queries for descriptions subsequently rewritten remain in the table with their
final action; they are audit records, not aliases retained in the manuscript.
Replacing a name by its explicit definition does not create a new named concept.
Scholar may normalize hyphens and punctuation even within quotation marks.
No articles for an exact query establishes neither novelty nor absence of synonyms.
A hit alone is not a harmful collision: standard meanings, other disciplinary
contexts, and nearby competing technical definitions are distinguished.
These checks do not establish priority or certify the originality of the results.

Nearby naming decisions supported by primary sources
1. Projected likelihood
McLeish and Small use projection of likelihood ratios onto inference-function
spaces in semiparametric models (Biometrika 79(1), 93-102, 1992).
Official DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/79.1.93
Official publisher abstract was inspected. A separate GP training-objective use
also occurs in https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16332 (primary abstract inspected).
The manuscript uses finite-marginal likelihood ratio for its actual marginal
Radon-Nikodym derivatives. Its exact query displayed no articles; no novelty
inference follows.

2. Projective criterion
Durieu and Volny (2008) explicitly use this name for L1 convergence of
sum_(k>=1) X_0 E[X_k | F_0] in stationary-process invariance-principle theory.
Official primary text, p.325: https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1214/07-AIHP123.pdf
Related author preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.1848
This differs from the manuscript's criterion using finite-marginal likelihood
ratios. The single-use phrase is replaced by criterion in terms of finite marginals.

3. Fourier feature
Tancik et al. use sine/cosine vector input maps before an MLP, with equation (5)
giving the map explicitly. Official primary text:
https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/55053683268957697aa39fba6f231c68-Paper.pdf
This is contextual overlap, not proof that the previous phrase was mathematically
invalid. Product of centered complex exponentials now describes W exactly.
The undefined fractional-feature aside is replaced by the explicit observation
that bounded test functions do not remove the admissible-row condition.

4. Density zero set
Inspected Scholar records use this string for sets of asymptotic density zero
in number theory and set theory, including Density zero slaloms (2000):
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168007299000184
The manuscript instead concerns {f=0}; its subsection now says zero sets of the
density. This is a lexical disambiguation, not a claim that the cited theory is
applicable to the manuscript's operators.

Load-bearing name retained after context check
Linear equivalence group returned three records, including Li, Huang and Pei,
The Recognition of the Bifurcation Problem with Trivial Solutions (2019).
Official primary text, pp.2-3:
https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/symmetry/symmetry-11-00935/article_deploy/symmetry-11-00935-v2.pdf
Its group acts on bifurcation-function germs, not probability measure classes.
The manuscript retains linear equivalence group of mu and the explicit definition
G_rho={T in GL(H): nu_T equivalent to mu}. The term is used in the classification
statement and throughout its development. The inspected overlap does not require
discarding this useful name. The one- and two-sided operator criteria are unchanged.
